## 1. Lock sheet

These decisions are binding unless deliberately amended in a later planning pass.

### 1.1 Core premise

Henry Cooper is a human-presenting orphaned teenager in Queens whose latent Jotun/Asgardian heritage manifests as ice and thermal manipulation. He becomes a masked, inexperienced local hero before he understands that the father absent from every ordinary record is Loki.

The MCU's canon events remain the long-form skeleton. Henry is a new element placed at pressure points, not a replacement for the characters who own those events.

### 1.2 Starting point

The main story opens in **fall 2014**, after *The Winter Soldier* and *Guardians of the Galaxy* have changed the background of the world but before *Age of Ultron*. Henry's powers awaken before the opening action sequence. The opening story is about a boy discovering what his body can do and making the first bad decision to use it in public.

The story does **not** open in 1971, with a TVA capture, with Loki explaining the ancestry, or with an omniscient prophecy. The 1971 and TVA material is a buried story layer.

### 1.3 Parentage and reveal

- Loki is the real D.B. Cooper in 1971, as established by *Loki*.
- During the short Earth-side period around the hijacking, Loki has a brief relationship with Henry's human mother, Eleanor Cooper.
- Loki does not know Eleanor becomes pregnant.
- Henry is conceived in 1971 and born in 1972.
- The TVA captures Henry as an infant because his existence creates a temporal anomaly the TVA classifies as a branch-level problem, not because he is a chosen savior.
- Sylvie is escaping at the same time, takes the baby during the chaos because she remembers her own childhood being ruined by the TVA, moves him to a safer point on the Sacred Timeline, and uses enchantment to conceal his Jotun/Asgardian origin.
- She drops him into the human world in 2000. He grows normally from infancy to his apparent teenage age. There is no unexplained magical aging.
- Henry's apparent age and the 1970s conception are not solved before the TVA story is narratively earned.
- The major Loki parentage reveal occurs during the **Age of Ultron era**, not during *Thor: The Dark World*.

### 1.4 Thor and Henry

Thor may meet Henry repeatedly before the reveal, but he must not become close, protective, emotionally attached, or bonded to Henry beforehand. Pre-reveal encounters are limited acquaintance, curiosity, battlefield respect, and occasional professional correction.

Thor's meaningful relationship with Henry begins after the parentage truth is known. A family tree does not create intimacy automatically; repeated choices do.

### 1.5 Peter and Henry

Peter has an earlier unseen spider-bite and patrol period in late 2013 or early 2014. This is the smallest chronology butterfly that allows:

- Peter to meet Henry before *Civil War*;
- the boys to become friends;
- both to attend Midtown in the same cohort/class;
- Peter to remain recognizably the young Spider-Man recruited by Tony in *Civil War*;
- Peter to remain absent from the *Age of Ultron* field team.

Henry operates masked for several days/weeks, makes mistakes, and takes on a situation beyond his skill. Spider-Man intervenes. Peter teaches him practical superheroing. They later discover they attend the same class. Henry notices first that Peter is terrible at maintaining a secret identity.

### 1.6 Canon protection

The following remain recognizable and are not to be replaced by Henry:

- the New York invasion in *The Avengers*;
- Tony's *Iron Man 3* aftermath and Extremis plot;
- the Aether/Frigga/Loki/Odin material in *The Dark World*;
- the HYDRA/S.H.I.E.L.D. collapse in *The Winter Soldier*;
- the Guardians' cosmic story;
- Ultron's awakening, Wanda and Pietro, Vision, Sokovia, and the Avengers' victory;
- the Sokovia Accords and Bucky in *Civil War*;
- Peter's recruitment and Vulture problem;
- Strange's mystic entrance and the Dormammu loop;
- Asgard's destruction and the refugee ship;
- Thanos, the Snap, Peter's death and return, and the Endgame time heist;
- Wanda's Westview grief story;
- Mysterio and Peter's public unmasking;
- the No Way Home spell and the other Spider-Men;
- the major TVA/Loki timeline developments.

Henry can change how a scene is experienced without taking ownership of a canon character's defining event.

## 2. Project identity

### 2.1 Working title

**The Cold Between Worlds** is a working title. It refers first to the emotional distance between a child and the people who should have known him, then to the coldness of records and institutions, and only much later to the literal gaps between timelines. It should not be treated as evidence that the early story is a multiverse story.

### 2.2 One-line pitch

A Queens teenager who can steal heat and shape ice becomes a masked hero beside Peter Parker, only to learn during the Age of Ultron crisis that his absent father is Loki—and that the impossible age of his own life belongs to a future TVA secret.

### 2.3 Long pitch

Henry Cooper knows how to live around missing information. His mother is dead, his father is absent, his records are ordinary enough to pass and strange enough to avoid questions, and his foster guardian has given him a decent home without being able to explain every blank space in it. When cold begins gathering under his skin, Henry treats it like any other practical problem: test it, set limits, hide the damage, and keep going to school.

Then he tries to help someone and Spider-Man has to rescue him.

Peter Parker is not an expert, but he knows enough to teach Henry the difference between a power and a plan. School, patrols, food, injuries, secrets, and the increasingly visible shadow of the Avengers turn the boys into close friends. Henry becomes a legitimate but limited young hero just as the world is becoming too large for local rules.

The Age of Ultron crisis puts Henry's records, his ice, and the Avengers' hidden evidence in the same room. A 1971 notebook, a sample from the Battle of New York, and an Asgardian object point toward an answer that should be impossible: Henry's father is Loki, the man who once called himself D.B. Cooper. Loki did not know Henry existed. Thor did not know Henry was family. Henry has to decide what those facts mean without allowing blood to erase the life he already built.

Years later, after the Snap, the Blip, the return of the dead, Spider-Man's public unmasking, and the first real fractures between universes, Henry finally learns why a child conceived in 1971 could grow up as a teenager in the 2010s. The answer is not destiny. It is a frightened girl escaping the TVA, an infant she refused to surrender, and a life hidden so thoroughly that Henry has to choose it for himself.

### 2.4 Story promise to the reader

The story promises:

1. A practical teenage voice rather than generic epic narration.
2. Ice powers that develop through experiments, costs, mistakes, and movement.
3. Peter and Henry's friendship as an actual relationship, not a cameo exchange.
4. An MCU that remains recognizable and populated by independent characters.
5. Family relationships that develop through behavior rather than instant declarations.
6. Mysteries that are seeded early, answered late, and not solved by convenient exposition.
7. Consequences that become the next problem instead of evaporating between fights.
8. Humor, school life, logistics, and food alongside danger.
9. No artificial misery, protagonist humiliation, power-system menus, harem structure, or generic chosen-one language.

### 2.5 What the story refuses

- No Chapter 1 exposition dump about Loki, the TVA, or the multiverse.
- No Thor/Henry pre-reveal closeness.
- No perfect father Loki immediately after the reveal.
- No instant hatred from Henry because the outline demands drama.
- No Mind Stone genealogy oracle.
- No unexplained magical aging.
- No Henry replacing Peter as the central Spider-Man of the Spider-Man stories.
- No Henry replacing Wanda, Tony, Bruce, Thor, Vision, or Steve in their canon functions.
- No escalation where every arc must be darker, bloodier, or more cosmic than the last.
- No copied passages from the resource bank; only functions, structures, mechanics, and adapted techniques are imported.

## 3. Story engine

The story has six linked engines. An arc should normally move at least three of them, and a major arc should move all six.

### Engine A — The local hero problem

Henry sees a concrete danger, tries to help, and discovers that ice can solve one part of a situation while creating another. His hero identity is built through repeated choices, not a costume reveal.

### Engine B — The friendship problem

Peter and Henry have different strengths, different secrets, and different ideas about what they owe people. Their friendship survives arguments because each conflict changes a rule, boundary, or understanding.

### Engine C — The family problem

Henry has a humane present and an impossible past. Elena Reyes is the adult who raised him; Peter is the friend who meets him where he is; Thor becomes family only after the reveal; Loki must earn any place at all.

### Engine D — The record problem

Dates, names, evidence, and memories do not line up. The investigation progresses from ordinary clerical oddity to impossible biology to TVA history. Records are treated as things made by institutions and people, not as magical truth.

### Engine E — The MCU pressure problem

Canon events put Henry in situations where his existing rules are insufficient. He helps where he plausibly can, stays out of the defining center of other people's stories, and carries consequences forward.

### Engine F — The time problem

The story begins with no explicit multiverse. Henry's age anomaly is first a personal and administrative mystery, then an Asgardian/biological mystery, then a historical mystery, and finally a TVA/multiverse mystery. Each escalation must be earned by the canon timeline.

## 4. Central character arc

Henry begins with the internal rule:

> If I am useful enough, nobody gets to decide that I am disposable.

This rule makes him observant, practical, and brave. It also makes him hide injuries, refuse help, treat relationships as exchanges, and rush toward danger to prove that he deserves to remain in the room.

His long arc is not learning to be selfless. He is already willing to be selfless. His arc is learning that:

- responsibility is not the same as solitary self-punishment;
- accepting care does not surrender autonomy;
- blood explains history but does not dictate loyalty;
- a record can be wrong without his life being unreal;
- being rescued does not make him weak;
- he is allowed to choose a future that was not chosen for him.

At the end, Henry does not become a cosmic king or the key to reality. He becomes someone able to say, without needing permission from Loki, Thor, the TVA, Peter, or the audience: **this is my life, and I am responsible for what I do with it.**

## 5. Main story and side-story architecture

### 5.1 Main story: the Henry line

The main story is the continuous line from the first frost through the TVA resolution:

1. powers awaken;
2. masked mistakes begin;
3. Peter and Henry become friends;
4. the boys learn to work together;
5. Henry becomes known to a few adults;
6. AOU reveals Loki's paternity;
7. Henry and Loki negotiate an unwanted relationship;
8. the Avengers' fracture and cosmic events test Henry's choices;
9. the Snap and return change the scale of his life;
10. multiversal events expose the hidden chronology;
11. Sylvie's rescue is revealed;
12. Henry chooses identity and responsibility over inherited explanation.

### 5.2 Side story 1: **Queens Weather**

This is the Peter/Henry school and neighborhood line. It includes homework, lunch tables, laboratories, costumes, patrol routes, injuries, guardians, public footage, and the boys' recurring arguments about which problem is actually urgent.

It is not filler. It supplies the ordinary life that the parentage and TVA plots later threaten to overwrite.

**Primary sources:** `The_Spider.txt`, `I just want to learn magic bro.txt`, `Kitsu.txt`, `Steal_the_Truth_Reach.txt`, `A_Body_of_Water_and.txt`.

### 5.3 Side story 2: **The Cooper Papers**

This is the evidence trail around Eleanor Cooper and 1971. It is told through present-day investigation, archive visits, a notebook, institutional gaps, and carefully placed historical interludes. It must never read like a secret prophecy.

**Locked evidence chain:**

- Eleanor meets a charming man using the name Dan Cooper during the short Earth-side period around the 24 November 1971 hijacking.
- He is Loki, but she has no reason to know that.
- She keeps a notebook, a photograph, and an unusual metal token.
- She gives birth to Henry in 1972 and loses him to the TVA's infant capture operation.
- Her surviving papers contain enough details to identify the D.B. Cooper connection only when combined with Asgardian and Battle of New York evidence.
- She dies in 2008 after a normal adult life marked by the unresolved loss of her child. Her papers are scattered into an estate/archival box rather than conveniently delivered to Henry.

The mother is a person, not a clue dispenser. The evidence chain must preserve her ordinary choices, uncertainty, and inability to know the cosmic explanation.

**Primary sources:** `All_the_Young_Dudes.txt` for school-era temporal texture, `A_Nascent_Kaleidoscope.txt` for hidden-guardian/record tension, `Faith_for_the_second_run.txt` and `Throw_Away_Your_Mask.txt` for discontinuity investigation, `A_Body_of_Water_and.txt` for social/archival texture.

### 5.4 Side story 3: **The God Who Wasn't Looking**

This is Thor, Loki, and Henry's family line.

- Before AOU: Thor knows Henry only as a young Midgardian with an unusual power.
- AOU: Thor learns Henry is Loki's son; this begins the relationship rather than confirming one that already existed.
- After AOU: Thor tries to protect Henry without turning him into a possession or replacing Loki's role.
- Loki: initially defensive, suspicious, curious, and bad at ordinary fatherhood; later choices create the possibility of affection.
- Infinity War: Loki's death leaves Henry with an incomplete relationship rather than a clean reconciliation.
- Loki series era: the variant is not Henry's father, but his existence opens the records needed to understand the TVA.

**Primary sources:** `The_Art_of_Burning.txt`, `The_Trickster_and_the.txt`, `Dost_Thou_Even_Steal.txt`, selected structural functions from `The_Spider.txt`.

### 5.5 Side story 4: **The Avengers' Child Problem**

Adults repeatedly disagree about what to do with a child who can help in emergencies:

- Tony sees a preventable liability and an engineering problem.
- Steve sees a person who needs a choice and an exit route.
- Natasha sees a pattern before she says anything.
- Bruce sees the medical danger of thermal manipulation.
- Thor sees, after the reveal, a child caught in Loki's orbit.
- Clint focuses on practical safety and home.
- Henry rejects being classified as a weapon, mascot, or mascot-shaped liability.

This line appears in small briefings, medical conversations, travel restrictions, and post-mission arguments. It must not turn Henry into a junior Avenger on demand.

### 5.6 Side story 5: **The Five-Year Quiet**

Henry is snapped with Peter. He does not live through the five-year gap, so he remains physically aligned with Peter after the return. Elena survives and ages five years; the adults carry the memory of Henry's absence. This creates a reunion problem rather than a second childhood.

The five-year period is primarily told through interludes from Elena, Thor, Natasha/Steve, and the world, not by pretending Henry experienced years he did not live.

### 5.7 Side story 6: **The Child in the File**

This is the late TVA/Sylvie line. It begins only after *Endgame*, *No Way Home*, *Multiverse of Madness*, and the *Loki* material have made explicit timeline travel part of the narrative vocabulary.

The author truth is fixed now, but reader access is delayed:

- first, a TVA file with a misclassified infant anomaly;
- then, an escaping Sylvie who takes the child without a plan for fatherhood or heroism;
- then, the Sacred Timeline drop and concealment enchantment;
- finally, Henry learns that someone saved him for a reason that was emotional and immediate, not prophetic.

## 6. Tone, voice, and point of view

### 6.1 Default point of view

Henry is the primary narrator in close first person. This carries the resource-bank strength of practical, dialogue-heavy MCU narration while keeping author knowledge separate from character knowledge.

Recommended distribution:

- 70% Henry first person;
- 15% Peter or Elena close POV when their information cannot be shown through Henry;
- 15% carefully controlled interludes from Thor, Loki, Eleanor, Sylvie, TVA personnel, or other canon characters.

The percentage is a planning guide, not a mathematical requirement.

### 6.2 Henry's voice

- practical before poetic;
- funny when cornered, not funny instead of feeling;
- tactical without being omniscient;
- willing to notice food, laundry, bus schedules, wet gloves, and medical tape;
- suspicious of grand statements;
- emotionally indirect until a choice forces directness;
- not a miniature Tony Stark, Loki, or Peter;
- able to be wrong without narration humiliating him for sport.

### 6.3 Action style

Action must show:

1. where Henry is standing;
2. where the water, heat, civilians, exits, and hazards are;
3. what he chooses to freeze or not freeze;
4. how the construct fails or changes;
5. what the rescue costs afterward.

Named attacks are optional and rare. A shape has a function before it has a name.

### 6.4 Emotional style

Use practical care as emotional language:

- Peter carries spare gloves without announcing why;
- Elena replaces ruined towels and asks what happened without pretending not to know;
- Thor remembers Henry dislikes being lifted out of danger;
- Loki returns a repaired object but refuses to explain the first attempt;
- Henry builds a better ice brace for someone else before he admits he is scared.

This is the slow found-family method drawn from the resource bank. Declarations can occur, but they must follow behavior.

## 7. Power and gear story bible

### 7.1 The power's physical rule

Henry manipulates heat and available moisture, then shapes ice with concentration. He does not create arbitrary amounts of ice from nothing. Atmospheric extraction is possible only as he grows and is expensive in dry or hot environments.

### 7.2 Cost model

Every serious use draws from some combination of:

- hydration;
- calories;
- concentration;
- body heat;
- muscular stamina;
- recovery time;
- tolerance for numbness and pain;
- environmental moisture.

The story never displays a mana bar. The cost is shown through behavior: shaking hands, thirst, missed meals, headaches, numb fingers, delayed reactions, hypothermia risk, and the need to stop.

### 7.3 Progression philosophy

Progression is mostly **control, timing, movement, and judgment**, not larger blasts.

1. Frost touch and accidental icing.
2. Small controlled freeze and moisture gathering.
3. Needle, wedge, cuff, hand shield, and frost film.
4. Short ramp, slide, brace, and emergency barrier.
5. Layered wall, route control, partial ice-body adaptation.
6. Team-scale battlefield shaping with preparation and serious cost.
7. Later thermal precision and limited atmospheric work.

### 7.4 Hard exclusions

- no instant blood-freezing;
- no casual organ attacks;
- no city-scale weather control;
- no effortless permanent constructs;
- no invulnerable ice body;
- no full hydromancy;
- no free healing;
- no power copied from every Frost Giant or Asgardian ability;
- no progression that makes Peter's movement and rescue expertise irrelevant.

### 7.5 Mask and equipment

Henry's first mask is improvised and bad. Over time it becomes safer, washable, weather-resistant, and compatible with his breathing and cold. His equipment is built or repaired through ordinary means, Peter's advice, occasional Avengers resources, and Henry's own mistakes.

The mask has three functions:

- physical safety;
- privacy;
- permission to act before he knows who he is.

The story must not treat the costume as the moment Henry becomes a hero. He is already making choices before the mask works.

## 8. Mystery and reveal bible

### 8.1 Mystery layers

**Layer 1 — ordinary gaps:** missing father, incomplete records, foster paperwork, a date that does not align.
**Layer 2 — biological oddity:** cold resistance and readings that do not look fully human.
**Layer 3 — historical link:** Eleanor's papers and the 1971 D.B. Cooper event.
**Layer 4 — Asgardian identity:** Loki's biological relationship confirmed in AOU.
**Layer 5 — time displacement:** Henry's birth/age records cannot be reconciled.
**Layer 6 — TVA truth:** infant capture, Sylvie's escape, Sacred Timeline drop, concealment enchantment.

### 8.2 AOU confirmation method

The reveal is a **combined evidence event**, not one magical answer:

1. Henry's blood has unusual markers revealed during a medical/technical check connected to the Avengers' crisis.
2. A sealed S.H.I.E.L.D. sample from the Battle of New York provides a reference from Loki. The sample is a connective addition from cleanup/evidence storage, not a claim that the films show this file.
3. Henry's markers match Loki's at a biological level, not merely to “some alien.”
4. Eleanor's notebook and metal token connect her brief relationship to the 1971 D.B. Cooper event.
5. Thor recognizes the private Asgardian story and identifies the man in the historical evidence as Loki.
6. The Mind Stone may produce pressure, visions, or emotional disturbance around the event, but it does not perform the paternity test.

The confirmation should arrive in fragments because the AOU plot is still moving. The characters do not pause the Sokovia crisis for a genealogy lecture.

### 8.3 Henry's response

Henry's first response is not a clean emotion. It is a stack:

- “That cannot be right.”
- “If it is right, why did nobody know?”
- “Why does this explain some things but not my age?”
- “Do I have to call him anything?”
- “Is Thor looking at me differently now?”
- “Is Peter going to make a joke, and if he does, will I let him?”

Curiosity is allowed to coexist with anger. Hatred is not required.

### 8.4 Loki's response

Loki's first moves are recognizable MCU Loki:

- suspicion of manipulation;
- anger at being used as an explanation;
- testing Henry with questions;
- humor at inappropriate moments;
- attempts to control the conversation;
- genuine curiosity he refuses to call tenderness;
- a refusal to perform a perfect father role.

His later growth is measured through what he does when he cannot gain anything by doing it.

### 8.5 TVA reveal

The TVA truth is held until the long-form story has earned it. It should explain the chronology without retroactively turning every early scene into TVA surveillance.

Final author-side mechanics:

- Henry is born in 1972.
- TVA hunter protocols flag the infant as a non-native biological anomaly whose branch has crossed a pruning threshold.
- The TVA contains him rather than immediately pruning him because the case is both biologically unusual and temporally impossible in their records.
- Sylvie's escape occurs during that operation from her subjective point of view, even though TVA time does not map cleanly to Earth dates.
- She steals the infant and a damaged TemPad, then selects a low-visibility Sacred Timeline insertion point in Queens in 2000.
- The drop produces a foundling record rather than a magically aged teenager. Henry is an infant when he arrives and grows normally.
- She leaves him with the Cooper surname, either through a surviving blanket/label or a brief enchantment that preserves the name. She does not create a detailed fake family history.
- Her concealment enchantment masks Jotun/Asgardian signatures and softens temporal detection; it does not erase Henry's memories because an infant has no usable narrative memory to erase.
- The TVA assumes the original anomaly was lost during the escape and later reads the 2000 foundling as an ordinary human case.
- The enchantment weakens near major temporal stresses, TVA technology, and Henry's own increasingly controlled use of his heritage-linked power.

This is not a chosen-one rescue. Sylvie saves one child because she recognizes the shape of what is being done to him.

## 9. Canon integration bible

### 9.1 Event placement

| MCU station | Henry's narrative position |
|---|---|
| *The Avengers* | A child witness through news, adult reaction, and later school memory. No New York fight. |
| *Iron Man 3* | Background public trauma and Tony's changed behavior; Henry is not inserted into Extremis. |
| *The Dark World* | Optional short aftermath/contained Asgardian consequence. No reveal. Thor/Henry remains limited acquaintance. |
| *Winter Soldier* | Street-level pressure, surveillance, and institutional failure; the boys do not solve the Triskelion plot. |
| *Guardians* | Mostly off-screen cosmic station; Henry hears about it later. |
| *Age of Ultron* | Main first-book climax and paternity reveal; Henry supports without replacing canon heroes. |
| *Ant-Man* | Interlude/background unless later outline proves a useful small contact. |
| *Civil War* | Peter's recruitment remains Tony's choice; Henry's allegiance is separate and conflicted. |
| *Homecoming* | Peter's school and Vulture story remain central; Henry is friend/support, not solver. |
| *Doctor Strange* | Mystic rules become available but do not explain Henry's age. |
| *Ragnarok* | Thor/Loki/Asgard story remains central; Henry's relationship with Thor is tested. |
| *Infinity War* | Henry helps in a plausible Earth-side role and is Snapped; Loki's death ends the first phase of fatherhood. |
| *Endgame* | Henry returns through the Snap reversal; the time heist creates the first direct TVA pressure. |
| *WandaVision* | Peripheral aftermath, grief, public institutions, and the changed world. Wanda is not Henry's diagnosis. |
| *Far From Home* | Peter's grief and Mysterio remain central; Henry is friend and pressure point. |
| *No Way Home* | First full multiverse contact in the Peter line; Henry helps but does not own Peter's choices. |
| *Multiverse of Madness* | Reality travel becomes dangerous and explicit. Henry's TVA mystery becomes accessible, not automatically solved. |
| *Loki* | The 2012 variant and Sylvie provide records and truth; distinguish the variant from Henry's father. |

### 9.2 Canon character independence test

Before adding Henry to a canon event, ask:

1. Would this event still happen without Henry?
2. Does the canon character still make the defining choice?
3. Does Henry's presence create a cost, complication, or local rescue rather than a replacement victory?
4. Is Henry's knowledge at this point plausible?
5. Does the scene need Henry, or is this only a cameo impulse?

If the answer to question five is “only a cameo,” use an interlude or omit the scene.

## 10. Stakes ladder

The escalation is deliberately uneven.

1. **Body:** Henry's power hurts him and can hurt someone else.
2. **Home:** Elena can lose trust, sleep, money, or legal security.
3. **School:** secrets, injuries, and public footage affect ordinary life.
4. **Neighborhood:** rescues involve police, criminals, fire, traffic, and witnesses.
5. **Institution:** S.H.I.E.L.D., HYDRA, Avengers, and government systems classify him.
6. **Family:** Loki's identity changes how Henry is seen by Thor and Asgard.
7. **City/world:** Ultron, Sokovia, Accords, Ragnarok, and Thanos alter the world.
8. **Time:** Henry's age and records become impossible.
9. **Reality:** multiversal contact threatens the boundaries around identity.
10. **Choice:** Henry decides what to do with a life no one person fully authored.

A later scale does not erase an earlier one. After a fight with Thanos, Henry still needs to eat, sleep, explain a missed assignment, and decide whether to call Elena.

## 11. Relationship contracts

### Henry/Peter

Their friendship must contain:

- unequal experience without a permanent hierarchy;
- jokes that are affectionate and occasionally badly timed;
- arguments over secrecy and risk;
- practical teaching in both directions;
- school life that exists independently of costumes;
- a bond that survives Civil War disagreements and the Snap;
- no romance required to make the friendship meaningful.

### Henry/Thor

Before the reveal: limited acquaintance only.
After the reveal: awkward concern, boundaries, gradual care, and a relationship that neither boy is required to name immediately.

### Henry/Loki

Biology creates a question, not a relationship. Loki has to learn that Henry's life is not a territory he can claim. Henry has to decide whether curiosity can coexist with boundaries.

### Henry/Elena

Elena is not an obstacle to the superhero plot. She is the ordinary adult whose practical care makes the story's stakes real. She can be angry without being cruel, frightened without becoming controlling, and supportive without approving every decision.

### Henry/Avengers

The team is not a replacement family waiting to adopt him. Individual relationships develop at different speeds. Steve offers boundaries; Natasha notices; Tony worries loudly; Bruce explains risks; Clint asks whether he has eaten; Thor changes after the reveal.

## 12. Ending contract

The story's final resolution should include:

- the TVA/Sylvie truth being fully legible;
- Henry understanding that his life was displaced and concealed, not magically fabricated;
- acknowledgement that Sylvie's rescue was compassionate but also a violation of his right to know;
- Loki's death and variant distinction remaining emotionally real;
- Thor's bond with Henry resting on post-reveal choices;
- Peter and Henry choosing a future friendship that is not dependent on being the only two young heroes;
- Elena's role in Henry's life not being erased by the biological reveal;
- Henry choosing a name, mask, or public/private balance because it fits him now, not because blood demands it;
- no coronation, prophecy, or cosmic throne as a substitute for character resolution.

The last emotional victory is not defeating a bigger enemy. It is Henry no longer needing the impossible parts of his past to make his present valid.

## 13. Planning quality gates

No chapter packet may be written until the relevant arc passes these checks:

### Story check

- What external problem is active?
- What relationship changes?
- What knowledge changes?
- What consequence carries forward?
- Which canon event remains independent?

### Resource check

- Which source function is being adapted?
- Is it labeled SOURCE-DERIVED, SOURCE-COMBINED, CONNECTIVE, ORIGINAL, or CANON?
- Has plot/prose/character material been copied instead of adapted?
- Does the scene belong to Henry and the MCU after adaptation?

### Continuity check

- What does each viewpoint character know now?
- What is Henry's age and power state here?
- Where are Peter's identity and school obligations?
- Is Thor still only an acquaintance before AOU?
- Are TVA/multiverse facts withheld until earned?

### Tone check

- Is the narration practical and dialogue-driven?
- Is the danger physical and legible?
- Is humor relieving pressure rather than erasing pain?
- Has the story manufactured a loss, humiliation, or defeat without cause?
