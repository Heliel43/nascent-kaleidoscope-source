# Henry Cooper — Foundation Pass

**Status:** planning / continuity pass only
**Scope:** first-response deliverable; no Chapter 1 has been written.
**Chapter scale:** target average of 10,000 words, with variation when a chapter's movement requires it.
**Working date:** 2026-09-06.

This document establishes the first story foundation for the Henry Cooper project. It is deliberately not a novel outline pretending that every future answer is already known. The resource bank supplies the scene grammar, useful mechanics, relationship patterns, and several structural solutions. MCU canon supplies the chronological skeleton. Henry is the new element that has to fit into both without swallowing either.

## Label key

- **CANON** — MCU material that remains true unless a listed divergence changes it.
- **SOURCE-DERIVED** — primarily adapted from one resource-bank work.
- **SOURCE-COMBINED** — assembled from multiple resource-bank functions.
- **CONNECTIVE** — original material needed to make canon and source material meet.
- **ORIGINAL** — a genuinely new Henry-specific decision or plot element.
- **WORKING** — a planning decision, not yet a locked scene fact.

The labels identify provenance, not quality. A source-derived scene still has to be rewritten for MCU characterization, Henry's age, and the established continuity.

# A. MCU canon baseline

## A1. What the MCU gives us

1. **CANON — Loki's D.B. Cooper identity.** *Loki* episode 1 establishes that the 1971 hijacker was Loki, operating under the alias D. B. Cooper. Loki says the stunt followed a lost bet with Thor; he jumped from the plane and was taken back to Asgard by the Bifrost. The series uses the event as a joke and a piece of Loki's pre-*Thor* history, not as a secret mythology arc. The fanfiction addition is only that Loki also met Henry's mother during that Earth-side period and never learned that she became pregnant.
2. **CANON — Loki's family.** Loki is Laufey's biological son, Odin and Frigga's adopted son, and Thor's adopted brother. He is a sorcerer with, among other abilities, cryokinesis, shapeshifting, conjuration, telekinesis, teleportation, and illusion-based skills. Henry's ice powers can therefore carry a later, retroactive biological resonance without making the early story announce the answer.
3. **CANON — Thor and Loki are complicated, not simple enemies.** Thor and Loki know one another intimately, repeatedly betray one another, and still retain attachment. Thor's belief that Loki can change is a useful characterization anchor. Henry must not become a replacement Loki or a device that makes Thor stop caring about the existing brother.
4. **CANON — Peter Parker is a teenage Midtown student.** Peter's first speaking MCU appearance is in *Captain America: Civil War*. The films establish him as a very young student from Queens who is already operating as Spider-Man before Tony recruits him. The exact birth date and the calendar math around his age contain continuity fuzziness. The story will use the on-screen school-age characterization and the film's stated age rather than pretend every date is mathematically clean.
5. **CANON — *Avengers: Age of Ultron* is spring 2015 in the working official timeline.** The Avengers raid Strucker's Sokovian HYDRA base, recover Loki's scepter, and return it to Avengers Tower. Tony and Bruce use the intelligence in the scepter to awaken Ultron. Ultron turns on the Avengers, works with Wanda and Pietro, seeks vibranium and a new body, and ultimately raises Sokovia. Vision is born from the Mind Stone, Thor's lightning, the cradle, and the Avengers' intervention. The team wins, but Sokovia is destroyed and the Avengers' roster changes.
6. **CANON — *The Dark World* is not the Henry reveal.** The film remains the Malekith/Aether/Convergence story. Loki is imprisoned, Frigga dies, Loki fakes his death, and he ultimately takes Odin's place. Henry can be present only in a small, plausible side lane or in aftermath material. No paternity reveal occurs there.
7. **CANON — TVA material is later.** In *Loki*, the TVA exists outside ordinary time and space, enforces the Sacred Timeline as presented in season 1, and treats Loki's 2012 Tesseract escape as a temporal violation. The narrative later reveals that the TVA's official story is incomplete and that branching timelines and multiversal consequences are real. This material is reserved for a later escalation, not used as an early explanation for every oddity.

## A2. Canon usage rule

The story will not treat MCU canon as a checklist of cameos. Each station needs to remain recognizable on its own terms:

- *The Avengers* remains the New York invasion and the first public super-team event.
- *Iron Man 3* remains Tony's post-invasion PTSD and the Extremis/Mandarin plot.
- *The Dark World* remains an Asgardian family and Aether story.
- *The Winter Soldier* remains the HYDRA/S.H.I.E.L.D. collapse.
- *Guardians of the Galaxy* remains a primarily cosmic story; Henry does not need to be sent into space because the title is on the timeline.
- *Age of Ultron* remains an Ultron story. Henry's reveal must happen in the margins and pressure points of the film, not replace its central conflict.

### Chronology decision

The story begins in **fall 2014**, not in 1971 and not in the middle of *The Dark World*. This gives enough time for:

- Henry to be a genuinely inexperienced hero without making him a preschooler;
- Peter and Henry to have a meaningful friendship before spring 2015;
- Thor to have several limited encounters with Henry before the reveal, without creating a close bond;
- the AOU reveal to feel like a consequence of accumulated clues rather than a random genealogy twist.

The 1971 material is a buried story layer. The audience can learn that it exists before it understands what it means.

# C. Peter Parker / Henry timeline solution

## C1. Smallest workable butterfly

**WORKING choice:** Peter's spider bite and first low-level patrols happen earlier than the films show, in late 2013 or early 2014. This does not change the events of *Civil War*, Tony's recruitment, Peter's first public MCU speaking appearance, or the eventual *Homecoming* plot. It only fills an unshown period in Peter's backstory.

Henry is placed in the same Midtown cohort as Peter in fall 2014. Both are approximately **13–14** at the start, both are young for the role they are trying to play, and both reach the AOU period at roughly the same age. The exact school-year mathematics is tracked as a known MCU ambiguity rather than repeatedly discussed in dialogue.

This is preferable to:

- making Peter secretly much older;
- moving the entire MCU chronology;
- giving Henry a magical school placement for no reason;
- making Peter a full Avenger before canon does;
- explaining away the friendship with a multiverse crossover.

## C2. Sequence of the relationship

1. **Masked first contact.** Henry has been operating for several days/weeks and takes a situation beyond his skill. Spider-Man intervenes. Peter is not an expert; he wins because he is more experienced in movement, rescue priorities, and reading a street fight.
2. **The rescue is not a recruitment speech.** Peter first helps Henry get out alive, then gives him practical criticism. Henry is annoyed because the criticism is correct.
3. **School collision.** Henry and Peter meet as civilian classmates. Peter has the exact combination of bad excuses, suspicious injuries, disappearing at the wrong time, and implausibly useful school knowledge that makes the secret obvious to a careful observer.
4. **Henry discovers Peter first.** Peter does not dramatically unmask in front of Henry. Henry notices the pattern, tests it, and tells Peter that his secret identity is terrible. This preserves the requested dynamic and gives Henry an earned social advantage over Peter.
5. **Rules before trust.** Peter teaches Henry how to avoid collateral damage, how to leave before police arrive, how to handle a civilian who knows too much, and how not to freeze a person when a weapon or limb would do.
6. **Partnership.** The two remain friends when they are not in costume. School jokes, unfinished homework, bad food, and arguments about patrol routes matter as much as fights.
7. **Experience without flattening the age gap.** Peter is more practiced, not wiser in every area. Henry has better control of a power Peter cannot imitate; Peter has better movement, improvisation, and rescue instincts. They fill different gaps.
8. **AOU separation.** Peter is not pulled into the AOU Avengers operation. Henry is close enough to be involved in a limited way. This prevents the friendship from becoming a reason to erase Peter's canon absence.

## C3. Peter's secret-identity rule

Henry's line is not a one-off gag. It becomes a recurring relationship joke with practical consequences:

> Peter's civilian identity is protected by a mask; his behavior is not.

Examples of the recurring pattern, to be adapted rather than copied:

- Peter arrives at school with the exact injury Spider-Man suffered the night before.
- Peter uses an excuse that would be convincing if he had not already used it three times.
- Henry quietly hands Peter an ice pack while saying nothing.
- Peter loudly denies being Spider-Man while wearing a school backpack that Henry saw hanging from a rooftop.

**SOURCE-COMBINED:** *The Spider*'s school/hero balancing and amateur-costume logic + the resource bank's recurring mask/identity misunderstandings + original Henry/Peter banter.

# D. Henry Cooper's starting point

## D1. Public facts

- **Name:** Henry Cooper.
- **Age at opening:** approximately 13–14; exact birth record is a future continuity clue, not an early exposition dump.
- **Residence:** Queens, in a foster/guardian arrangement close enough to attend Midtown. He has a bed, food, school, and at least one adult contact. The story is not going to use orphanhood as an excuse for endless abuse scenes.
- **Family:** his mother is dead; his biological father is absent from every ordinary record. Henry has no reason to suspect Loki at opening.
- **School:** Midtown School of Science and Technology, same class/cohort as Peter under the small butterfly in section C.
- **Social position:** smart enough to be bored sometimes, not a genius who solves every problem; socially functional but guarded; funny when cornered; more comfortable making a plan than discussing a feeling.
- **Inciting condition:** his cold/ice manipulation awakens before the first chapter's main action. The opening should show the awakening and its immediate practical problem, not explain his ancestry.

## D2. Henry's voice

**SOURCE-COMBINED / ORIGINAL:** The voice should draw from the resource bank's QQ-style practical narrators rather than from generic epic fantasy narration.

- Short and medium paragraphs during ordinary scenes.
- Dialogue does most of the work.
- Internal thought is frequent enough to show planning, but not every emotion becomes an essay.
- Mundane details are welcome: school forms, wet socks, cheap gloves, an empty fridge, an ice pack melting on homework.
- A joke can interrupt a serious beat without erasing the seriousness.
- Henry is allowed to be frightened, but he does not need to narrate himself as a doomed antihero.
- When he loses, the loss should change a decision or a technique.
- When he wins, the win should create a new logistical problem: witnesses, property damage, an injury, a suspicious video, or a relationship complication.

Henry's orphanhood is a fact that influences logistics and trust. It is not a license for the story to manufacture a new tragedy every arc.

# E. Henry's power system

## E1. Design basis

**SOURCE-COMBINED:**

- The user's Hie Hie no Mi / ice manipulation premise.
- Canon Loki's official on-screen cryokinesis and Frost Giant heritage.
- *Enchanting Melodies*' distinction between pure manipulation of ice/cold and basic beginner limits.
- *Yakuza*'s useful separation between ice control and water control, along with beginner constructs such as needles, icicles, arrows, bucklers, skins, walls, mines, and slippery terrain.
- *Wandering Prince* and *Burn the World Down*'s emphasis on experimenting, training through failure, sensing an element, and using an element as part of movement rather than as a collection of named attacks.
- *Borne of Caution*'s careful observation and risk assessment.

The game-system labels, gacha rewards, harem material, and effortless progression are **not** imported.

## E2. Core rule

Henry does not create arbitrary ice from nothing at arbitrary scale. He manipulates thermal energy and available water/moisture, then shapes ice with focus. He can eventually produce frost in dry air, but the cost rises sharply as humidity and available water fall.

His power has three linked modes:

### 1. Thermal manipulation

Henry can draw heat out of a touched or nearby object, causing frost, freezing liquids, cooling overheated machinery, or slowing a hot surface. Early on, contact is much safer and more precise than distance.

### 2. Ice shaping

He can shape existing ice or newly frozen water into shields, wedges, spikes, cuffs, ramps, platforms, thin armor, walls, and temporary tools. Complexity depends on concentration. A wall is easier than a moving articulated construct; a rough bridge is easier than a precise lockpick.

### 3. Cold-body adaptation

Henry is unusually resistant to cold and can partially ice over his skin or limbs. This is not total intangibility. Impacts can shatter the layer; heat, electricity through wet ice, vibration, and repeated attacks remain dangerous. If a limb is fully frozen and broken, recovery requires water, time, and metabolic cost; it is not a free reset button.

## E3. Boundaries

| Area | Early rule | What improves later |
|---|---|---|
| Range | Touch to a few metres; line-of-sight shaping is crude. | Range and precision, but never effortless city-wide control. |
| Moisture | Easier near water, rain, snow, pipes, or condensation; difficult in heated dry rooms. | Better atmospheric extraction and recycling of meltwater. |
| Living targets | A sustained touch can numb or immobilize. Instant blood-freezing is not available. | More precise non-lethal restraint, still resisted by durable or powered targets. |
| Temperature | Can cool objects and make frost. Cannot casually drop an entire room to lethal temperature. | Local cold fields and larger freezes require preparation and serious exhaustion. |
| Constructs | Small spikes, buckler, ice skin, short ramps, and crude walls. | Larger barriers, layered structures, moving platforms, and battlefield shaping. |
| Elemental body | Partial ice-body effects are possible; physical force still transfers and can damage him. | Better reforming and defensive timing, never invulnerability. |
| Water control | Henry can freeze and redirect water he can affect, but he is not a full hydromancer. | More efficient conversion, not unrestricted waterbending. |
| Heat/fire | Cold can counter ordinary heat and buy time. Energy weapons, magical fire, and sustained high heat can overwhelm him. | Better thermal choices, not universal fire immunity. |
| Cost | Hydration, calories, body heat, focus, headaches, numbness, and fatigue. | Stamina and technique improve; the cost never disappears. |

## E4. Progression checkpoints

- **Awakening:** frost touch, accidental icing, cold sensitivity, small ice film, one crude spike, and a bad first rescue.
- **First patrol:** hand-sized shield, cuffs, slippery patch, short ramp, and controlled cooling. Henry is still more likely to make the wrong shape than the perfect one.
- **Peter partnership:** movement becomes the breakthrough: ice slides, short platforms, emergency braces, and walls used to redirect rather than simply attack.
- **Thor/Avengers period:** Henry can freeze sections of Ultron drones, build evacuation barriers, and shape a temporary route. He cannot solo Ultron, survive a direct energy barrage indefinitely, or decide the Sokovia battle by himself.
- **Later:** large-scale freezes become a team tactic with water, time, and preparation. They remain costly enough that using one is a decision, not a default special effect.

## E5. Power-related mystery

The story may allow characters to wonder whether Henry's abilities resemble Frost Giant or Asgardian magic. That is an observation, not an answer. Sylvie's later concealment enchantment hides the Jotun/Asgardian origin well enough that early tests can produce ambiguous results. Henry's power is first treated as a power he has to train, not as proof that he has a destiny.

# F. Resource-bank harvest

The repository contains **58 text files totaling roughly 26.8 million words**, plus a PDF duplicate/alternate of *All the Young Dudes*. The following is the relevant index, not a claim that every file needs to be mined for this story.

## F1. Priority sources

### 1. `The_Spider.txt` — direct MCU structural source

**SOURCE-DERIVED / SOURCE-COMBINED uses:**

- Chapter 1–2: sudden power discovery; the immediate argument between wanting a normal life and choosing to help; improvised first costume; first high-risk rescue; an adult at home being angry because the protagonist is alive and reckless; the next-day school transition.
- Chapters 3–5: school as a real location rather than a pause between fights; friendship and social awkwardness; making equipment badly before improving it.
- Chapters 13–15: allies moving from isolated heroics into a team, a first mission, and the consequences of adult organizations noticing a young hero.
- Chapters 19–20: Avengers recruitment/briefing rhythms and the tension of a young hero standing among established adults.
- Chapters 21–29: Loki/Thor/SHIELD material, evidence work, investigations, and the cost of treating a young hero as useful.

**Adaptation rule:** use the scene functions and pacing, not the SI protagonist, Felicia/other romance plots, Fantastic Four continuity, Peter's parents plot, or explicit material. This is the most important single source because it already understands an MCU Peter balancing school, a mask, family, and increasingly large threats.

### 2. `enchanting melody.txt` — grounded cryomancy and slow school worldbuilding

**SOURCE-DERIVED mechanics:** the cryomancy passage in the Basilisk arc distinguishes elemental manipulation from a list of spells. A beginner can move an ice cube but cannot yet strip heat from the atmosphere or create giant icicles. Mastery takes years/decades. Cold can alter a battlefield without instantly winning it.

**Adaptation:** Henry starts with the ice-cube equivalent: small, useful, unimpressive control. The source's description of controlled, rhythmic detachment can inform the sensation of using ice, but it will be translated into Henry's voice. School, friendships, research, and emotional obligations can continue while the power develops.

### 3. `Yakuza.txt` — cryomancer taxonomy and construct ladder

**SOURCE-DERIVED mechanics only:** the source separates cryomancy from hydromancy and provides a logical progression from needles and icicles to arrows, short blades, bucklers, ice skin, walls, slippery floors, mines, and larger structures. It also makes the character notice when a defensive technique is useful before chasing a bigger attack.

**Adaptation:** remove levels, mana bars, gacha, system prompts, instant healing, and lethal escalation. Keep the design insight that defense, movement, and battlefield control are as important as projectiles.

### 4. `I just want to learn magic bro.txt` — QQ voice, mundane practicality, anti-trope posture

**SOURCE-DERIVED style/function:** the narrator undercuts grandiose genre expectations with practical domestic problems, uses humor to keep the protagonist from becoming an edgelord, and treats power as something that has to fit around food, chores, guardians, and ordinary conversation.

**Adaptation:** Henry can be wry and practical without becoming a copy of the narrator or acquiring the source's magic/gacha system. This is a style reference, not a plot source.

### 5. `A_Nascent_Kaleidoscope.txt` — awakening and absent-guardian tension

**SOURCE-DERIVED structure:** school scenes that are interrupted by inexplicable personal information; a protagonist who notices that his life has been arranged by an unseen guardian; a reveal of old memories that answers some questions while creating more; emotional reactions that are angry and practical rather than a single melodramatic speech.

**Adaptation:** this is useful for Henry's missing father, inconsistent records, and the later chronology mystery. Do not import the reincarnation, Highschool DxD cosmology, or sudden omniscient knowledge.

### 6. `The_Art_of_Burning.txt` — slow found-family process

**SOURCE-DERIVED relationship model:** trust is built through repeated practical care, shared work, cultural misunderstandings, and choices made when nobody is obligated to be kind. A damaged teenager does not instantly call a new protector “Dad.”

**Adaptation:** use this for Thor/Henry and later for the wider Avengers found-family layer. Keep the happy, patient movement; do not import the source's darker captivity/abuse plot.

### 7. `Wandering prince.txt` and `Burn the World Down (Gamer SI, Avatar The Last Airbender).txt` — elemental practice and movement

**SOURCE-DERIVED techniques:** training through experiments, learning from failure, using sensory perception of temperature/energy, combining elemental control with footwork and tools, and having family banter interrupt serious practice.

**Adaptation:** no gamer system, no Fire Nation plot, no named skill screens. Henry's skating, ramps, thermal sensing, and rescue-oriented positioning can grow from these functions.

### 8. `Borne_of_Caution.txt` — caution and tactical observation

**SOURCE-DERIVED structure:** the protagonist studies the environment, treats unfamiliar powers as dangerous, and makes plans around what is not known. The story also lets training and emotional support coexist with action.

**Adaptation:** Henry's first lesson is not “make a bigger spike”; it is “know where the water is, where the civilians are, and what happens if the ice breaks.”

### 9. `Kitsu.txt` — young cape logistics, reputation, and consequences

**SOURCE-DERIVED structure:** a young powered character gets absorbed into a cape ecosystem, argues about whether she is a hero, is forced to negotiate rules with adults, and learns that accidental harm cannot be treated as a joke even when the narration is funny.

**Adaptation:** Henry can debate the word “hero” with Peter, deal with school and patrol schedules, and learn from an accidental overreaction. Do not import the source's power set, sexual material, or intentionally extreme violence.

### 10. `Faith_for_the_second_run.txt` and `Throw_Away_Your_Mask.txt` — time anomaly handling

**SOURCE-DERIVED structure:** characters notice temporal discontinuity before they can explain it; they test memory, records, and social behavior; uncertainty is lonely and sometimes absurd; a school day continues even while reality is wrong.

**Adaptation:** use this for Henry's age discrepancy and impossible records. Do not introduce a time loop or time travel into the early plot. The useful part is the method of investigation and delayed comprehension.

### 11. `Dost_Thou_Even_Steal.txt` — masks, loyalty, and chosen bonds

**SOURCE-DERIVED structure:** the “beneath the mask” material and the gradual brotherhood/friendship beats can inform Henry/Peter's trust. The mask is both practical equipment and a social boundary.

**Adaptation:** no Persona cosmology. A mask scene should reveal a choice or change a relationship, not exist only because the costume is visually cool.

### 12. `The_Trickster_and_the.txt` — ensemble rhythm and family by choice

**SOURCE-DERIVED structure:** team briefings, training areas, arguments about methods, limits that matter, post-mission exhaustion, and the idea that a team can become family through chosen responsibility.

**Adaptation:** useful for Avengers-adjacent scenes and Thor/Henry without turning Henry into the leader of the Avengers. Keep the ensemble's ability to disagree.

### 13. `Ghost_in_the_City.txt` and `Burn_Like_A_Dragon_Rise.txt` — support networks and reputation

**SOURCE-DERIVED structure:** a young/isolated protagonist builds a network, earns trust through repeated actions, and encounters family-like responsibility in practical contexts. The orphanage/family logistics in *Burn Like a Dragon* are especially useful as a reminder that care involves money, food, schedules, and protection, not just declarations.

**Adaptation:** Henry's adult support can be quiet and logistical. Avoid manufacturing abuse simply to justify a found family.

## F2. Secondary watch list

- `A_Gamers_Grand_Journey.txt`: large ensemble escalation and action rhythm are potentially useful; the Gamer mechanics, power creep, and harem structure are not.
- `Steal_the_Truth_Reach.txt`: long-term school/friendship and alternate-timeline continuity management.
- `All_the_Young_Dudes.txt`: school-era relationship pacing and mundane detail, not MCU or magic.
- `A_Body_of_Water_and.txt`: water imagery and restrained interpersonal scenes, not its supernatural setting.
- `The_Exhibition_Of_A_Low.txt`: comic timing and social friction, not its protagonist's power fantasy.
- `The Spider` remains the direct MCU anchor; the other MCU-name hits in unrelated files are not automatically relevant just because they mention Thor or Loki.

## F3. Style fingerprint to carry forward

Across the useful source set, the most compatible style characteristics are:

- high dialogue density;
- clear physical blocking in action scenes;
- short paragraphs during danger;
- a mundane detail immediately before or after a major event;
- protagonists who think tactically but still make adolescent decisions;
- jokes used as pressure release, not as a replacement for emotion;
- training shown through failed attempts and small improvements;
- family/friendship expressed through practical acts;
- consequences that create the next scene;
- gradual escalation from a local problem to a wider world.

The story should not default to purple, cinematic, or solemn prose. The resource bank's actual working rhythm outranks genre assumptions.

# G. Proposed story spine

The outline below is a set of stations. It is not a mandatory chapter count. At a 10k average, a chapter should usually contain **three to five linked scenes**, with each scene changing a practical situation, relationship, or piece of knowledge. A chapter can be slow; it cannot be inert.

## Arc 1 — Cold Start: a teenager, a power, and a bad first idea

**Approx. 6–8 chapters / 60–80k words.**
**Labels:** SOURCE-COMBINED + CONNECTIVE.

- Henry's mundane life and first uncontrolled freeze.
- Small experiments: ice in a cup, frost on a railing, a soaked sleeve, a failed attempt to make a lockpick.
- A first rescue where he saves someone but makes the environment more dangerous.
- The provisional mask and name are functional, not iconic.
- He gets noticed by Spider-Man after taking on a situation beyond him.

The arc ends with Spider-Man intervening, not with Henry becoming competent overnight.

## Arc 2 — Same Class, Worse Cover Story

**Approx. 6–8 chapters / 60–80k words.**
**Labels:** SOURCE-DERIVED from *The Spider* + SOURCE-COMBINED from school/identity sources.

- Henry and Peter discover they attend the same Midtown class.
- Peter offers rules; Henry offers criticism; neither is fully qualified to be in charge.
- School, homework, cafeteria politics, and patrol timing become recurring pressure points.
- Henry works out that Peter is Spider-Man because Peter is catastrophically bad at hiding it.
- Their first partnership succeeds at rescue but creates evidence, footage, or adult suspicion.
- Peter's Aunt May/other adult home life keeps the story from turning into two isolated superheroes in a vacuum.

The arc's climax is not a villain with a world-ending weapon. It is a situation in which the boys have to choose between preserving a secret and protecting a person.

## Arc 3 — Rules, Reputation, and the Avengers' shadow

**Approx. 7–9 chapters / 70–90k words.**
**Labels:** SOURCE-COMBINED.

- Winter Soldier-era New York: more surveillance, more fear, and fewer reliable institutions.
- Henry and Peter learn the difference between stopping a mugging and disrupting a larger operation.
- S.H.I.E.L.D. notices a pattern but does not automatically recruit Henry.
- Tony may hear about him before he meets him; Natasha may notice more than she says.
- Henry's power grows mainly in control and movement, not raw scale.
- A first injury forces Henry to admit to Peter that he cannot keep improvising medical care.

This arc ends with Henry being known to a few adults, but not being promoted to an Avenger.

## Arc 4 — Thor, the cold, and the wrong kind of familiarity

**Approx. 6–8 chapters / 60–80k words.**
**Labels:** SOURCE-COMBINED + ORIGINAL relationship work.

- A contained Asgardian/alien aftermath problem gives Thor a reason to meet Henry.
- Thor initially treats him like a brave, underqualified Midgardian child; Henry objects to being treated like a child while proving the point.
- Thor notices Henry's stubbornness, humor, and sideways intelligence. The resemblance to qualities Thor associates with Loki is an observation, not an emotional bond.
- Henry and Thor have several short encounters: a correction during a rescue, a brief battlefield exchange, and perhaps one ordinary conversation. Each ends before it becomes intimate.
- Thor remembers Henry as a competent young hero and may be mildly curious about him, but he does not become protective or personally attached before the reveal.
- Henry learns some Asgardian facts but not the parentage truth. The Dark World remains a background pressure and foreshadowing station, not the reveal.

## Arc 5 — Age of Ultron: the machine, the team, and the name behind the cold

**Approx. 8–10 chapters / 80–100k words.**
**Labels:** CANON backbone + SOURCE-COMBINED ensemble structure + ORIGINAL reveal.

1. The Avengers raid Strucker's base as in canon. Henry is not inserted into the team just to be present.
2. At the Tower, Henry's status as a young powered contact gives him a limited reason to be there: medical observation, evacuation planning, or a controlled technical consultation. Peter is not part of the AOU field team.
3. Ultron awakens and the canon conflict proceeds. Henry helps with containment or civilians, not with replacing Tony, Bruce, Wanda, or Thor in their defining scenes.
4. The first age clue becomes useful: a record, sample, or magical/biological reading about Henry cannot reconcile with the calendar.
5. Thor's prior familiarity is enough for him to recognize Henry's powers and behavior, but not enough to make the revelation feel like a betrayal of an existing close relationship.
6. Evidence links Henry's mother to the D.B. Cooper period. The link is not delivered as an author speech; it is found, compared, and resisted.
7. The paternity conclusion lands during the AOU period, in a private or semi-private scene between canon action beats. The Avengers do not stop fighting Ultron for an hour.
8. Henry reacts with confusion, curiosity, irritation, and the absurd awareness that his father being Loki is an objectively ridiculous fact. He does not automatically hate Loki or embrace him.
9. Thor's reaction is startled and conflicted rather than deeply wounded by a pre-existing bond. He does not blame Henry for Loki. Any real attachment begins after the revelation through choices, not because the outline says Thor already loves him.
10. Loki's first response is delayed only as much as canon requires. He is disbelieving, defensive, amused in the wrong places, curious, and unwilling to perform instant fatherhood.

The arc ends with the truth known but not emotionally solved. The victory over Ultron remains the Avengers' victory; Henry's revelation is an additional fault line.

## Later stations, intentionally less detailed for now

- **Arc 6 — Consequences:** Loki/Henry contact, Thor's divided loyalties, and the practical problem of a secret that cannot remain private.
- **Arc 7 — Civil War:** Peter's recruitment remains recognizable; Henry's position is a separate decision, not a second Spider-Man recruitment.
- **Arc 8 — Doctor Strange / Ragnarok:** magic and Asgard expand the rules, but the multiverse remains out of reach.
- **Arc 9 — Infinity War:** Henry's competence and relationships are tested; no manufactured defeat just to make Thanos look strong.
- **Arc 10 — Endgame:** the Blip, the five-year gap, and Loki's 2012 escape create legitimate temporal pressure.
- **Arc 11 — Post-Endgame:** WandaVision-era consequences and the changed world.
- **Arc 12 — No Way Home:** the first full multiversal access point for the story.
- **Arc 13 — Multiverse of Madness / TVA:** only after the MCU has earned the escalation; TVA questions become answerable rather than decorative.

# H. Major canon divergence points

1. **Peter's unseen early patrol period is moved earlier** so he can meet Henry before *Civil War*. The public film chronology remains recognizable.
2. **Henry attends Peter's school/class** in the same cohort.
3. **Henry becomes a minor known contact of the Avengers before AOU**, but not a replacement Avenger.
4. **Thor meets Henry repeatedly before AOU** but remains an acquaintance and occasional adult contact; the real bond develops after the reveal.
5. **Loki's unknown human relationship in the D.B. Cooper period exists in the story.** It does not overwrite the joke that Loki was D.B. Cooper or turn the hijacking into a grand prophecy.
6. **The Henry/Loki paternity reveal occurs during the AOU era**, not *The Dark World*.
7. **Henry's age is a real temporal mystery.** Public records will not immediately provide the answer; the TVA connection is delayed until later canon makes it possible.

Everything else should be treated as unchanged until a future outline proves a change is necessary.

# I. Relationship progression

## I1. Henry / Peter

| Phase | Relationship fact | Knowledge |
|---|---|---|
| First rescue | Peter is the more experienced masked hero; Henry is annoyed and grateful in unequal amounts. | Neither knows the other's civilian identity. |
| School collision | They are classmates and both are trying to act normal. | Henry suspects; Peter thinks he is hiding things better than he is. |
| Identity discovery | Henry identifies Peter without being told. | Henry knows Peter is Spider-Man; Peter knows Henry has powers, but not all of Henry's capabilities. |
| Training partnership | Peter teaches rescue, movement, timing, and secrecy. Henry teaches Peter how to think about ice terrain and thermal problems. | Both know the other's identities. |
| Friendship | They share school and patrol life. | They know each other's basic home pressures, injuries, and boundaries. |
| Wider MCU | Peter is still a teenager, not a junior Avenger on demand. | Tony/SHIELD/other adults know different amounts. |
| AOU | Their paths are not identical: Henry is closer to the event; Peter is not folded into the AOU team. | Peter knows Henry's secret history is odd, but not the Loki/TVA truth. |

The recurring emotional rule: Peter and Henry can argue without the story treating every argument as a friendship-ending betrayal.

## I2. Henry / Thor

1. **Recognition:** Thor notices courage, stubbornness, and an unusual cold-based power, not a blood relation.
2. **Amusement:** Henry's ability to answer Thor's grand speeches with a practical objection is memorable, but not yet intimate.
3. **Limited respect:** Thor sees Henry choose rescue over a flashy win and files him away as a promising young hero.
4. **Brief instruction:** Thor may offer one or two battlefield corrections, while Henry's ice makes Thor rethink how a non-Asgardian fights. These are short encounters, not mentorship.
5. **Reveal shock:** Thor must process that a young hero he recognizes is Loki's son. He is concerned, suspicious, and protective of the truth without having been emotionally close to Henry.
6. **Post-reveal care:** Thor starts checking on Henry, remembering details, and making room for him only after the revelation.
7. **Long-term:** a brotherly/uncle-like bond can emerge through choices. It is never granted by a family tree alone.

## I3. Henry / Loki

- **Before the reveal:** Loki is absent from Henry's personal life. His name appears only as a public enemy, an Asgardian figure, or an old clue.
- **Evidence stage:** Loki is treated as a hypothesis that sounds absurd, then as a problem that refuses to go away.
- **First reaction:** disbelief; Loki suspects manipulation, a spell, an Avengers trick, or a temporal fraud before accepting biology.
- **First contact:** curiosity and defensive humor. Loki asks questions that sound like interrogation because he does not know how to ask for personal information without making it a contest.
- **Henry's side:** Henry is curious, irritated, suspicious, and entertained by the absurdity. “I hate my father” is not his default reaction because he has spent his life not knowing there was a father to hate.
- **Early boundary:** Loki cannot simply demand access to Henry's life. Henry has a guardian, a school, a friend, and a self that existed without Loki.
- **Long-term possibility:** affection develops only through repeated choices and honesty. Loki is not instantly a perfect father, and Henry is not instantly a grateful son.

# J. Reveal progression and the TVA mystery

## J1. Early clues — audience can notice; characters do not solve

- Henry's official age and some old records do not line up cleanly.
- A school or medical record has a metadata/date anomaly that can be dismissed as clerical error.
- Henry's mother left fewer ordinary records than a person should have.
- An old photograph or document may contain a detail that is harmless in isolation but impossible beside another date.
- Henry's ice sometimes responds to an Asgardian object or magical environment in a way that is not explained as “because he is Loki's son.”
- Thor recognizes something familiar about Henry's style and temper but interprets it as personality.
- Henry occasionally describes a mundane detail he should not know, then dismisses it as a memory, a dream, or something read somewhere.

These clues should be sparse. The audience should be able to miss them on a first read and understand them in retrospect.

## J2. AOU reveal engine — working design, not full scene

The most stable current design is a **two-stage reveal inside the AOU period**:

1. **Evidence:** an Asgardian, scientific, or intelligence record connects Henry's mother to the 1971 D.B. Cooper case. The record is discovered because the AOU crisis puts unusual data, objects, and people in one place; it is not created by the Mind Stone because the plot needs a genealogy.
2. **Confirmation:** Thor and/or an Asgardian method confirms the biological relationship. The exact method remains open until the Henry-mother history is chosen. It must not depend on a random “the scepter knows everything” convenience.
3. **Loki's reaction:** Loki learns through an immediate post-discovery contact, a private Asgardian communication, or a short aftermath scene. He does not need to stand in the middle of the Sokovia battle for the reveal to count as part of the AOU era.

The scene should preserve the AOU rhythm: a discovery, a reaction, an interruption by the continuing crisis, and a later quiet scene where the characters are forced to sit with what they learned.

## J3. What the TVA backstory eventually explains

The intended future backstory now gives the chronology mystery a fixed spine:

- Loki's relationship with Henry's mother belongs to the D.B. Cooper period.
- The TVA later attempts to capture Henry as a baby. The exact reason remains open, but it must connect to his temporal anomaly rather than to generic “chosen one” importance.
- Sylvie is escaping at the same time. Remembering what the TVA did to her own childhood, she takes the infant during the chaos instead of allowing the TVA to keep him.
- Sylvie drops Henry onto the Sacred Timeline and uses an enchantment to conceal his Jotun/Asgardian origin. This is why Henry can grow up inside the ordinary human world without the early story immediately identifying him as Loki's son.

The future explanation still needs to account for:

- the exact date and location of the TVA capture;
- how Henry's Sacred Timeline placement produces his human records and current age;
- who finds and raises him after Sylvie drops him;
- how long the concealment enchantment lasts and what weakens it;
- why the TVA does not simply recapture him;
- how the truth becomes legible only after *Endgame* and *Loki*.

These are implementation questions, not permission to replace the fixed backstory. The early story should plant clues without naming Sylvie, the TVA operation, or the enchantment.

### TVA rules for this story

- No TVA agents in the early school arc.
- No “the TVA did it” answer whenever a normal mystery is inconvenient.
- The TVA is not automatically benevolent; its season-1 presentation is a propaganda layer.
- TVA/multiverse language becomes explicit only after Endgame/Loki makes it narratively available.
- Henry's age mystery is a character problem before it becomes a cosmology problem.

## J4. Locked future backstory anchor

**ORIGINAL / USER-PROVIDED FUTURE PREMISE:** At some point while Henry is still a baby, the TVA attempts to capture him and succeeds. At the same time, Sylvie is escaping the TVA. In the chaos, she takes Henry because she remembers her own childhood being ruined by the TVA. She drops him onto the Sacred Timeline and uses an enchantment to hide his Jotun/Asgardian origin.

This is the eventual backstory. It should be treated as author-side truth while the early chapters preserve the mystery. The exact mechanics are still ours to design: why the TVA targeted Henry, where Sylvie drops him, how his records are established, who raises him, how the enchantment interacts with his powers, and why the TVA does not immediately retrieve him. The answer should make the early age clues click rather than erase them.

Sylvie and Loki must also remain distinct continuity problems. Sylvie's intervention is a later reveal about Henry's survival and concealment; it does not mean the Loki who fathered Henry knew about him, and it does not require Sylvie to be present during AOU.

# M. Open questions and continuity risks

These are deliberately left open rather than being papered over.

1. **Henry's mother:** name, age, location, and what exactly happened to her after the D.B. Cooper encounter. The child-capture/drop premise is fixed; the mother's fate and the evidence chain still need to be designed.
2. **Duration of Loki's Earth-side period:** the canon joke shows the hijacking and Bifrost return, but does not give a full itinerary. The relationship needs to fit without rewriting the gag into an epic secret mission.
3. **AOU confirmation method:** scientific, magical, documentary, or combined. It must be earned and must not make the Mind Stone an all-purpose genealogy device.
4. **Thor's access to the truth:** decide whether he learns through Asgardian archives, Heimdall, an object, or a later conversation. He must not know simply because the outline says he does.
5. **Loki's physical availability in 2015:** he is politically and magically relevant but should not be dragged into Sokovia without a reason. An immediate aftermath contact is currently safer than putting him in the battle. Sylvie's baby-rescue backstory belongs much later and does not require her to appear in AOU.
6. **Guardian arrangement:** select a stable, humane adult arrangement that gives Henry ordinary logistics and someone to worry about him. Avoid replacing the absent father with an abusive foster plot.
7. **Power origin:** Henry's latent Jotun/Asgardian origin is concealed by Sylvie's enchantment. The early story can still let characters consider mutation, magic, or an unknown metahuman mechanism because the enchantment prevents the true answer from being obvious.
8. **Peter's exact grade:** use a working same-class placement and keep the age ambiguity in the ledger. Do not make the characters stop for a school-administration lecture about MCU date errors.
9. **AOU participation:** decide whether Henry is at the Tower, at a support site, or involved in Sokovia evacuation. The safest baseline is Tower/evacuation support, not raid-team membership.
10. **Source transplant boundaries:** no copied dialogue blocks, names, harem material, Gamer mechanics, or unrelated crossover cosmologies. Take functions, reassemble them, and make the resulting scene belong to Henry and the MCU.
11. **Chapter length:** 10k is an average, not a quota. A chapter ends when its local movement is complete. If three chapters in a row only repeat “Henry trains and gets stronger,” the outline has bloated even if each chapter reaches 10k.
12. **Losses and victories:** Henry can win when his plan and circumstances justify it. He can lose when the opponent, terrain, or information gap beats him. Neither outcome should be imposed to satisfy a formula.

## Chapter-level anti-bloat rule

Every planned 10k chapter must have:

- one concrete external problem;
- one relationship movement;
- one piece of continuity or world information that changes future options;
- at least one consequence that carries forward.

A quiet school chapter is valid if Henry and Peter's trust changes, a secret becomes harder to keep, or a power limitation is discovered. A fight chapter is not valid merely because it contains a bigger blast than the last one.
