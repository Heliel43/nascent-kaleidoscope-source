# PHASE 4 — PERSONA 5 ROYAL INTEGRATION: DEEP DESIGN

**Purpose (directive §22 Phase 4):** the complete mechanical and symbolic design for Haru's Persona line, the Meta-Nav, the anchor×Metaverse interaction rules, Theseus's trigger conditions, and the Akechi module gate. Everything here serves directive §9's questions — why Haru is involved, how the search leads him into the Metaverse, and what the Metaverse does to him.

**Locked inputs:** no Velvet Room, no wild card (session 3); one Persona, one evolution (pack); Fool arcana per pack — **amended reading: arcana is intrinsic metadata of the Persona itself (like Captain Kidd = Chariot), visible only to analysis tools, never assigned or discussed by any Room, because there is no Room.**

## 1. DON QUIXOTE — FULL DESIGN

### 1.1 Awakening grammar (session-5 ruling — REVISED)
A Persona awakens when the self revolts against the reality that has deemed it powerless. **Canon offers two grammars:** the rebellion-tear (Ren/Ryuji/Ann — rage → mask → tear → name) and the **shadow-acceptance** (Futaba — the Shadow confronted, heard, and *accepted*; the Persona born of self-reconciliation). **Haru's awakening uses the acceptance grammar.** Locked (session 5): it happens on the Kaneshiro calling-card invasion day (~Jul 6, 2016), inside the Bank of Gluttony, mid-heist, **witnessed by the Thieves** — the climax of a two-front conflict (Haru-vs-Thieves misread brawl → ceasefire; Haru-vs-his-own-Shadow → made up). Full beat map in §1.2.

**No Room, no contract (still locked):** nobody offers terms. The Thieves' reactions carry the lore-weight Morgana would otherwise: an acceptance-type awakening (Futaba-type — a Persona born of *reconciliation with the self*) happening to a civilian intruder with no Palace and no coach is, in Morgana's professional opinion, "not how any of this works." That alarm is a distinguishing mark of the anchor-anomaly and stays unexplained in Book One.

### 1.2 The awakening scene — beat map (ch. 7, "The Bank of Gluttony"; 14 beats; prose-ready architecture, not prose)

**Setting:** calling-card invasion day (~Jul 6). The Thieves are storming the Bank per canon. Haru, who has spent five months and five chapters working an earth-side trail to Junya Kaneshiro, hits the boss's real-world office the same day, because his Nav has been dead weight since May and fists have never let him down yet.

1. **The pull:** the office is wrong before he touches it — the corridor too long, the vending machine hum two beats slow — and the maintenance door his keycard theory says shouldn't open, opens. The castle treats a boy who came to collect as an asset in arrears; the distortion, flaring under the Thieves' invasion, sweeps him in like a tide taking a swimmer. (He is *pulled* — the author's word — and the story must make the pull feel like the bank's, not the app's.)
2. **The vault-lobby:** golden light off rows of glowing ATMs; a heist already in progress somewhere above; alarms with no sound, only pressure. The narrator treats the rules like weather (PDR "Metaverse 101" discipline).
3. **Collision with the heist:** shadow-guards wearing collector faces converge on the intruder — and then the Thieves' path crosses his. **CONFLICT, front one:** a masked boy on their objective's floor, no nav signal, no team. Ryuji swings first, questions never. The brawl: wrestling vs. Personas in a vault hall — the Charge has no Persona behind it yet, and it still buys him three seconds of Yusuke's undivided artistic attention. The team's first look at the style (PDR recognition mechanics, live).
4. **The ceasefire:** Makoto reads what the brawl is telling her — the intruder *bleeds* (a split lip, human red), takes hits like meat, and fights like someone who has never once been trained to quit. Ren stops Skull with one word. Front one resolves: not enemies; worse — a civilian.
5. **The separation:** a civilian, unprotected, deep in a Palace during a distortion flare. The canon rule fires. The castle's greed has been appraising him since the door — and what it finally collects is the thing he has been refusing to carry alone: **his Shadow tears loose.** It drops through the Mementos line under the Bank and comes back ahead of them, wearing his posture, walking his walk, carrying **THE VERDICT** it appraised out of his open mind: *Natsuki Subaru — deceased in absentia. Case closed.* The castle offered to buy it. The Shadow is here to sell it — to him.
6. **CONFLICT, front two:** the Shadow doesn't rage. That's the horror. It *comforts* — his own voice, his own tiredness: *you've carried it five months; sign the paper; rest; grieve; go home.* The Thieves see a boy arguing with a thing wearing his face and do the right thing wrong (Morgana: "don't let it merge—" / Ren, quieter: "wait").
7. **The chase down the vault tiers:** through deposit boxes of taken things — the cognition of everyone Kaneshiro has ever bled — the Shadow walking backward, patient, the way fear is patient. The team follows because Ren says to, and because the intruder is already running.
8. **The offer, priced:** in the deepest tier, the Shadow holds out the paper with both hands, the way you hand a mourner a pen at the counter. The world's kindest lie — everyone's mercy at once: his mother's logistics, his father's silence, his teachers' gentle voices, the city's news cycle. *Sign, and it stops hurting.* The castle hums agreement. It is the most reasonable thing Haru has ever been offered. That is the trap.
9. **The read:** Haru takes the verdict. Reads it. All of it. His hands do not shake, because he has logged worse than this every night for five months — this is just the sentence version of the notebook. And the reading tells him the thing the whole book has been building toward: **the paper is real. His grief is real. The verdict is not.** Feelings don't lie — he read them at a coward once already (ch. 3's confession, the first crack) — and none of these feelings, his or the world's, ever knew where Subaru went.
10. **The refusal without destruction:** he does not tear the paper (cognition props don't burn; he learned that never — but he learned *nothing here is final except what you agree to*). He folds it. Puts it in his own jacket, over the heart, where the notebook lives. "You keep the grief," he tells his Shadow. "I keep the ledger." The two of them agree on the only thing they have ever agreed on: *the ledger is real. The verdict isn't.*
11. **THE ACCEPTANCE → THE NAME:** the Shadow's hands let go of his — and the mask isn't torn off this time; it's *put on*: the washbasin helm, gold in the castle's eye, garbage to everyone else, working. **DON QUIXOTE** — his own refusal given syllables. The lance arrives like a decision already made. Rocinante stamps once in the golden dark and is gone.
12. **The first act:** the Charge — down the vault hall, through the collection-guard wall, clearing the tier while six Persona-users stare. The team's collective professional opinion recalibrates in real time (Ryuji: "okay but that was the coolest thing I've ever seen"; Morgana: "that is NOT how any of this works"; Yusuke, softly: "honest").
13. **The canon re-braid:** above them, the calling card does its work — Kaneshiro's shadow breaks (canon); the Bank comes down around their escape; the surface delivers **Jul 9's public confession**, watched from a rooftop by six Thieves and a boy who invaded the same bank alone. Jurisdiction argument. The truce. Iwai's "I told you" silence when Haru surfaces with a story he can't tell.
14. **The landing:** that night, the notebook gets a new tab — *castles* — and a first entry that is almost calm: *"It was real. It's all real. He could be anywhere."* The delusion's bankruptcy notice, filed as hope. (Raw-line budget: spent here, ch. 7.)

**Voice donor:** Kaleidoscope's flat declaratives under pressure. **Grammar donors:** canon Futaba (acceptance-born), Ace ch. 5 (outsider-awakening staging), DTES vault staging, PDR recognition mechanics. **The distinction that matters:** Ren's generation took their Personas *from* a hostile world; Haru's was *reconciled with* — which is why his never quite obeys like theirs (one beat for Book II: the Persona answers to the ledger, not the boy).

### 1.3 Form & imagery kit (the reader's picture of Quixote)

- **The helm:** the Mambrino washbasin — brass, dented, absurd, *gold in the Metaverse's eye.* Never explained in dialogue (directive §10). Team nickname fuel ("the knight thing"), and the fixed visual joke that isn't one: it never comes off in combat, and it *works.*
- **The lance:** a大道 hand-and-a-half lance, too long, tournament-ornate — a toy knight's ideal made functional. In his hands it moves like a wrestling weapon: spear-thrusts thrown with a lariat's shoulder, sweeps with a jab's timing.
- **Rocinante:** a lean, sway-backed nag that exists only inside the Charge — manifests at the run, gone at the stop. Never rides, never stays. The mobility he doesn't have, lent at a price.
- **The armor:** patchwork cuirass over his street clothes' silhouette — book-leather, pot-lids, a front breastplate shaped like half a mask. Protection assembled from junk that refuses to admit it's junk.
- **Windmill sails (PROPOSED, unlocked ~ch. 5–6, first full use in the Bank):** Quixote plants the lance and *the sails manifest* — four vast canvas planes rotating slow around a point he chooses, catching shadows and throwing them, a windmill built in the one place windmills live: in the way of a charging fool. Area-denial and crowd control; the castle-verb of his fighting. Keep as proposal; Phase 6/7 may promote or cut.

### 1.4 Ability toolkit (narrative-scale rules, not game stats)

| Ability | What it does | Limits / costs |
|---|---|---|
| **THE CHARGE** | Line-attack with Rocinante-lent mass; the signature. Breaks formations, doors, morale. | Straight line, telegraphed, one target group; leaves him overextended; useless vs. dispersed foes |
| **GUARD OF LA MANCHA** | The junk-armor projection; covers allies (a knight shields). Drains him fast | Static; covers one arc; he cannot move while holding it |
| **ROCINANTE'S LEND** | Short mobility burst — the nag's run granted to the boy for 3–5 seconds | Once per fight, realistically; aftermath: legs like wet rope (his body pays the toll the Persona borrows) |
| **WINDMILL SAILS** (proposed) | Area denial; throw-back; zoning | Channeled; immobile; loud (stealth-ending) |
| **Passive — WON'T STAY DOWN** | Quixote's essence: resolve-scaling. The more the world insists he's finished, the harder he is to finish | The reverse is the weakness: doubt genuinely weakens the Persona (canon Persona law). His bad days are visible in combat |

- **Elemental line (metadata-level, Futaba's scanner gags later):** resists Phys (the wrestling chassis), **weak to Curse** — the malice/hopelessness current, the element of the thing that actually kills Quixotes: not giants, but *certainty that the dream is dead.* Book One uses the weakness sparingly (one near-death lesson, ch. 6's Bank, where Kaneshiro's greed-shadow speaks pure despair at him and the Persona gutterstays). Theseus later inverts it (§5).
- **Growth:** no compendium, no fusion, no swapping (locked). Quixote grows the only way a single Persona can: bonds and resolve — mechanically invisible, narratively loud (after every real bond event, the next fight is measurably heavier).

### 1.5 Combat integration with the wrestling identity (directive §13)
The Persona does not replace the body; it *argues with* it. Choreography rule for every Metaverse fight: **the throw begins in the body and ends in the Persona.** He clinches a Shadow with mortal hands, and Quixote's mass arrives through his spine mid-throw — a German suplex with a god's weight behind it. The lance throws like a lariat; the Charge lands like a shoulder-block that was legal in no promotion ever. Earth-side (no Metaverse), none of it exists — which is the point: his Kamurocho fights stay mortal, expensive, and real (§13 directive: brutality from physicality, not physiology).

### 1.6 What Quixote CANNOT do (hard walls — never violated)
No second Persona; no fusion; no compendium; no Velvet access; no healing others beyond morale; no information about Subaru (the Persona knows what Haru knows); no power over the anchor (they are different systems); nothing that functions outside Metaverse/cognitive contexts. On the street, he is a big fifteenth-year-old with scar tissue and good footwork.

## 3. ANCHOR × METAVERSE — INTERACTION RULES (locked summary + scene mechanics)

1. The anchor predates the Metaverse by five months and is *not cognitive* — it is a thread. No scanner, no app, no Shadow perceives it. (Why Futaba's every analysis returns "no Earth-side cause," and why the log drives her quietly insane with interest.)
2. It grants **no powers** (locked, session 2). It never guides, never informs, never protects. Its only mechanical consequences in Book One: (a) the Yaldabaoth slip — chains that find purchase on everyone find none on a boy already threaded elsewhere (ch. 17, ONE showing, no narration explanation); (b) the Maruki silence — in the false world the thread points away and the pains stop (ch. 19, the body's evidence); (c) the crossing — the reversion tear catches the thread (ch. 22).
3. Persona-use costs are ordinary Metaverse fatigue and *separate* from anchor pains. The narration never conflates them; the notebook keeps separate tabs. (Haru's discipline: he is a boy who keeps clean data even when the data is unbearable.)
4. If Haru dies inside a Palace, he dies (mortal lock, session 2). No game-over rewinds exist for him — canon-shadows' killing intent is treated as real from ch. 2 onward, and the Thieves' protectiveness around him reads accordingly.

## 4. THESEUS — TRIGGER CONDITIONS & DESIGN (executes in Book II; designed now per directive §11)

**Arcana:** Sun (pack). **One Persona, one evolution, still no Room, still no wild card** (all locks hold).

**The four triggers (all four required; the evolution misfires without them):**
1. **FOUND:** Subaru located, alive, real. The search's terminal condition met — the thing every windmill was pointed at.
2. **THE CHARGE FAILS:** a situation where charging — the Quixote solution, the answer that carried him across a world — is actively wrong, and someone he loves pays for it. (Pack's slot: the Sloth arc; exact staging = Phase 8/9.)
3. **THE CHOICE TO NAVIGATE:** he sets the lance down *voluntarily* — chooses reading the maze over breaking it; chooses the plan he didn't author; chooses to trust the strategist (his brother) with the parts of the fight that aren't fists.
4. **THE ACCEPTANCE:** *found ≠ done.* The realization that "I have found my brother" is the beginning of a larger labor, not the end of a quest — protecting a person who lies to protect you, inside a labyrinth (politics, cults, Royal Selection, secrets) that punishes heroes who charge. (Directive §11's exact thesis, converted to scene-conditions.)

**Form changes (imagery bank — audit §2, Sun's Valour l. 41878, "the Club-Bearer, the Line-Bender, the Cliff-Kicker and the Wrestler… defeating his enemies by turning their own tricks and traps against them"):**
- The washbasin helm is **gone** — bare-headed now; the borrowed gold was always someone else's idea of a knight, and he no longer needs the costume to be one. (He sees clearly. That's the whole evolution.)
- The lance becomes a **club and a cord** — the labyrinth's own tools: the thread that finds paths (Mementos-shaped, later Lugunica-shaped), the club that turns traps against their makers.
- Rocinante stops being a charge-lent ghost and becomes simply *gait* — the nag is home; the mobility is his own now (narrative: the boy who was slow learned timing, the wrestler's true substitute for speed).
- The wrestling identity is *unchanged* — Theseus the Wrestler fights with the same body, the same vocabulary; what changed is that every move now reads the enemy's weight first.

**What Theseus grants (and doesn't):** a navigation-sense inside labyrinths (safe-paths, thread-sense, the smell of exits — Mementos first, Lugunica forever after); Curse weakness → Curse resist (the dream survived its disconfirmation; malice no longer gutters him); resolve-scaling retained. **Not granted:** foresight, information, any power over the anchor, any Room privileges. He is not stronger so much as *unlostable* — the difference between a knight errant and a man who knows the way out.

## 6. TEAM PERCEPTION OF QUIXOTE (what the Thieves see, per Phase 2 §10 + canon lens)

- **They watched the awakening (ch. 7):** the only canonical event of its kind they've ever seen — an acceptance-born Persona in a civilian, no Palace, no coach, mid-heist. It reframes their whole read of him before he's said a word about himself: *you can't fake what they saw in that vault.*
- **Morgana:** professional alarm as the default register ("that is NOT how any of this works" — said twice, louder the second time), escalating into grudging textbook respect; the one who first calls the fighting style "the knight thing."
- **Ren:** files the acceptance-grammar difference with his own Velvet gag order — things you don't ask out loud; their mutual-liars thread (vault ceasefire → rooftop truce, ch. 7–8) carries the subtext.
- **Futaba (post-ch. 11):** scans the Persona out of pure science-lust; reports, delighted: *"arcana: FOOL. Like, the card. Not the guy."* Haru: *"I know the difference."* (The only on-page arcana mention in the entire book — a joke carrying the locked rule.)
- **Ryuji:** thinks the Charge is the coolest thing anyone's ever done (said first in the vault, ch. 7, breathless); tries to learn the shoulder-block; dislocates nothing, barely.
- **Okumura Haru:** the name-treaty partner; privately relieved that "Haru" now maps to two entirely different people in the team's group chat, and that one of them is a knight.
- **Makoto:** the one who called the ceasefire in the vault (she read his pulse before anyone read his mask); the only one modeling his risk profile correctly (mortal body, castle-grade enemies) and writing the safety rules the team pretends he follows.
- **Yusuke:** watched the Charge clear a vault tier and said "honest" (ch. 7); the only one who watches Haru fight and calls it what it is; accidentally profound.
- **Akechi (public-only):** never sees the Persona; his file on "the seventh member" stays one page long and one page wrong.
