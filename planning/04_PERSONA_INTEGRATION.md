# PHASE 4 — PERSONA 5 ROYAL INTEGRATION: DEEP DESIGN

**Purpose (directive §22 Phase 4):** the complete mechanical and symbolic design for Haru's Persona line, the Meta-Nav, the anchor×Metaverse interaction rules, Theseus's trigger conditions, and the Akechi module gate. Everything here serves directive §9's questions — why Haru is involved, how the search leads him into the Metaverse, and what the Metaverse does to him.

**Locked inputs:** no Velvet Room, no wild card (session 3); one Persona, one evolution (pack); Fool arcana per pack — **amended reading: arcana is intrinsic metadata of the Persona itself (like Captain Kidd = Chariot), visible only to analysis tools, never assigned or discussed by any Room, because there is no Room.**

## 1. DON QUIXOTE — FULL DESIGN

### 1.1 Awakening grammar (canon P5 law, applied)
A Persona awakens when a person's suppressed will revolts against a reality that has deemed them powerless. Canon order of the moment: **rage → the mask (rage given form) → the tear → the name.** Haru's version obeys this grammar with one deviation: his rebellion is not against a captor — it is against a *verdict*. The Palace's cognition offers him the world's merciful conclusion (*your brother is dead; grieve and go home*), and the tear comes from refusing mercy.

**No Room, no contract (locked):** nobody offers him terms. He wakes up already contracted to nobody but himself. The Thieves will find this vaguely unsettling once they compare notes (Ren's gag order, the others' Membrane Voices — Haru has none; his awakening was silent because no one was watching, which Morgana will initially file under "impossible"). This is a deliberate distinguishing mark of the anchor-anomaly and stays unexplained in Book One.

### 1.2 The awakening scene — beat map (ch. 2; 12 beats; prose-ready architecture, not prose)

1. **The HIT rendered mundane first:** the Nav spits a route; Haru walks it in the rain past a shuttered pachinkro parlor, and the street does something a street shouldn't (the storefront he passes is on his phone's map twice, in different places). He logs it. Voice stays inventory-dry.
2. **The threshold:** a maintenance door his keycard theory says shouldn't open, opens. The Metaverse does not announce itself — the air goes thick, the rain stops hitting him, and the far side of the doorway is a courtyard of iron and chain under a sky the color of an old coin.
3. **The castle is his target's heart, read literally:** a fortress of deposit boxes and ledger walls — everything the man ever took, catalogued and hoarded. Cognition made architecture (DTES/PDR "Metaverse 101" on-ramp discipline: the narrator treats the rules like weather, not wonder).
4. **His reception:** the castle's cognition has already met him — it *knows* the stories its owner tells about "the snooping kid." Haru is expected. Shadow-guards wear collection-agent faces. The fight is real and he is losing on schedule (mortal boy, first visit, no mask yet).
5. **The throne room's gift:** the target's Shadow receives him almost kindly — a coward's courtesy — and shows him the castle's deepest vault: a single deposit box labeled with Haru's own name, inside it a *certified conclusion*: **Natsuki Subaru — deceased in absentia — case closed.** The cognition speaks it in his mother's cadence, his father's tiredness, his teachers' kindness, everyone's mercy at once: *grieve and go home, boy.*
6. **The offer is the trap:** the castle will even let him keep the paper — make it official — if he stops looking. The world's kindest lie, the one everyone has been too polite to say aloud since December.
7. **Rage, given order:** Haru has been furious for five months and has never once had anywhere to put it. The verdict hands it a shape. His notebook comes out — he reads his own pain log *at* the Shadow, entry by entry, dates and intensities — the ledger as declaration of war: *feelings don't lie. You do.*
8. **The tear:** the mask isn't given to him; it's torn off him — the castle's certainty that he's "just a grieving child" ripping away like wet paper, and what's under it is ridiculous and true: a washbasin helm, a knight's absurd donned garbage, golden to the cognition that mocked it (Mambrino's basin — the barber's brass he mistook for gold, and *was* gold the moment he put it on).
9. **The name:** *DON QUIXOTE* — not announced by any voice but his own refusal given syllables. The lance arrives like a decision already made. Somewhere in the new weight of him, a lean nag stamps once and is gone (Rocinante — present as charge, absent as pet).
10. **The first act of the new self:** he charges the throne — the taxonomy's first adult application: low, heavy, straight through a Shadow-guard like a sumo close (wrestling vocabulary + Persona mass). The castle learns what the reader already knows: the kid doesn't stay down.
11. **The exit discipline (PDR on-ramp):** he does *not* finish the castle. No calling card, no treasure. He breaks the vault box (the paper doesn't burn — cognition props are unburnable — he throws it down a stairwell instead), takes the Shadow's chilled, honest scream ("THE LEDGER — HE KEEPS A LEDGER—") as confirmation the target *knows something is coming*, and leaves before the castle seals. First visit = reconnaissance. He learned this much the sensible way: from watching what happened to boys who didn't plan.
12. **The landing:** the rain hits him again. Phone: 21:12. The castle stays (no distortion resolved). He logs the entry under a new tab — not pains, not training: *castles.* Last line of the night in the notebook: *"It was real. It's all real. He could be anywhere."* — the delusion's bankruptcy notice, filed as hope. (Raw-line budget: spent here, ch. 2.)

**Voice donor:** Kaleidoscope's flat declaratives under pressure. **Mechanics donors:** Ace ch. 5 "Awakening" (outsider-awakening staging), canon Ren/Ann/Ryuji grammar (rage→tear→name), PDR ch. 4 (rules-as-weather narration).

### 1.3 Form & imagery kit (the reader's picture of Quixote)

- **The helm:** the Mambrino washbasin — brass, dented, absurd, *gold in the Metaverse's eye.* Never explained in dialogue (directive §10). Team nickname fuel ("the knight thing"), and the fixed visual joke that isn't one: it never comes off in combat, and it *works.*
- **The lance:** a大道 hand-and-a-half lance, too long, tournament-ornate — a toy knight's ideal made functional. In his hands it moves like a wrestling weapon: spear-thrusts thrown with a lariat's shoulder, sweeps with a jab's timing.
- **Rocinante:** a lean, sway-backed nag that exists only inside the Charge — manifests at the run, gone at the stop. Never rides, never stays. The mobility he doesn't have, lent at a price.
- **The armor:** patchwork cuirass over his street clothes' silhouette — book-leather, pot-lids, a front breastplate shaped like half a mask. Protection assembled from junk that refuses to admit it's junk.
- **Windmill sails (PROPOSED, unlocked ~ch. 5–6, first full use in the Bank):** Quixote plants the lance and *the sails manifest* — four vast canvas planes rotating slow around a point he chooses, catching shadows and throwing them, a windmill built in the one place windmills live: in the way of a charging fool. Area-denial and crowd control; the castle-verb of his fighting. Keep as proposal; Phase 6/7 may promote or cut.

### 1.4 Ability toolkit (narrative-scale rules, not game stats)

| Ability | What it does | Limits / costs |
|---|---|---|
| **THE CHARGE** | Line-attack with Rocinante-lent mass; the signature. Breaks formations, doors, morale. | Straight line, telegraphed, one target group; leaves him overextended; useless vs. dispersed foes |
| **GUARD OF LA MANCHA** | The junk-armor projection; covers allies (a knight shields). Drains him fast | Static; covers one arc; he cannot move while holding it |
| **ROCINANTE'S LEND** | Short mobility burst — the nag's run granted to the boy for 3–5 seconds | Once per fight, realistically; aftermath: legs like wet rope (his body pays the toll the Persona borrows) |
| **WINDMILL SAILS** (proposed) | Area denial; throw-back; zoning | Channeled; immobile; loud (stealth-ending) |
| **Passive — WON'T STAY DOWN** | Quixote's essence: resolve-scaling. The more the world insists he's finished, the harder he is to finish | The reverse is the weakness: doubt genuinely weakens the Persona (canon Persona law). His bad days are visible in combat |

- **Elemental line (metadata-level, Futaba's scanner gags later):** resists Phys (the wrestling chassis), **weak to Curse** — the malice/hopelessness current, the element of the thing that actually kills Quixotes: not giants, but *certainty that the dream is dead.* Book One uses the weakness sparingly (one near-death lesson, ch. 6's Bank, where Kaneshiro's greed-shadow speaks pure despair at him and the Persona gutterstays). Theseus later inverts it (§5).
- **Growth:** no compendium, no fusion, no swapping (locked). Quixote grows the only way a single Persona can: bonds and resolve — mechanically invisible, narratively loud (after every real bond event, the next fight is measurably heavier).

### 1.5 Combat integration with the wrestling identity (directive §13)
The Persona does not replace the body; it *argues with* it. Choreography rule for every Metaverse fight: **the throw begins in the body and ends in the Persona.** He clinches a Shadow with mortal hands, and Quixote's mass arrives through his spine mid-throw — a German suplex with a god's weight behind it. The lance throws like a lariat; the Charge lands like a shoulder-block that was legal in no promotion ever. Earth-side (no Metaverse), none of it exists — which is the point: his Kamurocho fights stay mortal, expensive, and real (§13 directive: brutality from physicality, not physiology).

### 1.6 What Quixote CANNOT do (hard walls — never violated)
No second Persona; no fusion; no compendium; no Velvet access; no healing others beyond morale; no information about Subaru (the Persona knows what Haru knows); no power over the anchor (they are different systems); nothing that functions outside Metaverse/cognitive contexts. On the street, he is a big fifteenth-year-old with scar tissue and good footwork.

## 3. ANCHOR × METAVERSE — INTERACTION RULES (locked summary + scene mechanics)

1. The anchor predates the Metaverse by five months and is *not cognitive* — it is a thread. No scanner, no app, no Shadow perceives it. (Why Futaba's every analysis returns "no Earth-side cause," and why the log drives her quietly insane with interest.)
2. It grants **no powers** (locked, session 2). It never guides, never informs, never protects. Its only mechanical consequences in Book One: (a) the Yaldabaoth slip — chains that find purchase on everyone find none on a boy already threaded elsewhere (ch. 17, ONE showing, no narration explanation); (b) the Maruki silence — in the false world the thread points away and the pains stop (ch. 19, the body's evidence); (c) the crossing — the reversion tear catches the thread (ch. 22).
3. Persona-use costs are ordinary Metaverse fatigue and *separate* from anchor pains. The narration never conflates them; the notebook keeps separate tabs. (Haru's discipline: he is a boy who keeps clean data even when the data is unbearable.)
4. If Haru dies inside a Palace, he dies (mortal lock, session 2). No game-over rewinds exist for him — canon-shadows' killing intent is treated as real from ch. 2 onward, and the Thieves' protectiveness around him reads accordingly.

## 4. THESEUS — TRIGGER CONDITIONS & DESIGN (executes in Book II; designed now per directive §11)

**Arcana:** Sun (pack). **One Persona, one evolution, still no Room, still no wild card** (all locks hold).

**The four triggers (all four required; the evolution misfires without them):**
1. **FOUND:** Subaru located, alive, real. The search's terminal condition met — the thing every windmill was pointed at.
2. **THE CHARGE FAILS:** a situation where charging — the Quixote solution, the answer that carried him across a world — is actively wrong, and someone he loves pays for it. (Pack's slot: the Sloth arc; exact staging = Phase 8/9.)
3. **THE CHOICE TO NAVIGATE:** he sets the lance down *voluntarily* — chooses reading the maze over breaking it; chooses the plan he didn't author; chooses to trust the strategist (his brother) with the parts of the fight that aren't fists.
4. **THE ACCEPTANCE:** *found ≠ done.* The realization that "I have found my brother" is the beginning of a larger labor, not the end of a quest — protecting a person who lies to protect you, inside a labyrinth (politics, cults, Royal Selection, secrets) that punishes heroes who charge. (Directive §11's exact thesis, converted to scene-conditions.)

**Form changes (imagery bank — audit §2, Sun's Valour l. 41878, "the Club-Bearer, the Line-Bender, the Cliff-Kicker and the Wrestler… defeating his enemies by turning their own tricks and traps against them"):**
- The washbasin helm is **gone** — bare-headed now; the borrowed gold was always someone else's idea of a knight, and he no longer needs the costume to be one. (He sees clearly. That's the whole evolution.)
- The lance becomes a **club and a cord** — the labyrinth's own tools: the thread that finds paths (Mementos-shaped, later Lugunica-shaped), the club that turns traps against their makers.
- Rocinante stops being a charge-lent ghost and becomes simply *gait* — the nag is home; the mobility is his own now (narrative: the boy who was slow learned timing, the wrestler's true substitute for speed).
- The wrestling identity is *unchanged* — Theseus the Wrestler fights with the same body, the same vocabulary; what changed is that every move now reads the enemy's weight first.

**What Theseus grants (and doesn't):** a navigation-sense inside labyrinths (safe-paths, thread-sense, the smell of exits — Mementos first, Lugunica forever after); Curse weakness → Curse resist (the dream survived its disconfirmation; malice no longer gutters him); resolve-scaling retained. **Not granted:** foresight, information, any power over the anchor, any Room privileges. He is not stronger so much as *unlostable* — the difference between a knight errant and a man who knows the way out.
