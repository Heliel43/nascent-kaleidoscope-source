## 1. HARU NATSUKI — PROFILE

### 1.1 Identity
- **Natsuki Haru**, b. Dec 21, 2000. 15 years old at Chapter 1 (May 2016). Shujin Academy first-year. Subaru's younger brother by two years (Subaru b. ~1998, 17 when taken).
- The name joke that isn't a joke: the family name means *summer*, his given name means *spring* — a boy named warm seasons who took **"Winter"** as his street name. Alias origin (proposal for ch. 1's flashback to confirm): self-chosen, as a memorial — the season his brother was taken, the season missing from his name. He tells people it sounds cooler. It isn't about sounding cooler.
- **Day identity:** scholarship-and-legacy Shujin first-year. School is just school (pack rule #2). He is polite, unremarkable, and deliberately boring — a cover he maintains without ever calling it a cover.
- **Night identity:** "Winter," a Kamurocho street fighter with a notebook full of impossible data and a case file no sane person would build.

### 1.2 The core psychological insight (governs the whole Earth arc)
**The windmill is the explanation, not the belief.**

Everyone around Haru thinks he is a grieving boy who refuses to accept his brother's death. He is almost the opposite. The anchor (§2) tells him — wrongly in one respect, truly in the one that matters — that Subaru is alive and suffering. No one believes the one thing he is *right* about. So he built the thing he could act on: the yakuza theory, a complete, self-consistent, *false* explanation assembled from three mundane coincidences (pack rule #3). Don Quixote charged windmills because he mistook them for giants; Haru charges the Tojo Clan because he mistook a thread for a rope. **The mill is real. It just isn't a giant.**

This keeps directive §12 ("character before spectacle") intact and gives the delusion a precise failure mode: every piece of evidence Haru gathers is *real* — the pains, the camera glitch, the terrified man — and every interpretation he stacks on top is his own. The Metaverse's first lesson to him (his first Palace, §7) will be that it happily confirms what he already believes. Cognition does not adjudicate truth. That lesson has to bounce off him the first time, stick the second, and only fully land at Maruki's door.

### 1.3 Voice (from the author's own Kaleidoscope register + pack §5)
First-person, dry, blunt; competence performed quietly; emotion rationed — **one raw line per chapter, maximum.** Calibration text: *A_Nascent_Kaleidoscope.txt* ch. 1 — the flat declaratives, the *"that sounds like bullshit"* refusal to be handled, the anger that walks out of rooms instead of shouting in them. Haru's narration treats absurdity with inventory rather than wonder: he logs, he lists, he prices things. Grief appears only in small bursts (pack's "Maybe someday" register) — usually displaced onto objects: the notebook, a spare chopstick set, Subaru's game save he refuses to overwrite.

Comedy is structural (25–35% of scenes): the karaoke obligation, Boss the cat's contempt, the Okumura Haru collision, wrestling vernacular applied to non-combat situations ("that was a clean shoot, sensei" about a pop quiz — sample only).

### 1.4 Body and mortal stakes
- Big-built for fifteen: the power-forward frame. Slow for his size — and he *knows* it, because his older brother's old coaching taxonomy (§3) told him what he was at age eleven.
- **Mortal. No reset (locked).** Wounds heal at normal speed. The ch. 1 knife fight must leave a real, weeks-long problem. Jan 2016's beating leaves a worse one. This is directive §13 applied to Haru himself: no protections, real consequences, violence that is fun-brutal but never free.
- Pain is information he manages like anyone: he tapes his own ribs badly, gets better at it, and refuses hospitals with increasingly inventive stories (the ER visit that *wasn't* avoidable — Dec 2015's first anchor spike, misdiagnosed as an atypical seizure — is already in his medical file as a lie he has to keep maintaining).

### 1.5 Growth axis (Don Quixote phase)
Haru's Earth-arc flaw and strength are the same mechanism: **refusal.** Refusing Subaru's death made him a fighter; refusing disconfirmation makes him dangerous to himself; refusing despair (vs. refusing truth — the distinction Futaba will need, §9) is the strength he'll hand her. The Earth arc ends with refusal *intact* but *educated*: he stops refusing evidence and starts refusing despair — which is why he can survive Maruki's perfect world (locked, Option B) and why Theseus is possible later (pack §7: "the monster in the maze is the overprotective brother").

Corpus anchors for the register: Ichiban's belief-engine (*Burn_Like_A_Dragon* l. 11883) minus the delusion's object; Nanako's refusal-engine (*Steal_the_Truth* premise) at adolescent wattage; Ken's veteran-among-children position (*Ace_In_The_Hole*) inverted into seeker-among-children.

## 2. THE DEATH ANCHOR — LOCKED RULES

**Status:** KEEP, re-sourced to Persona metaphysics (session-2 ruling).

- **What it is:** the cognitive shadow of the brothers' bond — a "thread that was never cut," visible to the Velvet Room, invisible to everyone else. It does not belong to this room's guest; that is exactly why the god of control's scheme slips on it, and why the crossing (locked, ~Feb 2, 2017) has a hook to catch when reality tears.
- **What it does:** when Subaru dies inside a loop, Haru feels it. Phantom agony and cold, seconds to a minute. Logged with date, time, intensity, duration in a beat-up notebook.
- **What it never does (pack rule #7, unchanged):** no memories, no images, no words, no location, no cause. He cannot tell drowning from a blade. It gives him **zero information** — directive §16 compliant. It justifies belief; it never guides action. Every "prediction" it seems to make is post-hoc pattern-hunger (ch. 1's seed: the pain that arrived *before* the knife — coincidence, logged, mis-filed).
- **Frequency design (Phase 3 pins exact dates):** Dec 25, 2015 — first spike, the bath scene, ER misdiagnosis. Winter 2016: rare, overwhelming. Feb–Apr: settles into a loggable pattern; the notebook begins. By May: 40+ entries, **gaps shrinking** — the reader may eventually infer what Haru cannot: the intervals track the density of another boy's loops.
- **Who learns about it, in order:** Haru → (his doctor, lied to) → Naoko/Kenichi (know "episodes" exist, not their nature) → Futaba (August onward, §9 — the only person who treats the log as *data*, and her analysis quietly proves it fits nothing on Earth) → the Thieves, partially, post-Kaneshiro (Makoto's suspicion, §8) → no one else. Subaru never knows, on either side of the crossing, until THE REVEAL.
- **Third-semester consequence (locked, Option B):** inside Maruki's reality the anchor goes wrong in a new way — *quiet, directional.* The false Subaru at the dinner table produces no resonance at all, while the thread keeps pointing somewhere no one in that world is. Haru's body knows before his mind accepts it. The temptation fails on evidence the delusion itself spent a year training him to trust. This is the mechanism that makes the Option B finale character-driven rather than plot-forced.

## 3. FIGHTING IDENTITY — LADDER AND TAXONOMY

**Directive §13 locked:** WWE/NJPW movement vocabulary as genuine combat. Corpus donors: PDR ch. 1 (the stylistic taxonomy), PDR l. 15486 (wrestling violence as legend — the bicycle powerbomb story), BLAD tournament arc (venue), Sun's Valour l. 41878 (Theseus "the Wrestler" — the aspirational frame).

**Origin (replaces the removed Ajin hit-and-run beat):** futon wrestling with Subaru — the little brother always losing, the older brother always doing play-by-play commentary. It is the one physical language the brothers share, and after Dec 23 it is the only part of Subaru Haru can still touch without grief tax. He starts training *because it's theirs*, and keeps training because it works.

**The taxonomy he was raised on (adapted verbatim in spirit from PDR ch. 1):** Subaru, age thirteen, sitting cross-legged on a futon, explaining to a nine-year-old that power wrestlers don't learn leg sweeps — *"Pro bowlers don't become pro bowlers by playing golf. Play to your strengths."* Haru's whole style is that lecture, executed: power, durability, clinch, throws, momentum — with the known weakness (speed) that smarter opponents will eventually exploit on-page (directive: Haru is formidable, not untouchable).

**Ladder (Phase 3 places these on the calendar):**
1. **Futon league (childhood–Dec 2015):** play, commentary, the taxonomy lecture. Ch. 1 flashback material.
2. **Jan 2016 — the debt-collection beating:** first Kamurocho attempt; he follows the camera-glitch thread and is found by professionals. Real injuries. A named save (§6, Kiryu-ruling) is available here or shortly after — Phase 3 decides whether the save is Jan (immediate) or later (earned).
3. **Winter–spring 2016 — training:** public gyms, tape study, park work. NJPW-flavored self-education; his training log lives in the same notebook as the pain log — two impossible datasets, one boy.
4. **March–April — "Winter" coheres:** alley reputation grows (§5). First tournament-adjacent money fights if Phase 3 uses the underground venue (BLAD donor).
5. **May 7, 2016 — ch. 1 alley fight:** the knife. Real consequences. His style under real pressure for the first time on-page.
6. **June–July — Kaneshiro collision:** first Metaverse combat; mortal skills + Persona (§10). The team discovers that the quiet first-year fights like a main event.
7. **Ongoing:** Metaverse augments the style (charge, clinch, ring-ropes-mentalty on cognitive terrain) but never replaces it. No power escalation beyond the single evolving Persona (locked).

## 5. THE NATSUKI FAMILY

- **Natsuki Kenichi** (father): works too much by choice since Dec 23. Holds the line the story's quietest character holds: **he refused a grave** — *"Natsuki men and graves"* (pack proverb seed, DEFAULT-KEEP, now with its on-page meaning resolved): the proverb is Kenichi's answer to everyone who tells him to hold a funeral. He is not in denial; he is the adult version of Haru's refusal. **Father and son have never once said this to each other.** The May 8 family dinner (ch. 1) runs on this silence — comedy at the surface (Kenichi's terrible restaurant picks), the wound underneath.
- **Natsuki Naoko** (mother): grieving in the socially legible way — rituals, records, calls. Fears Haru's "episodes" (the anchor spikes she's seen) and his late hours; does not know about Winter. Her love language is logistics, and she cannot find the form to sign that fixes this.
- **Household geography (proposes resolution of the pack's Big Echo apartment vs. family dinners):** the family apartment sits between Shujin's district and Kamurocho, on the train line, above/near a Big Echo. After the vanishing, Naoko took a transfer for work; Kenichi lives at his office; Haru refused to move out of the apartment — because Subaru's room is in it — and the parents, split by guilt, let him keep it while each pretends the arrangement is temporary. Sunday dinner is the ritual that keeps them a family (rotating restaurant; Kenichi always picks wrong). Result: a fifteen-year-old with a front-door key to a grief shrine, a night identity, and parents who love him in the wrong directions. *Ch. 1 v4's established geography takes precedence — author confirms during the v4.1 pass.*
- **Family dinner register:** comedy over silence. Directive §12: the story stays grounded here. The Natsuki household is the Earth arc's ballast.

## 6. "WINTER" — ALIAS, REPUTATION, ECOSYSTEM

**Reputation ladder (Phase 3 calendars it):**
1. **Feb 2016 — "that kid":** shows up where he shouldn't, asks wrong questions, refuses to leave. Beaten, returns. Pity from professionals; annoyance from everyone.
2. **Mar 2016 — "the kid who doesn't stay down":** the label that will get him hurt worse than any insult. First small wins against punks his own age.
3. **Apr 2016 — "Winter":** the name sticks (origin per §1.1). Now he is a Kamurocho character — which is a resource (people talk to characters) and a trap (characters can't quit without an exit story).

**Ecosystem (pack running gags, roles defined):**
- **Mama Shizu** — proprietor associated with the Big Echo below his apartment. Believes a version of Haru's cover story; the monthly karaoke obligation is her price for the "family rate" and her way of checking he's eaten. Comedy engine + the arc's best example of unpaid, unglamorous adult care.

- **The notebook** — two sections: pains, and training. The single most important prop in the Earth arc. Its hand-off to Futaba (§9) is the emotional midpoint of the year.

## 7. THE DELUSION — SEEDS, PAYOUT, AND THEME

**The three seeds (pack rule #3; all shown on-page with mundane readings visible to the reader):**
1. The maintenance-window camera glitch (the last footage of Subaru, gone — a coincidence with a boring explanation Haru has been given and refuses to re-verify).
2. The retired old man who watched the Natsuki apartment (neighborhood watch volunteer; Haru read him as surveillance).
3. The scared man's invented "Tojo pin" (a debtor who told a desperate kid the scariest word he knew).

**The payout (ch. 2 brief, pack §6):** the security-company lead pays out a NAME — the man with the pin — and Haru feeds name + Millennium Tower + a distortion word into the Meta-Nav. Hit. His first Palace.

**The theme this buys (directive §10–11 demonstrated, never explained):** the Metaverse will confirm his delusion by *building a castle for it* — cognition manifests obsession, not truth. Haru's first Palace belongs to a coward (§10) and its lesson is: the answer to "where is Subaru?" is *not here, kid.* The second lesson (Kaneshiro) is that real predators also live in castles. The final exam (Maruki, locked) is a castle made of the one thing he wants. Three castles, one education. Don Quixote's windmills, rebuilt as cognitive architecture — and theseus's labyrinth waiting on the other side of the sea.

## 9. FUTABA — THE ROMANCE THREAD (locked beats from pack §2–3)

- Canon shut-in until August (locked). No early appearances. Meets the Thieves and Haru at her canon window.
- **Why it works (directive §12):** Haru's gift to Futaba is the distinction he lives daily — refusing the truth (her trap) vs. refusing despair (his discipline). He is the only person who can say "I spent a year refusing a death, so I know exactly what it costs, and here's what it's for" without it being a lesson. Her gift to him is the notebook scene: she is the only person who treats the pain log as **data** — and her analysis (pattern intervals, no Earth-side cause) quietly hands him the shape of the truth he can't yet read. He doesn't understand the finding. The reader starts to.
- **Register:** two kids fluent in grief-jokes; she mods his phone; he duets at her door; blush-level, PG (pack). Confession before endgame (locked).
- **Third-semester consequence (locked, Option B):** in Maruki's world, Haru has Subaru — and Futaba has Wakaba — and the whole Thieves' circle has every dead or broken thing made whole. Her resistance to the perfect reality is the mirror of his: she, of all people, knows a restored mother can be true *and* false at once. **And after the crossing, her line — "he's not dead, he's LOST — we find him" — fires at Haru's empty spot on the floor, repeating his own arc with the polarity reversed.** The pack's best line, now load-bearing twice.

## 10. THE THIEVES — INTEGRATION MAP

**Induction (locked: Kaneshiro, Jun–Jul):** Winter tails a shakedown crew from his list; the crew launders through Kaneshiro's network; he walks into the Thieves' operation from the wrong side. Bit runs: "Haru Two" (vetoed), "Haru-kun vs. Haru-san" (vetoed by both Harus in unison, the first thing they ever agree on), until Morgana lands on **KNIGHT** — because he keeps throwing himself at castles. - **Ann:** the skeptical big sister; the one who says out loud what everyone thinks ("you're going to get yourself killed over a maybe") and then covers his blind side anyway.
- **Morgana:** jurisdiction war (Winter operates alone; Mona enforces teamwork) → grudging respect → the two best nappers in the group.

- **Yusuke:** the only one who watches Haru fight and calls it honest; art-of-violence observations; accidentally profound.
