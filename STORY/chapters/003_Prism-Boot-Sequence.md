# Chapter 3 — Prism, Boot Sequence

*from **A Nascent Light** — Vol. 1: First Light*

---

The counter took a month to fill, and it did it the way paint dries: not once, but in a hundred places you weren't looking.

[Booting… 78%] while I skinned a rabbit. [91%] during Bailiff Kel's spring audit, when he ran his finger along our tally-boards and found nothing wrong with them, which he clearly resented. [97%] the night Father taught Astrid and me to read the weather off the water — "smell's the same as a barometer, boy, cheaper and less likely to lie" — and I understood, mid-sentence, that I was inheriting a skill from a man who'd never known how good his own hands were, and that this fact would someday be load-bearing for something.

[Booting… 100%] came at dawn on the Greywood trail, standing in my own snare line with the mist up to my elbows and a hare kicking in a knot that I had tied too loose. That detail — *too loose* — was the last thing I processed before the world put on its new layer.

*Pop. Pop. Pop.*

[Congratulations on completing calibration of the Nascent Kaleidoscope (build 0.3.1-shard).]
[Welcome, host. The shard is now bonded. The room you left is not coming back. This is not a tragedy; it is a schedule.]

"Huh," I said, to a forest, which is how most of my conversations have gone lately. My hands were shaking, and I was annoyed about it, because I'd had a month, and a year, and a whole *previous life* of warning shots. "Alright. Let's see the menu then."

I thought *Status*, and the air obliged.

Name: Kale Vester
Title: **Nascent**
Race: Human
Alignment: True Neutral
Level: 1
HP: 120/120
SP: 100/100
LUM: 40/40
VIT: 20 | STR: 12 | DEX: 15 | END: 16
INT: 14 | WIS: 13 | MAG: 10 | LUK: 6
Motes: 0
Money: 0

I stared at it for a long moment. It was real. An actual status screen, in an actual field, attached to an actual eleven-year-old, and I had to lean on my billhook to keep the grin off the record, because a grin would have to be *explained* and I had gone to a lot of trouble curating this face.

The numbers read like a truth serum. VIT twenty, because five years of farm-chores had *earned* their entry in the ledger; STR twelve, DEX fifteen, because a body tuned by intention moves better than it hits; and LUK six — six — the room had not been wrong when it said my luck wasn't a miracle. The LUM line ticked upward a fraction every few seconds: *0.3/s*. Regeneration. Everything on the page was a receipt. I found I respected the system for that. Receipts you could audit were receipts you could plan against.

[Mirror Lv.1 — active appraisals are free. Passive ambient scan is enabled. You will be charged for nothing you have not agreed to see.]

"Fair," I said, and then, because the sentence had arrived fully formed in the voice of every reader I'd ever envied: "I *knew* I was special."

Quest Alert!

Quest: All-Seeing Eye
Look around, and get accustomed to your new surroundings.
Objective: Level Mirror to Lv.2
Rewards: 200 EXP, Mote Sense
Note: this is the tutorial. There is no skipping it. I checked.

"I thought you said I'd never be charged for things I didn't agree to—"

You agreed to be *born*. This is the same paperwork with different ink.

"That sounds like bullshit."

That is an accurate translation of the original.

The hare, sensibly, resumed kicking.

So: Mirror. The skill did what it said on the window, with a caveat that the window had buried in a note so small I nearly missed it — *Mirror reflects only what the host can comprehend* — and I learned the caveat the hard way, on the hare.

**[Hare — *Lepus* of some stripe; don't be smug, the taxonomy here isn't in Latin]**
Status: stressed (acute), left hind limb compromised (snare constriction), dehydration (moderate).
Assessment: it will die in this knot within the hour if untreated. It will die in this knot within the hour even *with* treatment, because the limb is gone past any setting.
Intent: none. Fleeing. That's all. That's the whole thought.

That was what the skill would *not* do, and I understood, sitting back on my heels in the wet leaves, that this was the actual lesson the tutorial had for me: there was no read-depth here because there was no *me* here — no hidden statline of a hare's ambitions, no boss health bar, no cutscene. It gave me what a decent hunter already knew about a trapped animal and gave me no more, because a thing is only as legible as I am able to read it. The axe, by contrast, told me a story — **[Ash-hand axe, 2.1 lb, edge angle uneven (left bias 4°), haft sound; owner's maintenance habits: frequent, correct, hurried]** — because axes were a language I'd spent five years learning from the inside. My mother, when I tried her that evening over the supper-table noise, returned nothing but **[Signe Vester — adult human female; midwife; fatigue (chronic, moderate); affection (toward you: unexamined, enormous)]** and I had to excuse myself to the woodshed and sit a while among the tools.

The hare could not wait for me to finish having feelings about the system. That was the mercy of this life: the work tells you when your feelings are finished.

I carried the knot home on a switch like a fish. Mother was mid-soup and saw the whole thing in one look, because she was my mother, and because there was nothing to see — a boy, a snare failure, a dead-ish animal. "You'll use the legs," she said. It wasn't a question, and it wasn't a rebuke. I got the killing blow right, on the block, the way Father had taught me two winters ago with the hen that had gone mad with lice — one clean strike at the base of the skull, no hesitation you can see in the wrist. The hare stopped being an event and became meat.

And then the window lit like someone had flipped a breaker behind my eyes.

[A Mote has been harvested.]
Motes: 1
Note: a Mote is the light of a will at its ending. The Kaleidoscope gathers what is already released. It does not ask *how*. It remembers that you did.

I stood there with the axe in my hand and the soup going on without me and the number glowing in the corner of my vision, and my first thought — my honest, uncurated, adult-in-a-child's-body first thought — was: *oh, no, what have I signed on to.* My second was worse: a quick little arithmetic of snare-placement along the south line that would improve yield. My third thought was about those second and first thoughts and how close together they'd sat, and that one I did write down in my head, in the ledger, on the page labeled *things the Prism does to me that I must watch.*

[Skill unlocked by practice: **Dressing Game (Common) Lv.1** — you are proficient at bleeding, skinning, and butchering small game. Gains apply in butchery, field-dressing, and negotiation with anyone who can smell that you know which end is which.]

"Well, that's *litRPG* of it," I said, and even the rabbit couldn't laugh at that, but Mother, from the doorway, asked me to stop narrating to the shed again, and to come salt the hare, and — with the flatness she reserved for matters of medicine and finance, which in our household were the same matter — "if that thing's been dying since dawn you'll put it out before you gut it, and you'll do it *before* you start talking like your grandmother when she's pleased with herself."

"Yes ma'am."

I put it out. I did not start talking like my grandmother when I was pleased with myself, which was a shame, because there was a lot happening in there that wanted celebrating, including the small cold fact that I was a person with a skill *tree* now, and skills in trees grow. That's what a tree's for.

The Mote spent itself that night, because the system made it simple — made it *tempting*, and I wrote that down too.

[You may invest Motes to advance a Refraction.]
**Refraction (Cold) Lv.1 → Lv.2.** Current: a chill you can hold, a breath on a mirror in July.
Invested: a chill that *takes*, on your terms, at 3 LUM per cast, twice before the pool flags you.

**[Confirm]**

I confirmed. I'd spent the hour testing what Lv.1 could do on a tin cup of creek-water — *chill*, nothing more; it felt like holding snow I'd made myself — and I wanted, with a want that surprised me by how ordinary it was, to see a *frost* I could hand to my father on a morning when the birch-sap run came late and a killing frost could save a stand. The air above my palm took shape: a knot of blue-white the size of a marble, and I understood it — genuinely understood it, the way I understood an axe edge — as *a cold gathered along a seam in the light*, because that is what the Kaleidoscope was, and my skill made sense as a skill only when I let it be a *lens* and not a *wand*.

The marble left my hand and the cup cracked clean down the middle, ice blooming inside it like a flower on a clock.

[**Frost Lance Lv.1** acquired. Refraction (Cold) advanced by understanding; Motes spent: 1.]

I read that line four times.

*By understanding.* The Mote hadn't bought the thing; it had *advanced the seam* — I'd needed to know what cold was before the skill could give me a lance. The currency wasn't the gate. My mind was the gate; the Mote just filed the paperwork. In the old life they had a phrase for that arrangement, and it had nothing to do with magic: *accessibility*. The world had simply not been built for the person who already knew.

Quest Complete!

Quest: All-Seeing Eye
Objective (Complete): Level Mirror to Lv.2
Rewards: 200 EXP, Mote Sense
Level Up!

Level 2 reached. Unspent points: 3.

Mote Sense: you may feel, within a hundred paces, the moment a living thing is about to stop. The Kaleidoscope has found this useful. You are not obliged to find it useful. You will.

And that last part — *You will* — sat with me like a stone in a shoe the whole walk back to the house, up past the fence Astrid and I had made sagging and straight again, into the kitchen light, where my family was eating bread at nine o'clock at night and pretending not to have waited on me.

"Mood improved?" Father asked, not looking up.

"Mm," I said, handing him the cracked cup like a man presenting a small miracle with an invoice attached. "Had a bit of trouble with the ice."

He looked at the split bowl, then at me, then set down his bread with the slow care of a man recalculating everything he'd assumed about a morning. The fire ticked. In the window at the corner of my eye, my level sat solid and unboastful at **2**, the number with no name yet, and Motes: 0 glowed beneath it like an account at zero balance and a bank down the road that had my number.

[New quest generated. Priority: core-refractive.]
[The howe opened something. Your village will go look at it in the spring.]
[You should be ready before then. Quest: **Winter Readiness**. Objective: reach Lv.4 before first snow. Rewards: ?]

I took a bite of bread to hide my face. First snow, in this valley, was a week the farmers had a saying for and a week the bailiff had a rate for.

"Kale?" Mother said. "You're *grinning*, and you have bread on your chin."

"Good soup," I lied, and the frost in my palm hummed, one half-charged lance deep, like a second heartbeat learning my name.
