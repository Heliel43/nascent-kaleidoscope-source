# Chapter 8 — The Kaleidoscope Turns

*from **A Nascent Light** — Vol. 1: First Light*

---

The crew put their price on me the same week they put a price on rope, and the price, as it turned out, was a shield.

Brant — the crew's shieldman, a man built like a door that has been *right about* every hinge — found me at the meeting-yard stumps the morning after the satchel, with two practice-shields and one of those expressions grown men get when they've decided in advance to be disappointed.

"Benn says you're the one who read the howe's arithmetic," Brant said. "Cato says you stood in front of a *keeper* and threw light like a hand grenade. Runa says your knots hold." He dropped one of the shields at my feet. "I say none of that means you can take a hit. You'll go with us to take orders and carry line. In a ruin, the thing that keeps a runner alive isn't his legs, it's the fact that he's *hard to break* and *boring to chase*. So: put that up. Don't swing it. I'll show you what a hit costs, and you'll tell me whether you can pay it, and if you cry I'll carry you to your mother myself and she'll fix me for it."

I put it up, because by then I had learned that this is how the world interviews you: a big man in a muddy yard, holding a second-best shield with his left hand, and the whole question being whether your feet are where your mouth is.

What followed was the most *useful* beating of either of my lives, and I want to be exact about it, because exactness is what the beating was for. Brant hit me with the boss of his shield and I went down like a sack of wet flour, and he let me lie there and *watch* the sun while I got my hearing back, and then did it again after I'd moved three inches — and by the fourth time, the fifth, I had learned the two facts my system had never once thought to put in a box: the first is that a body with a level on it still has a head on top of it, and the way you eat the ground *matters*, because the ground is the one opponent that never gets tired; the second is that Prism Calm is *magnificent*, in exactly the way a locked room is magnificent, because when Brant's shield came around for the sixth time my fear went down to its proper volume and what stayed up was the *footwork*, and I took the hit on the shield's angle, went *with* it, came up on the off-side, and put my shoulder — carefully, carefully, the way you put a tool down — into the seam of his guard, and he laughed, once, like a man finding a coin in the winter coat.

"You don't die easy," Brant said, hauling me up by the front of my shirt. "And you don't cheat when you win a foot of ground. Ove's a *smith's* Ove, don't you go telling village stories about it — but in a ruin, boy, the barrow needs your back more than your sword needs to be good. I'll tell Benn you cost your wage."

I stood there in the mud with the last part of that going through me, because that is the sentence the Prism never bothers to put up in lights: *everything I get, I get because somebody let me see it*. The white room had picked a jack-of-all-trades for the job, and the *trade* has a name in every language, and here in the March they call it learning, and the fee is always paid by the humility of the one collecting.

***

My father, it turned out, had one more conversation in him, and he had saved it — the way he saved the good rope — for the last evening before the crew's trial dig was to start, when the light was going the color of weak tea over the stubbly south field. He'd had it out with Benn at the meeting-yard that afternoon, at full length, in public, in the dialect of *terms*: nine marks of fee against three marks of runner's wages and the town-rate work Benn had offered, with a guardian's seal on the indenture and the solstice clause, and *two* clauses from Cato the hedge-mage that nobody expected, which Benn accepted with the particular respect of a man discovering his counterpart across the table: "The boy's board and shoe at *your* risk, not the job's. And any light he throws, he throws at *what* — that he tells you. Any light he keeps —" here my father had paused, and finished it, plainly, as a man moving a stone, "—that's between him and his maker and the howe, and if you get curious past the contract, the contract ends, and you find out what my *family* is worth on the March market."

I know that transcript because I was behind the woodshed knowing it, at the exact distance a child is supposed to be at, and because, at the end of it, Halfdan came around the shed corner and stood over me with his hands on his hips and said the terms in a different dialect:

"You'll go, because you've got your mother's face on you when you've made your mind up, and it's a hard face to argue with, and a worse one to lie to." He sat down, heavily, on the block, and the whole of his shoulders *settled*, and that was the real conversation. "I can't keep you out of that. I could, and then you'd go anyway at fourteen with some *factor's* company and not mine, and I've read enough ledgers to know a bad investment when it's made for me. So: the solstice at home or I walk to Saltmarsh and make *noise* where noise costs. You report what the light does — *what*, not *how* — to Cato, and *everything* to your mother, and the knot-line you teach Astrid. You come back with all your parts and *count them at the ford*. And Kale—"

"Sir."

"You were a hard child to raise because you were never *afraid* of the right things," my father said, and I sat up off my own back at that more than the shield-boss ever got me, because the man had been *watching*, all my careful five years, every curated face, and the words came out now not as an accusation but as a bill he was choosing to settle — "and the night of the wolves I found you checking *boards*. So I need it from your own mouth, once, not twice: is the thing under your ribs the reason you don't *flinch* the way a child does. And if it is — do you think, boy, with the flat look you've had since the fever, and do me the respect of not *pricing* the answer — do you still *want* anything? Badly? Enough to come home?"

I want to record, in the ledger of this life, exactly what I answered, because it is the first true sentence I have said to a Vester with the Prism's filter down and my whole heart in the room like a bad dog.

"When the wolves came I felt my *aim*," I said. "And Astrid came down the ladder anyway, and I would have died in that yard, and my hands would've been *right*. I want — yes. I want *them*, specifically, and the want is under the quiet and not in it, and the proof is that I'm *scared right now* of what saying that costs me, and I said it." I looked at him, because the howe had taught me what a straight answer was for. "You asked me *what* I touch. That's the thing I touch, sir. That's my whole inventory."

My father did not speak for a while, and I could hear the creek past the pen and the crew's horses at the ford, and the sky was doing that thing where the stars come up through the blue like ink in water. Then he reached out and took my face in both hands the way Mother does for the eye-check, except he is not a midwife and did not know what he was doing and did it like a man greasing a hinge: *wrong, with total sincerity*.

"Good," he said. "You'll be a strange man, whatever else you become. Go inside. Your mother's making your favorite and pretending she isn't, and there's an eight-year-old at the window doing the *arithmetic* on that squirrel supply, and she's going to want her share of this." And he got up off the block, and before I could stop him, he said the last thing, to the shed, to the creek, in the tone of a man posting the notice: "She tied the pen brace right. Your *mother's* knots." And he went in to the supper, and I sat a long while in the dark on my block, not feeling very much, and feeling — under the calm, where the shard pulsed its tempo and my heart insisted on its own — *too much of everything, at once*, and I let it, exactly as my mother had ordered: I shook, in the woodshed, where she could hear, all the way through the door, like a man being *paid back in his own coin*.

***

Astrid's terms were delivered at the supper-table, in the voice of a girl of eight holding her full fists on the table like a bailiff's clerk:

"I am *storing* you," she said, "the way the squirrel stores the nuts. You are going to Saltmarsh and being *buried* there for *work* and you will *forget* and come back in the spring, so I have made provisions." She opened the left fist. Four acorns, a chipped clay bead, and — for *economics*, I understood — a single copper penny, bent.

"Two percent a day," I said solemnly, and put it in my shirt with the shard's light, which hummed at the acorns. "That's a fortune, Astrid. You're going to make a bad man of me."

"It's *my* bad man," she said, and Mother said, from the stove, "You're both of mine, eat," and that is the whole of the negotiation.

The trial dig was set for the first dry morning, six men and one runner, and the *fifth* man on the crew's side was a hired line-hand from the Ford who would go home as soon as the howe was open — and then it was the last night, and I was up at the fence doing what I always did when the day had *spent* me: not thinking, just standing near where the light from the house fell short.

Maren came up the lane from the ford at a walk, horse not saddled, and she stopped on the far side of the fence with the whole of the dark of the valley behind her and did not say hello, because she was not built out of that.

"Benn says the rope's yours on the trial," she said. "I checked the anchors. The howe held the ones your father threw over the breach — that's the *only* thing it's done all week, and it's done it *neatly*, which is worse, and I want to know the one true thing about it before I stand in the doorway holding your line." She looked at me, sideways, over the top rail. "Not a report. Not the story. You're *flat*, stranger-Boy — you've been flat since the wolves, and you've been *wrong-flat* tonight, and wrong-flat is a thing that gets delvers killed in a bad hall. So: whatever's under your ribs. Do it *hurt*?"

And there it was: the door standing open, in the dark, in the voice of a girl who had been bought off a wagon at nine and had *never once* in her life seen an opening like that, closing up to keep *herself* dry. And the Prism put the ledger up in front of me and I looked at the ledger and closed it, and I opened my hand over the fence rail, and I let the calm come down to its lowest, and I *did it the way it wants to be done*, for the first time since the hare, in front of a person:

The frost went over the top rail in a low white breath, and where it touched the wood it *stood*: a lattice, a prismatic thing, every facet of the ice a separate small window — and, in each window, *bent*: the yard-light, the fence, the horse's eye, all of it refracted *wrong*, and in the wrongness you could see — you could *see*, if your name was Kale Vester and the shard was learning to use your eyes — the howe's cold as a *thread*, a seam, running up the hill into the black like a road that had been *drawn* there.

The shard pulsed once, satisfied, the way a cat crosses the room to a lap.

Maren stared at the ice-lattice until it sublimated off the rail into a kind of cold steam, and the whole time she didn't move, and neither did the horse, and when it was gone she turned one shoulder onto the dark and said, with her back to me, the thing she had come forty miles to be able to say:

"My grandmother was right about the keepers," she said. "And I've got a debt to a factor in Saltmarsh that has its own ledger, and a name — Skell's what they put in the book, 'Skell's *thing*,' the year they dragged me out of that wagon at nine with a coat too big for me. I've been paying it back in *miles* for seven years. If you tell a soul I saw a boy do that on a fence, Benn will hear it before morning, and Drask's factor will hear it before the week, and I will lose the last of what I'm *earning* toward a hearth of my own, and you will have a friend who doesn't *trust* you in a line where you need one." She looked at the howe, up there, shut and patient. "So. I saw *nothing*, and the boy's knots are fine, and the line's *mine* tomorrow, and if you die in there, I'll be *mildly* inconvenienced and I will tell Cato the lattice was *my* idea." She swung up. "And if it *hurts*, you tell the midwife, and not the light. That's not me being kind."

"'Water,'" I said, softly, after her, and she — at the top of the stirrup, in the black — lifted one hand off the saddle horn and held it up, two fingers, for a count of three, the exact length of a courtesy owed, and then the two of us, the girl and the howe, were dark and quiet together, one of us *keeping score*, the other one, apparently, at last, *at peace about the price*.

***

The indenture was read at the meeting-yard stumps in the first thin light, and Bailiff Kel officiated with the face of a man who has just been made a *witness* in a document that outranks him, which he enjoyed less than no money, and my mother put the family seal to it — *Signe, her mark, the midwife's* — because Halfdan had ridden to the Ketterings for the week's one lambing, and this is the thing the ballads never tell you about a frontier contract: the party most likely to read it aloud, and *understand* it, is a boy of eleven who did the arithmetic of a fine before the men had finished the first draft.

We closed the howe on the third day: opened it, in fact, the way you close an *argument*, on the crew's terms — no fire in the water-room, no steel past the third threshold, the two dead robbers brought up and down through a cut of the builders' own making, which Cato chalked, and named, and *paid* the howe's ledger for, four silver marks laid across the breach-stones by the outrider's own hand: "recompense for the light of two lamps," he said, in the voice of a man who had read the same Runa-script a century *too* well. The keeper did not come. The water came. They flooded the reliquary room at the *howe's own sluice gate*, on the fifth evening, like a door being locked from the inside with *our* permission, and the last thing I saw on the way down the scarp was the breach knitting itself, at last, out of what it was — a seam, and then a scar, and then the plain cold hill — and nothing in the world but four men's graves and a village's roof and a debt at Saltmarsh and a *thread* of cold I alone could see running up under the snow, patient, unpaid, and — for the first time — *counting back*.

I took a breath that smelled of river and horse and cold ash and somebody's bread, and I mounted the hired mule that was, on paper, now my *salary*, and the crew's four riders went off down the lane like a sentence being started, and behind me, at the fence of a good hand of a farm, an eight-year-old was *storing* the winter's acorns with the furious arithmetic of a person who means it, and the howe, on the hill, held its breath out of habit, and my heart — the small, ordinary one, the un-Prism'd, the Vester one — was, for the record, *at the wrong pace on purpose*, and I did not check it.
