# Chapter 1 — An Offer You Can Refuse

*from **A Nascent Light** — Vol. 1: First Light*

---

I slowly opened my eyes, only to be met by an unfamiliar ceiling and the sensation of a mattress that wasn't mine. I rose from the bed and looked around, to find that I was in some sort of empty room with no windows and no doors.

"Where the hell am I?" I looked at my hand, clenched it, and then unclenched it. It was unnaturally cold. "Something doesn't feel right…" I looked up at the white ceiling. "Am I… dead?"

You would be correct.

"Gah!" I got spooked by the sudden voice and jumped off the bed. "Wh—who said that!?"

I did.

The voice was coming from everywhere, even from inside my own head. "Who are you? What are you? What happened to me? Where is this place!?"

In that order: I do not have a name. I am a function left attending this room, retained for the sole purpose of helping you in your transition to your new life. You suffered a heart attack while you were sleeping. Your landlady found you in the morning and called the hospital, where you were pronounced dead on arrival. Finally, you are in the nexus where the earth and the afterlife meet. You were brought here because you were chosen.

My face fell at the news. "Oh. So I really am dead."

You seem… awfully calm about this, considering the situation.

"Would it be better if I was kicking, screaming, and cursing? It wouldn't bring me back. Not counting the shock that's keeping me calm right now."

As amusing as it would be to watch that happening, I'd rather get this along.

"Get what—"

Congratulations! You have been chosen to be the new host of the Nascent Kaleidoscope.

I blinked at the reveal. "The what?"

The Kaleidoscope. A device for refracting one thing into ten thousand, built before your species had words for light. It is broken, and it is dying, and it requires a mind to finish the piece of itself you are now carrying. You will note that I said *requires*, not *offers*. This is not a courtship.

"So I'm a spare part with anxiety."

You are adapting well.

"I'm dead, bored, and being talked to by a room. I'm doing great, actually." I sat back down on the bed because my legs had started doing something untrustworthy. "Alright then, spare-part duty. What does the, uh. The Kaleidoscope. Do."

It is most commonly understood through the grammar your species developed for it. I will guide you through the process so that you can get acquainted with the system.

"A *system*." A screen snapped into existence in front of me, blue-white and hovering, and I felt my dead heart try to beat out of reflex. "Okay. Okay, that's—yeah, that tracks."

Quest Alert!

Quest: Tutorial
Pretty self-explanatory, yes?
Objective 1: Open Status Menu
Objective 2: Open Inventory
Objective 3: Create your character
Rewards: 1000 EXP, Skills, Perks, ?

"So my first quest is a tutorial quest." I looked back and forth across the empty room, as if expecting a proctor with a clipboard. "Um. How do I get started?"

The first step is to open your menu. Simply make a mental command, or say "Status."

"Status."

Immediately a long screen appeared in front of me with information.

Name: ?
Title: —
Race: ?
Alignment: True Neutral
Level: 1
HP: 100/100
SP: 100/100
LUM: 100/100
VIT: 10
STR: 10
DEX: 10
END: 10
INT: 10
WIS: 10
MAG: 10
LUK: 10
Money: 0

I was completely in shock. This was real. This was an actual status screen. A *real* status screen.

"Haha! This is awesome!" I exclaimed, and then I noticed something. "Why doesn't it say my name? Or my race for that matter?"

All new users must register under a new name and race. If you use your original name, you will experience HELL. Please keep in mind that we are not responsible for any permanent harm that comes to you.

"Well," I said, "that's one way of saying we don't give a damn about your life choices."

It is the honest way. Proceed. The next step is your inventory. Either tap the bag icon on the screen or simply think "Inventory."

I did just that, and the window quickly changed. It now showed a series of empty boxes that held nothing except two highlighted slots containing my current clothes — the flannel shirt and sweatpants I'd died in, folded like evidence. On the left of the screen showed what I had equipped right now.

"Memorialize me in laundry," I muttered. "Nice."

Please select a race:
[Human] [?] [?] [?]

"One option is visible while the other three are unknown. What's that about?"

The first icon is self-explanatory.
The second is for low-tier entities: slime, goblin, skeleton, and so on.
The third is for mid-tier entities: demi-human, beast-kin, and the lesser immortal races.
The last is for high-tier races: dragon, archon, outer god, and worse.
These three choices are randomized per host. Choose at your own merit.

By its logic, humans would be considered a mid-tier race, filed in with the monsters. I could choose Human, or try my luck with the other three. I don't really like taking risks, especially when they're luck based. My luck has never been good; there's a statistical term for what usually happens to me, and it isn't "miracle."

Despite how tempting it was, I played it safe and went with [Human].

Race selected: Human.
A universal race, existing across all of time and space. Despite their organic and overall weak appearance, they make up for it with their wits, tenacity, and overall stubbornness. A jack of all trades. They are creatures of great compassion and great violence. Their true potential, however, lies in their ability to learn and understand, as the strongest and most intelligent of the species can turn the tables on the most powerful of opponents.

Racial modifiers:
VIT ×2, STR ×4, DEX ×3, END ×4, INT ×3, WIS ×3, MAG ×2, LUK ×2, CHA ×1

"Huh. I did not expect racial stat bonuses." I scrolled the description again. It read like a recruitment poster, but the last line sat well with me. *Their true potential lies in their ability to learn and understand.* It was the only kind of cheat code I'd ever trusted. "This could be helpful. Though I still have doubts, at least I know what I'm dealing with."

The guide's next window was a diagram of a human body alongside several icons.

Lastly is character creation. This is where you will create the body that will house your soul for the foreseeable future. You will be reborn into the world you choose; until you awaken fully within it, a pseudo-personality will protect the body and live its small life.

"Protect it from what?"

From loneliness. The interval varies. In your case, it will be long. I am sorry; the piece you carry requires a child's patience, and the seeding begins at the first memories, which you will not hold.

I stared at the diagram for a long time. Somewhere in that fine print was the sentence *you will not remember this room*, and I found that I didn't even resent it. When the last decade of your life amounts to a lease, a job that fixes other people's printers, and a cat with a heart condition, the erasure of the handoff reads less like a punishment than like a mercy with good manners.

"Alright," I heard myself say. "Let's make something sturdier."

The icons moved under my hands like a character creator I had never been allowed to enjoy at fourteen. I gave the body my mother's coloring and my father's height. I widened the shoulders, thinned the wrists of an office lifer, and set the eyes a plain, hard-to-remember grey. I spent the longest on the hands. In twenty-eight years I had never done one single thing with mine that outlived me; if the joke was that I had to try again, then let me at least try with better knuckles.

Is this the look you desire?
[Y/N]

I pressed *Yes*.

Now it is time to type your new name.

I put in the first name I could think of. Not the old one — the room had been clear about that, and I had read enough of these stories to know the fine print on fine print — but the name my mother had used when she was proud of me. Two syllables, no surname worth burning.

Kale. Is this correct?
[Y/N]

I pressed *Yes*.

Quest Complete!

Quest: Tutorial
Objective 1 (Complete): Open Status Menu
Objective 2 (Complete): Open Inventory
Objective 3 (Complete): Create your character
Rewards: 1000 EXP, Skills, Perks, Broken Light, ?
Level Up (×4)!

Perks acquired:

[Prism Calm]

Note: the calm is a filter. The host will feel events at reduced volume while it is active. Prolonged engagement is not recommended.

[Prism Body]
Grants a body that interfaces with the physical world as a coherent system.
Fully restores HP, SP, and LUM when asleep.

[Regeneration]
Restores HP, SP, and LUM per minute.
HP regeneration = VIT ×4
LUM regeneration = MAG ×3
SP regeneration = END ×2

Skills acquired:

[Mirror Lv. 1/10] — Cost 0 LUM
Provides information on targets, and, at higher levels, on their intent.
Note: Mirror reflects only what the host can comprehend. It cannot show you what you are not yet able to understand.

[Refraction Lv. 1/1] — Cost variable
The host's light may be bent into a working effect: heat, cold, force, flash. Effects are learned, not granted. The Kaleidoscope supplies the bend; the host must supply the idea.

And one final line of the rewards list, in a window that flickered when I looked at it directly, like a bad pixel in the air.

Broken Light (unique, untradeable, unremovable)
A shard of the shattered Kaleidoscope, grafted to the host's soul. It is one of many. It will draw to its siblings and it will be drawn to. Reassembly is not a request.
Sealed within: 1 class key (locked until the shard takes root).

"Well… that's going to be a problem," I said. "'Unremovable' is doing a lot of work in that line. Drawn *to*, huh. Sounds like I just became a lighthouse for people with knives."

That is an acceptable summary.

"And the class key? Locked until it 'takes root'? What's the timeline?"

Uncertain. Previous hosts ranged from a decade to a lifetime. Most of them are still lists of things the shard-wardens found under rubble.

"Previous hosts." Of course. "What happened to the ones who finished? Who won?"

The Attendant's silence had a texture, which shouldn't have been possible for a voice with no throat.

You are the first host it has liked, it said at last. That is not a promise. It is an observation, and it is why you are still in one piece.

I laughed once, short. "Cool. Cool. Anything else, or do I get to the part where I pick a world and stop being a metaphysical debate?"

All that is left is to select the world in which you desire to be reborn.

A list unrolled, and it was not a list of vacations.

Choose a Refraction:

**The Long Autumn** — a march of farmed valleys beyond the last mapped keep, where the old kings lie under grass and the crown owes more than it can pay. Magic is weather: common, cold, and uncared for. *Recommended for those reading the genre for the first time.*

**The Drowned Mile** — a coast of wrecked gods and tide-locks, where the priesthood is a shipping company and the sea collects debts.

**The Ash Court** — an empire built around a bonfire that has not gone out in nine hundred years, where every noble family is descended from a funeral.

**The White Panopticon** — a tower-city of sealed windows, where every citizen is graded nightly by a committee that has not slept in four centuries.

"Why these?" I asked. "Where's the fire-breathing, flying-castle, harem-with-a-side-of-kingdom option?"

They exist. You would be assigned to them on request. The shard, however, is currently resonant to the first. This is not a recommendation, but it is what it is.

"Resonant to the farm valley. Figures. I get reincarnated and the relic has *taste in real estate*." I looked back at the list and gave it the respect of actually thinking. The coast had shipping, and shipping meant records, and records meant people who could find a broken light in a ledger. The tower was a prison with good lighting. The bonfire empire was exactly the kind of place that would burn a thing like me on principle. "The Long Autumn. No gods watching, plenty of weather."

An acceptable choice. Beginning calibration.

I felt a sudden jolt in my brain as a screen popped in front of me.

[1%… 45%… 88%… 100%! Calibration complete!]

"What did you do?"

I downloaded a copy of my program into your system. It will regulate the pseudo-personality and will awaken you when the time is right. It will also, in all likelihood, outlive my interest in you. Are you ready?

I nodded.

There was a hissing noise, and when I turned, a section of the wall broke down, revealing a light so blinding I could have gone blind if I had dared open my eyes wider. I covered my face as I approached it. I entered and vanished into the portal, and not long after, the wall began to repair itself, restoring the room to what it was before, minus one bed with a faint body-print and one man-shaped dent in the universe.

[Game Start!]
