# THE BEAT 'EM UP — system spec

Two tiers. **BRIEF** = from the locked premise, immutable. **RULING** = settled by the agent to make the BRIEF run; user may veto any by number. Nothing else gets added silently — new mechanic beats get a ruling number and a line in `README.md`'s decision log *before* they appear on the page.

## The BRIEF rules
1. Weekly naming right; one established-IP fictional character.
2. Arrival in the target's universe, within 200 m.
3. Target must be capable of self-defense at arrival (not asleep, unconscious, incapacitated, mid-fight).
4. Then he fights them. Win = full copy of their abilities: powers, skills, knowledge.
5. No time limit. Abandon/return at will.
6. Cadence ~monthly ⇒ growth engine; each win changes what's winnable next.
7. Challenge treated as a mini arc; Eli may run a story in-world before leaving.

## RULINGS (vetoable)

**R1 — Naming & windows.** Every Monday 00:00 Eli gets one **naming window**: he names a character, a card forms (below). He can re-name/re-pick any number of times *within the week* — free, no cost — but only one card can be **armed** at a time. Arming does nothing; **entering** the card is what starts the challenge.

**R2 — The card.** A plain box, no voice, no emoji. Fields: `TARGET / WORLD / STATUS` (live snapshot of what they're doing right now, one or two sentences), `PACKAGE` (the exact list the system will copy — clinical, noun-heavy), `STATE` (eligible/unavailable + reason), `COOLDOWN`. The card is the only "personality" the story's UI gets: it answers questions only by being terse. Formatting per `09_style.md`.
- If `STATE: unavailable` (target currently helpless/dead/mid-combat/etc.), Eli can't enter *that snapshot*; cards re-snapshot every Monday with the window. Story uses this: bad luck with windows, and the horror of reading a `PACKAGE` list for someone Eli watched on TV this morning (`WORM LOCAL: see R13`).

**R3 — Entry & arrival.** Eli arms a card, says/“thinks” *enter* — hard to misuse, since he carries a backpack he chose. Arrival at a safe point 150–200 m from the target, ground level, no airspace/mid-ocean spawns. The system picks arrival **location** only; the **time** is that world's living now (R4). He arrives with what he's wearing plus whatever's on his person; no ID, no local currency, no gear beyond physical items (which are NOT copied on exit — see R10).

**R4 — Living worlds.** Every fictional IP exists as a real universe *somewhere*, running in its own real time. The card shows the character as they currently are — which may be before, during, or past their "canon." Eli's franchise knowledge is therefore a **hypothesis**, never a script. (This is the same lie his Worm knowledge tells him, mirrored — thematically load-bearing; do not explain it away on the page.)

**R5 — What a fight is.** The system adjudicates **combat contests**: strikes, grappling, weapon bouts, sparring with injury on the table. Games, races, debates, sports — no (a card can show "declined" if Eli tries to launder basketball into combat; the reader learns the boundary the way Eli does, by getting told no in the terse voice).
- **Defeat** = target can't continue, concedes mid-contest, is rendered unable to fight, OR — the system's quiet extension — the target is beaten at their own declared terms *if they accepted the bout as a fight*. Never by points, never by argument, never against someone who didn't agree to be fought.
- **Killing** wins the card. The system doesn't block it (Manton, not mercy) and never comments. Cost lands on Eli; see `08_roadmap.md` (this is a loaded railgun for later arcs; do not fire early).

**R6 — Who may refuse.** Targets can and do decline; a declined bout simply never becomes a fight — the challenge stays open, Eli may walk, talk, leave, or re-attempt later *within the same visit*. The system will not force a fight onto a person (their consent to defend themselves is assumed only by *acceptance* of the contest). Exception: hostile targets attacking Eli may be beaten validly in self-defense — the "duel" framing doesn't protect people who try to mug him.

**R7 — Copy scope, the hardware rule.** Win = copy of **abilities**: supernatural powers (they're abilities; the body is rebuilt as needed to carry them — a copied quirk/bending/stand functions), **trained skills at the owner's demonstrated level** (forms, techniques, craft procedures, professional knowledge), and **languages/qualifications**. It does **not** copy: raw IQ, personality, memories-as-experience (he gets her *knowledge*, not her *life*), physical build beyond what a power requires, possessions, relationships. Ceiling rule: he gets what they can do, not what they are. This cap is what keeps the snowball from becoming a god-ball — and Eli discovers it the hard way in 1.9 (tries to out-design a design, hits his own working memory, is *pissed*).
- Copy lands at the **moment of victory** (he can't delay, can't choose which slice). No take-from-her: copies, not thefts. The target loses nothing measurable. (The guilt still lands because the *door closes* — R8.)

**R8 — One character, one challenge.** Never re-challenge the same individual, ever, win or not — **and the visit is non-renewable**: travel happens *only* as part of an armed challenge. No tourism, no going back to fix things, no returning a borrowed jacket. Every world is entered at most once a month, and only someone in it can be the reason. This is the price beneath every prize — Eli learns it in ch 11 and it colors every arc after.

**R9 — Return & abandon.** *Leave* — a thought, any phrasing, instant, no matter where he is or who's watching (a hard rule: no hostage standoffs with the exit door, no "I couldn't because they were watching" plots). Consumes the month whether he won or not. **Death-gate:** if Eli takes an injury he couldn't survive untreated, he's pulled home at the last non-death moment — wound included, challenge consumed, plus a **14-day naming lockout** (the system's only "punishment," and not clearly one; could be nothing but structure. Don't explain.)

**R10 — Nothing comes back physically, nothing goes except him.** No stolen goods carried home, no local money kept (what's in his pockets converts at a brutal rate the card warns him about once: "items not of your origin: returned to origin." So he can *carry* a coin all month and lose it at the gate — one scene worth teaching readers.) Conversely BB items go with him fine (his pack, his knife, his wallet).

**R11 — Time is 1:1.** A day there is a day here, same flow, no frozen-time mercy. Absences must be lived and covered for. His mother has hospital-grade bullshit detection. Budget absences into every arc.

**R12 — Language pass.** He gets functional comprehension + speech in the target's living language (accent intact, grammar bad, reading slow — reading only if it's his own or the copy includes it, per R7). Enough to get in trouble, not enough to blend forever.

**R13 — Worm locals are untested, not forbidden.** Eli has never named a Brockton Bay character and **won't** — the reasons are his and ugly (if the card shows *his* classmates' `PACKAGE`s, either free will is a joke or he's just a boy who can duel other kids; he prefers not knowing). The system never explains why it would or wouldn't accept. Keep this in the drawer; the ch 15 hook is a card naming *someone from here* that he declines to open — reader decides.

**R14 — SI/insert policy.** Cards for characters who themselves carry a meta-system (Gamer/SI/insert-type "characters" of other people's stories, as Eli half-understands) display `STATE: reserved — occupied line.` Eli doesn't know what it means; no further comment. Seeded mystery; pay off (or keep unpaid) deep-game.

**R15 — What the system is NOT.** No quests, no shops, no stats sheet, no levels, no dialogue, no hints, no rewards for anything except victory in an accepted fight, no UI outside cards and the one-line confirmations. It is a machine with a form field. Every "personality" the reader suspects is projection — including Eli's.

## Worked examples (locked rulings, used in Arc 1)
- Eli names the ATLA water tribe's strategist (in-story Sokka beat, ch 2): card forms, `PACKAGE: planning, swordsmanship (self-taught, adequate), leadership (situational), inventories (partial), fluency: common tongue`. Eli: "that's a *boy with a boomerang*." He learns: package list is the *system's* valuation, which is blunt enough to be funny and honest enough to be terrifying — the same box that rates a farmboy's boomerang rates, say, Legend, and never says which is which.
- Eli names **Aang (currently)** (ch 2, rejected in his head before naming): the system would happily card him — but whatever's true of a living Avatarverse "now" could be a 100-year-old monk or a wartime head of state. R4 taught him the word he adopts as policy: **no main characters** (until Arc 3's thesis breaks him: main characters are the point).
- Naming Mei (ch 4): `STATUS: testing equipment, ground level, alone (voluntarily, loudly, at a mannequin).` `STATE: eligible. She is awake. She is armed. She has accepted nothing yet.` R6 on the page: Eli has to *ask her to fight him*, and the system won't do it for him. That awkward ask is the thesis of the first challenge.
