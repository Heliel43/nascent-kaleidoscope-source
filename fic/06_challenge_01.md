# CHALLENGE #1 — HATSUME MEI (My Hero Academia) — dossier

**Canon verification (2026-09-21):** Hero.fandom + Fanlore + NamuWiki character pages; depicted in-bank by *Ill-Gotten Gains* (MHA-verse canon texture, 500k words) and *Dost Thou Even Steal Hearts* (MHA×P5 — includes Hatsume as a live minor). **Facts used on the page are limited to the box below; everything else in the arc must not contradict them.**

## The verified box
- U.A. **Support Course, first year** at Sports-Festival era; ~15 years old; **Quirkless** (never any Quirk shown/stated). "Genius-level intelligence, inventing prowess, mechanical knowledge"; calls her inventions **"babies"**; obsessive about testing; misses social cues; self-advertising to the point of pathology; Power Loader is her teacher-scion ("it doesn't help that she is easily distracted... Power Loader scolding her for her recklessness").
- **Canon fight data (the whole basis of Eli's odds):** at the Sports Festival she took **Tenya Iida — a hero-course student wearing *her own* support buff — to ten minutes** of pure gear-play, then **forfeited once the ad read was done**, having won nothing and gained everything. She is an engineer performing "combat" as marketing. Her gear malfunctions *on purpose* in demos and *accidentally* in battle. She has no combat training, no killer instinct, wind-issues, and zero intent to hurt (a spar, to her, is a *field test*).
- Her arena = a festival prep field / a support testing yard, i.e., **open ground, known times, cameras pointed at the other event.**

## Why Eli picks her (Q1: why would he choose this person?) — the in-story logic chain
1. **Win condition is mechanical, not mystical:** a baseline boy who spends his one free afternoon doing nothing but *counting* — her rig's runtime is public in her own footage, the batteries are rated, and the math a hardware-store kid can do says: **run the clock, don't touch the wires, tag her when the demo dies.** He is right about her gear. That's the terrifying part — the fandom was *right* sometimes.
2. **Worth-it by his own list:** support-gear engineering = money (the shop, the college fund), medicine (his mother's hospital, Nora's nebulizer, "a real splint that inflates"), safety (lights, alarms, a winch on the bike that can pull a body out of a burning car — **ch 14 is this sentence on the page**). A fireball makes Eli a cape; *her package makes Eli a supplier.* He says the quiet part at 2 a.m., and it doesn't sound bad until he hears himself.
3. **Clean-loss rule:** she loses nothing permanent (copy ≠ theft, R7) — and her *story* actually improves (a brutal test subject who patches her prototypes in the field is the greatest gift to her portfolio). He needs that sentence; he knows he needs it. That's the moral toll and its receipt.
4. **The consent problem is the arc:** R6 says she has to accept a fight. There's no villainy in her schedule. He has to walk up to a girl, ask her to spar, and not lie about why. He tries the truth once — it lands as a *pitch* ("she's sold on being a test") — and the lie is what he can't forgive himself for: **the system's card wording turns 'duel' into 'exhibition' in translation (R12), and Eli lets her read it as her own idea.** Every future arc's price begins here.

## Why he survives her world (Q2)
U.A. campus, sports-festival week = the most supervised ground in that verse; the threats are *paperwork* (unregistered minor, foreign, no Quirk → expulsion-level if caught), not lethality. Arrival: support course's back field, 190 m from a mannequin and a cackling girl — a "safe" spawn with a joke in it. Exit rule: `leave` always works, so the arc never needs him captured for suspense; the tension is *social* (badge access, curfew, cameras, Power Loader's memory), which is a more Worm-shaped horror anyway.

## What winning costs (Q3, the actual ledger)
1. **Time, paid 1:1 (R11):** eleven days out — "cousin in Scranton, funeral" (the lie he'd never tell Diane *to her face*, delivered by text). Truancy letters, a vice principal meeting, Nora doing his algebra to cover the wrongness at home. He came back with a *craft* and no excuse for the hole he cut in his family's worst month.
2. **The door closes (R8):** he can never go back to warn her, thank her, or hand back the *one* physical thing he borrowed (a multi-tool she'd already thrown away and he kept). Her workshop, meanwhile, names a line of safety gear after a "test subject who vanished": a gift for an audience of one. He sees the *ad read* on a borrowed screen before he leaves and understands he's a product testimonial in a world that got nothing out of him either. Nobody is ruined. Nobody is evened.
3. **The ceiling (R7):** he comes home *able* and not *genius*; the first three builds work, the fourth fails and the failure is *on him*; the grief of discovering the gift has a roof (beat 1.9), fought with a soldering iron at 3 a.m.
4. **Visibility:** one saved kid + one powered cart on a shop invoice = the first file with his name in it — in a city where the wrong files get people *disappeared* (Coil's interest, arc 1 close/arc 2 open). He can't un-be-useful. That's the whole bargain: the power didn't cost him a soul. It cost him *the option of being a nobody.*

## The duel (Arc 1, beats 1.6–1.7, beat-locked)
Ground: an empty prep field behind the support stadium, dusk, the festival crowd audible (canon's offscreen while they watch the event). Terms: she proposes best-of-tags, "no face, no gear damage, and you hold still for four seconds so I can film the 'I-95 field rig' demo." He agrees to all three, including the stupid one, and that's why he wins: **the four seconds cost her her second battery** — he tags her, she laughs *while down*, Power Loader referees the rematch nobody wants, and the card flips: `RESULT: defeat (nonstandard, accepted).`
- **No injuries beyond a wrist burn (his, self-inflicted mid-duel — he catches a superheated demo plate off his face; treated in 1.7 with first-aid accuracy: he does it exactly right, badly, with his teeth on his shirt).**
- **No humiliation of her; the crowd that sees it (12 kids from the Support course) is *recruiting* her, not laughing; the one adult who sees it (Aizawa, passing, dead-eyed) sees two kids who didn't damage anything and walks on, and that's the whole reason the campus stays quiet.**
- Transfer beat (ch 10): lands at the moment of victory (R7), so the middle of the duel is when he becomes *an engineer* — the second half of the fight is already performed by someone new. That's the beat the style guide protects at all costs: **do not narrate the copy as level-up lust; narrate it as *hearsay* arriving in his own skull.**

## What the copy actually grants (locked list — the card text, on the page, ch 4)
```
PACKAGE: support-item engineering (U.A. methodology), power systems (electrical
design, battery practice, field-repair), fabrication (machining, welding,
composites), prototype documentation habits, hero-equipment industry norms
(Musutafu), first-aid integration (support-grade), "the pitch" (advertising,
sponsored-demonstration practice), languages: Japanese (functional).
QUIRK: none on record. Ceiling: human.
```
**Deliberately excluded, per R7:** her IQ, her "genius" as a *quantity*, her relationships, her drive. Eli gets her *toolbox and her blueprints*; he has to supply the *hours*. (Reader lesson seeded: the system copies *what a person does*, never *why*.)
