## Why Eli picks her (Q1 — the mc-brain chain, on the page in 1.2)
1. His fandom model says "side character = weak." He rejects Megumin (main character; also a child; also the girl is a *disaster* with a body count of one village's patience) and Aqua (goddess = rule 1 dies; also church-level attention). He lands on **the rival friend nobody counts**: a teenager in the *safe* town, doing *errands*, whose canonical record includes losing to **frogs**.
2. **Easy:** the fight isn't against her; it's against her *rules* — one explosion a day, ceremony first, pride that can't dodge an audience, zero grappling curriculum. A kid who can count to forty-five seconds and roll like an amphibian wins *on the math* — and he checks the math in the bank's terms: spent caster = "mana exhaustion and loss of bodily strength."
3. **Worth it, his way:** a craft would have paid his family's bills; a *power he can hide* (heal a cut, one big crater, walk away) buys the thing he actually needs: not dying, and being able to fix what a first-aid kit can't. The card's package list is so long he laughs out loud in the kitchen (Diane turns; he's "reading the back of the cereal box").
4. **Clean loss for her (rule 4):** she gets a story, a rematch-dinner, the town's adoration for having *fought at all* — she leaves the duel better-known than she came in. He tells himself that's consent *enough*. (He's wrong, and the arc shows him being gently wrong by, specifically, *Berosal biting his sleeve at the goodbye* — the only grudge, level: toddler.)
5. **The world welcomes him:** Axel is an adventurer town; a polite foreign teenager is *Tuesday* (Kazuma precedent). Guild registration = the whole legal problem, solved in a chapter and a half, no paperwork thriller. He can stay a month without making an enemy, which is the user's structural instruction and the point: the arc **lingers**.

## Why he survives her world (Q2)
Belzerg's danger is *outside the walls*, and the arc treats that honestly: he almost gets stepped into paste by the toad problem *twice* (both times saved by being useful instead of brave — a porter with a splint kit and a horse-collar is unkillable *because he's carrying the medkit he's used to handing to actual fighters*). Inside Axel: the guild, the church, Wiz's shop, and the town's own rule of law cover every "outsider gets robbed" plot hole; the worst case is *expelled*, not executed. `LEAVE` is instant; his exit is never suspenseful (rule: no cliffhangers on the door, per `09` §cards).

## The duel (1.6 — beat-locked; "tricks rather than brute force")
Terms (his, dressed as hers): guild-ground bout, "no lethal or structural spells after the opening, first pinned ten seconds loses, and — for fairness to the town — you lead with your best." She agrees because refusing the ceremony in front of an audience is not a thing a Crimson Demon can *do*. Prep (six in-world weeks, all comedy): toad gait drills in the marsh edge (witnessed by Megumin, who mistakes it for a summoning ritual; the misunderstanding *becomes a friendship* and is never corrected); smoke pellets and goggles from the guild quartermaster, paid with porter coin that the gate confiscates later (his only loss, and the system's one joke); a straw dummy wearing his jacket left standing where "the nervous kid" would freeze.
The fight: crowd, announcer, the full chant (he counts aloud with her, to the crowd's delight, because *the chant's rhythm is the clock*); the explosion — fired at what she's sure is him — blows the wall behind *the dummy* forty meters off line; she's empty mid-second-hand; he does not tackle her (brute force: banned). He **sits on her shadow and takes the staff with the mud trick** (a splint-wrap; he's carried one for years); she can't cast what she doesn't have and can't out-grapple a boy on a thing she was never taught because village duels are *spell trading*; she goes down under him like a seal, and concedes at **nine** — because at nine it looks to *the crowd* like a draw, and that's her own pride saving her the tenth (he'd have let go at ten; tell it like she tells it later: "I allowed it").
`RESULT: defeat (accepted, public, voluntary).` Transfer lands **mid-let-go** — the hearsay beat (`09` rule; no level-up lust, no throat-clearing: the copy arrives as a second set of *notes in his own handwriting*, a whole childhood of drills in a village that trained her to chant at a wall before she could read). No one is hurt. Power Loader equivalent: **none** — the guild master offers him *membership*; he declines (one line; it's tempting; the temptation is the last beat of the chapter, no speech).
