# Writing principles

Short on purpose. These exist to keep the voice right and the planning out of the prose — not as quotas.

1. **Consequence first, melodrama never.** (Author directive, absolute.) No invented tragedy, no suffering for gravitas, no spiral scenes. Emotional fallout is welcome when it's the *natural* result of what a person did — keep it honest and short, then let the scene move.
2. **His choices are real *now*.** A character never refuses a thing "because it causes Y six arcs later"; he refuses it because of what he believes today. If that later produces an interesting consequence, let it arrive on its own.
3. **Canon is weather, not a script.** Worm's world has facts and a timeline (see `06`); the fic's plot is what Eli's presence does to the weather. Never "fix" or "fulfill" fandom's version of canon on purpose.
4. **The system is a machine.** A terse box, no voice, no hints, no jokes, no shop. Never let it wink. Everything "personality-like" about it is projection — the reader's or Eli's.
5. **Every fictional world is a place, not a vending machine.** Time spent in-world (before and after a fight) is arc material with its own logic, characters, and costs-of-living — but discovered during writing, not pre-scripted joke by joke.
6. **Voice: competent-but-scared.** Open chapters on hands doing things, not feelings named. Long-then-short sentences; humor lands on the short one and is never explained. Violence gets physics first (weight, surface, breath), feeling second; medical detail must be *correct* — his mother would check.
7. **Fights are preparation paid out.** Eli wins by arranging for the other person to lose their own fight — that difference is characterization, not coolness, and the prose plays it straight and a little funny.
8. **The prose must never feel algorithmic.** No mandatory motifs, tics, recurring objects, chapter-end rituals, or symmetry requirements. If a phrase starts showing up because the *planner* likes it, cut it. Callbacks appear because the chapter misses them, or they don't appear.
9. **Format housekeeping:** first-person past; Worm-style numbering and three-word chapter titles ("1.0 The First Aid Kit"); 8–15k words per chapter; dates on chapter headers when they help the reader track a busy week.
10. **Length is earned, not padded.** QQ serials keep readers with forward motion; an arc runs as long as its story is. If a chapter exists to hit a beat count, the beat goes, not the chapter.
11. **The user's rewritten chapter is canon**, always and immediately — it overrides every plan file here. Update `03_continuity.md` from *their* version before drafting the next chapter.
