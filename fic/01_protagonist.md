# Protagonist — Eli Carter, 17

**The trait that matters:** he doesn't walk past a problem just because he's scared of it — and he isn't fearless, and he isn't reckless. He won't step between fighters, won't charge a gang, dives for cover in cape fights like everyone else. But once he's somewhere safe he doesn't shrug: he calls the ambulance, gets an adult for the harassed kid, returns the wallet. His reflex on seeing something wrong is *"what can I actually do?"* — not "I'll save everyone," not "someone else will."

Not a paragon. Impatient, a bit judgmental, gets pulled into arguments he should walk past, stubborn about unfairness even when it costs him. His morality isn't faith that he can fix things — he knows exactly how powerless he is and refuses to use that as an excuse.

**Background.** Working class, East End. Mother is a nurse at Brockton Bay General (ICU, rotates shifts); he grew up around her job, is comfortable with blood and injury and the unpleasantness of helping people on their worst day. Into emergency medicine, nowhere near qualified for it. Carries a cheap first-aid kit because people get hurt in this city and someone has to help.

**Voice.** First-person past, close. Narrates problems as logistics (who's hurt, where's the exit, who's the adult, what's in my bag). Funny without trying; jokes arrive after the adrenaline, usually mid-chore. Self-aware in a *tired* way, never smug — he doesn't monologue about tropes, he flinches like a witness. Genre knowledge shows as wrong reflexes: checks exits in places he's never been, avoids people he "shouldn't" know, buys supplies in patterns that would concern a tail.

**Limits (real ones).** No combat training. Can't lie well to his mother. 17 with a learner's permit and no car. Money is tight and the fix isn't a wish.
