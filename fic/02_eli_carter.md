# ELI CARTER — character bible

**Elias "Eli" Carter, 17.** Born June 1993 (summer baby — old for his class). Senior at Winslow High, fall 2010. 5'11"/170-ish, runner's legs, hands like a hardware store's; face "forgets itself" — the talent he most relies on.

## Voice (the most important section)
- First-person past tense. He narrates the way he'd talk to a guy he likes at a bus stop: quick, specific, low on adjectives, funny without trying.
- Registers problems as *logistics*: "what's the nearest exit, who's hurt, who's the adult here, what do I have in the bag." Interior jokes arrive *after* the adrenaline, usually while he's doing something else.
- Vocabulary: ER-adjacent (he knows what a suction machine is and what it's for), hardware-store, and an unreasonable amount of internet. Swears when surprised, not for flavor. Never narrates feelings by name ("I was sad" — banned; show the 17-year-old doing three sets of pushups at 1 a.m. instead).
- His genre-awareness is *tired*, not smug. He doesn't monologue like a critic; he flinches like a witness. The Worm-knowledge shows as **wrong reflexes** (he checks exits in places he's never been, avoids people he "shouldn't" know, buys supplies in patterns someone would notice).
- Never a wisecrack under a dying man's gaze. He saves the jokes for after.

## Family & money
- **Diane Carter, 44** — ICU nurse, Brockton Bay General; day-shift now but rotates; divorced 2004 by attrition, not disaster. Reads lies by their timestamps. Loves him in practicals: soup, money in a tin, not asking twice.
- **Nora Carter, 12** — seventh grade at Bryant; asthma (mild, managed, still a liability in this city); draws horses; not a prop. She sees everything.
- **Ray Carter, father** — auto plant until it wasn't, now installs furnaces county-wide; decent man worn thin; sees the kids twice a month; pays half the rent late, every month eventually. (Deliberately *not* an abuser — the fic's tension comes from the city, not home. The one soft spot Eli keeps trying to protect is his family, not his secret.)
- Row house, two blocks off a "nominally fine" street, East End. Money: tight but not drowning — the difference being Eli's Saturday job and Diane's overtime. Eli's savings: **$413 in a jar + $1,900 "college money"** in a savings account he has no legal use for until June.

## Skills & limits (pre-challenge)
- **Can:** Bystander-level CPR/first aid (a real certification from the Red Cross course the school nurse ran; he's the only male in the group photo), bleeding control incl. tourniquets (hospital-mother drilled it), splinting, moving an unconscious person, staying calm while someone else vomits. Driving: learner's permit (insurance costs more than the car — an ongoing Eastside joke with real teeth).
- **Can't:** fight (no training; he runs, blocks, and covers his head; *wins by not losing*), cook beyond rice, lie well to Diane, ask teachers for help.
- **Habits:** the pack is always packed (first-aid kit, work gloves, headlamp, duct tape, a 4-in-1 screwdriver, granola bars, the *same* paperback, a small flashlight he checks out of reflex); counts doors; memorizes phone numbers as a party trick; apologizes to people who are angry at someone else.
- **Bike:** a rescued steel hybrid, fixed and re-fixed — his engine, his savings account, and (post-Arc 1) his first engineering bench.

## His relationship to the fiction
- Treats the memories like an untreated injury: mostly ignores it, checks it sometimes, angry when anyone else touches it. Doesn't "plan around canon" — he can't trust fanfic memory, so his policy is *be ready to help, be impossible to see, be gone before the trouble*.
- The one canon belief he never doubts, and will be punished for doubting late: "I have about a year before everything starts." (See `04_fanon_map.md` #1.)

## Combat identity (what his fights should feel like, all series)
Prep > gear > positioning > stamina > the punch he didn't want to throw. He doesn't win fights; he **arranges for the other person to lose theirs** — and that difference is what he'll argue with himself about for 90 chapters. In a fight, prose slows, sentences shorten, and the sound design is specific (his own breathing, grit, the wet zip of a tourniquet). Post-fight he vomits or doesn't (the alternation is character). He always checks on the person he beat. He hates that this is the fun part of the day.
**Anti-pattern, spelled out:** he is **not** Greg Veder (`Duelist.txt`). No harem, no murder-fun, no "cool SI" larp. His self-awareness is a brake, never an alibi.
