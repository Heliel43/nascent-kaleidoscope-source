# Current arc — Challenge #1: Yunyun (KonoSuba)

Status: **active plan, not a locked outline.** Whatever gets written beats this file; if the story finds a better fight, better trip, or a different shape, change the plan instead of defending it.

## Why this target (his reasoning, so far)
First challenge = delicate. He refuses anything flashy. He wants the rare thing that is both *easy for a baseline human* and *worth having*: someone whose power is real but whose **combat doctrine is a set of rules he can learn and exploit**. Yunyun (KonoSuba) fits, for reasons he can state without numbers:
- Arch-wizard: a genuine, large power (explosion, a broad spell list including **heal magic**, mobility via waypoint teleport, a magic-enhanced body, a wizard's study habits) — a foundation, not a finish.
- Her power has a *budget*: the village's signature spell is functionally once a day, and spending it empties her body's reserve. A fight against her is a fight against her schedule.
- She's a duelist by culture in a guild town where formal challenges are normal and losing publicly costs a man nothing — the fight can happen *as a bout*, with consent, without making anyone a victim.
- She is 17 (the card says so; leave it there), unattached in town, and canonically *miserable at hitting evasive small targets with her big spell* — his fandom memory's one checkable joke about her.
- The world is the friendliest in his pool to a nobody: an adventurer town that exists to absorb weird strangers. He can *live there a while* while prepping, which is exactly what "as long as he needs" is for.

## Shape of the trip (structure, not scenes)
1. **Selection on the page.** The first chapters are the picking — his notebook logic, real doubts, the first names he considers and rejects (including the "worthless package" mistake his fandom brain tries to sell him). This is the sport of the premise; let it breathe.
2. **Ask.** The system won't arrange the fight; he has to ask a person to fight him. That awkward, funny, revealing negotiation is the arc's thesis about what he is willing to be.
3. **The stay.** Meaningful time in Axel *before* the duel: how the guild actually works, what the town runs on, what the walls keep out, what magic costs (literally). He takes work, learns the world's reality against his comedy-world model, meets people. Who he meets and what he does there is **discovered during drafting** — the cast and gags listed in the idea bank are seasoning, not menu.
4. **The fight.** Won by *tricks, not brute force*: preparation, reading her rules, timing her budget, and the ground game nobody in her village curriculum teaches. The tactical design should be worked out during writing so it stays a gamble that pays off, not a checklist that executes. Constraints held: she must have been able to defend (she was, and she chose the terms), nobody gets seriously hurt, and the town is no worse for his visit.
5. **The consequence that's actually his:** he out-thinks a friendly, lonely, trusting girl and *likes* doing it. That gets one honest paragraph of reckoning — no spiral, no lecture. Then: he comes home.
6. **Integration into Brockton Bay.** What arch-wizard kit does to a nurse's son's life: healing hands he can't explain, one big bad day per day he mostly refuses to use, a magic-enhanced body, teleport he has to *learn* (waypoints are his now). The practical fallout: any loud test makes noise the PRT, PHO, and the wrong kind of recruiter will chase; and "no trigger event" is an anomaly his cover story can't answer. Consequence, not tragedy.

**The prize must come with trade-offs, not curses:** one-explosion-a-day is a budget, overdraw hurts like mana depletion, aim is practice he has to actually put in. He got her *abilities*, not her *life* — the difference is where all his future work lives.

## Arc size and cut
Roughly arc-length (~10–15 chapters at 8–15k), but **end when the story is done**: selection, trip, fight, return, fallout. If Axel wants to be a longer stay while drafting, it can be longer. If it's snappier, snappier. No forced epilogue beats.

## Open questions the drafting answers (deliberately not pre-decided)
How long he stays; who befriends whom; exactly how the bout is structured (with her, terms she agrees to); whether anyone in-town suspects what he is; what Megumin thinks of him; what he keeps (nothing physical — but what does he *keep* mean); whether he'd answer a Wards recruitment flyer if one landed in his hand this month (he might; it's not decided).
