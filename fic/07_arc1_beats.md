# ARC 1 — "The First Aid Kit" · ch 1.0–1.15 · Sep 15 – Nov 8, 2010

Target length 8–15k words/chapter. POV: Eli, first person past, entire arc. Each chapter: one entry-image beat (cold open at something *physical*), the week's problem, a system or shop beat, an exit-hook. Interludes: none yet (save for arc 2).

- **1.0 "The First Aid Kit"** — Sept 14–15. Winslow day-two. Cold open: the freshman bathroom, the girl with the pill-case and the shaking hands — Eli runs for *an adult*, then handles it himself when the adult's forty seconds slow; first-aid competence on display, no heroics, one wrong call he fixes. The card lands *after* dinner, mid-argument with Diane about the electricity bill: `[ WEEKLY WINDOW OPEN — NAME A TARGET ]`. (The Monday anchor starts *from here* — the first window is whatever day the box lands; every Monday after is the refresh.) Ends: he doesn't sleep; he writes one word in a notebook. (Reader meets the power the way Eli does: like a tax.)
- **1.1 "Nobody Sees Anything"** — Establishing: Nora, the shop, Kowalski's scrap bin, the bike, the PHO boards, the E88 recruitment table by the bus loop (Kaiser's city, politely). **The ambulance-that-wasn't beat:** a cape skirmish (two unknowns, ABB-adjacent) three streets over; Eli ducks behind a Dumpster like the brief demands, drags a delivery driver down with him, *and* calls 911 with the right words at the end. Nobody thanks him; he checks the PHO casualty list that night (his genre-brain's tic: `04_fanon_map.md`). First *rules draft* of the notebook: he tries the Sokka name (in-story), reads the card's PACKAGE line, and laughs like he's been slapped. **Hook:** the card refresh mechanic understood; he starts the list for real.
- **1.2 "The List on the Fridge"** — The candidate-process chapter (the user's-feedback chapter, made scene): Eli turns his notebook into a scoring sheet; rejects YGO with dread; rejects Hogwarts out loud to an empty kitchen; *almost* names a Worm kid in the hallway of his own head and closes the box (R13's founding moment). He teaches Nora a first-aid knot; she asks why he's "suddenly doing homework about other countries' schools." Diane's face at the truancy warning — she *sees the lie in the syllabus*, says nothing yet. **Hook:** card flips: `PACKAGE: support-item engineering…` — he stares a long time. "That's not a power. That's a *job*." (He has no idea the system just agreed with him.)
- **1.3 "Ask Her To Fight You"** — The consent chapter. Two prep days (shopping with stolen hardware-store time, a rain poncho, the insulated gloves "for the batteries," a fake-name plan that immediately fails R6's honesty problem), then **arming the card** and entry: `190 m`, the prep field, the cackling, the mannequin on fire. Ends on the moment before he speaks.
- **1.4 "Introduce Yourself, Dumbass"** — The meet. He tells the truth sideways; she hears a *sponsor*; the negotiation of terms; Power Loader's distant disapproval; first look at U.A.'s support campus (the fandom chapter that's about *awe without worship* — Eli is too scared to gawk, so he notices systems instead: safety rails, insurance placards, how a school that trains children to be cops *looks*, and what BB's "school" looks like by comparison; one paragraph, held). Ends: the spar scheduled for dusk; the festival starts tomorrow; he has 24 hours in a world where kids fly.
- **1.5 "Field Notes, Musutafu"** — The one day of *looking around* (the brief's mini-arc clause, used): the city at the festival; merch, hero ads, a rescue demo in a plaza where the crowd *applauds logistics*; he almost cries in a vending area and decides the systems here are *also* fiction to somebody; Iida and Mei's rig fitting as background canon cameo (no timeline interference — Eli watches from a "family member" badge she gave him for "test subject visibility," and he *hates* how easy being here is). **Hook:** Aizawa-style near-miss (curfew, a camera, a lie he didn't need to tell because the teacher was too tired).
- **1.6 "Ten Minutes of Ad Read"** — THE DUEL (per `06` beats). The fight: slow, sound-designed, funny *to the reader* and *mortal* to him (he's never been hit before; she hits like a machine with brakes). Tag, rematch, the four seconds, the superheated demo plate caught off his face (the wrist burn — he doesn't acknowledge it for two beats), the second battery dying, `RESULT: defeat (nonstandard, accepted)`. **Transfer beat done as hearsay.** Ends: Power Loader's silence, Mei *up* first, laughing, already on a phone, "you are absolutely in the ad."
- **1.7 "What The Gift Costs"** — The aftermath day: the footage, the testimonial line with his alias on it, the *ad read with him in it* that plays while he's eating a protein bar behind the stadium and nearly stops his heart (R8's lesson lands: he can never come back to *anything*); the wrist burn treated (competence showcase); the goodbye that isn't one because it's a *door*. `LEAVE.` Eli returns mid-bite, protein bar gone (R10 — the multi-tool gone; he kept the receipt, there was never a receipt). Cold open beat for next chapter.
- **1.8 "Eleven Days, Three Texts"** — Home. The lying to Diane chapter (the hardest chapter of the arc; *play it dry*): truancy, the Scranton funeral, Nora's "your friend died?"; the vice-principal meeting; the first *build test* at the shop (the powered cart — mundane, enormous); the ceiling's shadow: he knows things he can't show his hands doing yet. **Hook:** November's first storm forecast + a PRT outreach flyer with a phone number on the kitchen counter, and Diane *meanings* it when she says "you could call someone."
- **1.9 "Hands That Know Too Much"** — Integration montage chapter with a spine: the build *works* (the cart sells; Kowalski's face when the invoice math lands), the fourth build *fails* (the powered winch spools wrong — **the ceiling scene**: he finds his roof mid-failure, not mid-triumph, which is funnier and meaner); the *Japanese* in his head surfacing where nobody hears it; the first *patient* — a bike-chain laceration he cleans at the shop counter with techniques that are not, legally, his. **Hook:** a Merchant junkie at the shop door asking, politely, if "the kid who builds things" is in. Skidmark's people are *shopping*.
- **1.10 "The Train Yard Fire"** — The arc's action centerpiece (two chapters, 1.10–1.11). Setup only here: a fire in a squib-occupied rail car off the Boat Graveyard access road; Eli is *everywhere except* heroics (he was walking *home*), then is in it the way the brief demands: the scene's axis is *triage, not combat*. The trapped kid. The bike-winch. The hooded-sweatshirt moment one bystander films. Ends: he hears sirens *before* the smoke lets him and the last beat is a tourniquet on his own forearm and a voice that is *not* friendly: "you're the tinker." (E88's street crew — wrong label, right instinct; he lies badly and it only half-works.)
- **1.11 "Nobody's Hero"** — The bill comes due in real time: the second crew, the arrival of a *cape* (Glory Girl, canon-plausible PR patrol; she does *not* monologue, she is *fourteen feet tall with a halo and a curfew to enforce*), the news van that films his side of it, Diane's phone call that he can't answer because of the blood on his hands. Ends: the fire's casualty count on the radio: one dead, a squib no one could reach — **the cost line for the arc's last movement, and he says it: this is what one bad second buys.**
- **1.12 "The Petition"** — The anticlimax chapter (deliberate, and it's the *Worm* chapter of the arc): the school's bathroom-lock petition (fanon belief #11's redemption beat), a PRT outreach meeting Eli attends *dressed wrong on purpose*, the woman who is *nothing like the fic* (belief #5 bites soft), PHO-thread fallout: ENE boards buzzing "unregistered tinker, East End" — **and one post, three replies deep, in a shop-account name he half-recognizes, asks if the winch guy wants a *contractor rate*.** Coil's reach, on-page, first contact, no drama at all.
- **1.13 "Contractor Rate"** — The temptation chapter: the job is *better than the PRT would be*; the money would fix things (the bill, the nebulizer, Nora's braces); the interview is in a coffee shop with a man whose *watch is wrong*; Eli leaves having built nothing illegal and agreeing to nothing (the interview turns on him asking what "the exclusivity clause" costs in *people*; the smile doesn't move; "think about the November events, Mr. Carter" — the Happening's still coming, and someone wants to be *first* to ask him). **Hook:** the storm forecast became a storm.
- **1.14 "The Beat 'Em Up"** — The storm + the arc's moral thesis: 2 a.m. windows down, water rising in the East End underpasses, a *normal* Tuesday with no capes; Eli's night is one call at a time (the shop storm-shutters, Mrs. K's sump pump built from *his* parts, a kid on the L-street bridge — *the ambulance call that this whole story started with, returned to the sender in November with the lights out*). Ends in the ER parking lot with Diane at shift change, ten minutes, no lies available; she looks at his hands, not his face: "You're not going to stop." Not a question. (The closest this arc ever comes to her *accepting* it — she never uses the power-word; neither does he; both mean it.)
- **1.15 "Next Monday, 00:00"** — Coda + the arc-1 hook. The card window opens: the new `STATUS` line is *not* another world — `WORM LOCAL: eligibility test — DECLINE [Y/N]?` (R13's fuse, lit and *not* touched; he picks `Y` — Decline — and the box just *goes*, no reply, no judgment, which is worse than a taunt would've been); the city's mood (the Happening *building* at the arc-2 door, on TV as a police-union story); a *package* line left half-open on the fridge-notebook with a list of names (the roster in `05_target_engine.md`, diegetic: the kid planning his own next month like homework); Nora's drawing in the bag; the last line is the one the title pays: he *can't* name what he is, so he names the week. (Final beat, unsentimental, cut like a Worm chapter: the headlamp clicks off, and the city makes its noises through the window, and the next Monday is coming.)

**Continuity notes to carry into arc 2:** the *contractor* file open; the E88 wrong-label standing; the Glory Girl "saw-the-face" partial (she files nothing — WMPs don't; the *camera* does); the wrist scar; the *ceiling* (his engineering is borrowed skill, and he's now *better than Mei's peers* on nothing but *as fast as* her on documentation); Diane's non-question; the Happening ~3 weeks out (Dec, canon sheet) — arc 2's cold open is the night before.
