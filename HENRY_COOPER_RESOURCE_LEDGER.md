# Henry Cooper — Resource-Bank Mining and Adaptation Ledger

**Status:** active planning reference
**Purpose:** prove where the story's scene grammar, power mechanics, relationship movement, and pacing come from, while preventing direct plot/prose transplantation.

The resource bank is the primary creative foundation. MCU canon supplies the timeline skeleton. Henry-specific invention is used only after fitting and adaptable source functions have been exhausted.

The labels used here are:

- **SOURCE-DERIVED** — one resource supplies the dominant scene function or technique;
- **SOURCE-COMBINED** — several resources are reassembled into a new MCU/Henry scene;
- **CANON** — MCU event/character material;
- **CONNECTIVE** — required bridge material;
- **ORIGINAL** — Henry-specific decision or new plot;
- **EXCLUDED** — material deliberately not imported.

No prose, dialogue block, character name, explicit scene, or proprietary plot sequence is to be copied into the fanfiction. The useful output is a function map.

---

## 1. Resource hierarchy for every future scene

When planning a scene, ask in this order:

1. Is there a fitting resource-bank function already available?
2. Can an adaptable function be combined with another resource?
3. What MCU canon behavior or event must the scene preserve?
4. What connective tissue is necessary?
5. What Henry-specific invention remains after those checks?

If an original invention appears before the first three questions are answered, the scene needs another resource pass.

## 2. Priority source harvest

## 2.1 `The_Spider.txt`

**Role:** direct MCU/Spider-Man structural source.
**Observed material:** the opening presents a young hero balancing a sudden power change, home, school, equipment, and the question of whether helping people is a duty. Early chapters move from power discovery to a public rescue, adult anger and concern, broken equipment, school social pressure, and later adult/team contact. The resource's chapter structure includes school transitions, training, first missions, Avengers-adjacent briefings, and Loki/SHIELD material.

### Functions to adapt

- **Sudden power as an immediate practical problem:** Henry's first concern is not destiny but what to do about frost on a sink, a ruined sleeve, or a damaged pipe.
- **The first rescue changes the hero decision:** a civilian rescue makes the protagonist choose whether the mask is an obligation rather than a costume fantasy.
- **Adult reaction after danger:** anger and care coexist; the adult is not written as an obstacle who “doesn't understand heroes.”
- **Broken equipment:** Peter's equipment failures become a model for Henry's first mask, gloves, and emergency gear.
- **School as a real place:** lunch tables, class periods, bullying, cliques, homework, and friendship scenes continue to matter after the first fight.
- **Hero ethics through dialogue:** questions about whether the protagonist is police/army/hero can become Henry/Peter's early rule-setting conversations.
- **Team scale:** later briefings show a young hero standing among established adults without automatically becoming their equal.
- **Evidence/investigation:** Loki/SHIELD material supplies the function of adult records and intelligence, not a plot to transplant.

### Planned uses

- A1: awakening, first rescue, first adult confrontation;
- A2: school/identity/equipment partnership;
- A3: local institutional attention and evidence handling;
- A4: Avengers-adjacent briefing rhythm;
- A5 onward: team/family disagreements and young hero boundaries.

### What is excluded

- the resource's SI premise;
- its romance/crossover structures;
- Fantastic Four/X-Men/Sinister Six material;
- copied jokes or dialogue;
- Peter's exact origin mechanics;
- adult sexual material;
- power escalation that would make Henry or Peter implausibly competent.

### Adaptation test

A Henry scene based on this source must still contain Henry's ice mechanics, Elena's home, Peter's MCU voice, and a specific MCU location/problem. If it could be pasted into the source with only names changed, it has failed.

## 2.2 `enchanting melody.txt`

**Role:** slow school-worldbuilding and elemental progression reference.
**Observed material:** the work treats learning as a long process, shows school and domestic life continuing around unusual abilities, and includes an elemental/magic distinction where small controlled effects precede larger manipulation. Relevant functions include environmental interaction, heat/cold consequences, practice in safe spaces, and the idea that scale increases difficulty and control requirements.

### Functions to adapt

- start with a small, testable elemental effect rather than a spectacular opening;
- make mastery a matter of repeated practice over years, not a reward for emotional intensity;
- show that the environment changes the ease and danger of an elemental technique;
- use school, food, research, and ordinary obligations as the rhythm around training;
- let an elemental effect change a battlefield without automatically winning it;
- treat concealment/wards as layered effects with limits rather than perfect invisibility.

### Planned uses

- A1: Henry's first experiments and poor control;
- A2–A3: training around school and home;
- A8: Strange's explanation that concealment can alter what a test detects;
- A15: Sylvie's concealment enchantment as a limited masking layer, not a complete rewrite.

### What is excluded

- Harry Potter setting, wand/spell system, prophecy, wizard institutions;
- inherited magical social hierarchy;
- copied sensory language or specific spell scenes;
- any assumption that Henry's power is a spell list;
- decade-long mastery compressed into weeks.

## 2.3 `Yakuza.txt`

**Role:** cryomancer/hydromancer distinction and construct ladder.
**Observed material:** the resource explicitly separates cryomancy from hydromancy and progresses through ice needles, icicles, arrows, blades, bucklers, ice skin, towers, walls, mines, spikes, and slippery ground. It also shows the practical insight that defensive/area-control techniques may matter more than a larger projectile. The source's game mechanics are visible and are useful only as an index of design possibilities.

### Functions to adapt

- ice is a family of uses, not one attack;
- defensive shapes and movement control should arrive before battlefield-scale offense;
- a buckler can transform into partial ice coverage;
- a wall is simple in concept but difficult in scale and focus;
- slippery ground is useful and dangerous to allies;
- a trap has setup, placement, and friendly-fire concerns;
- failed attempts at moving constructs are more interesting than an instant army;
- a heat/fire counter should exist.

### Henry translation

| Source function | Henry version |
|---|---|
| Ice Needle | small precise spike/wedge; used rarely and non-lethally when possible |
| Icicle/arrow | later projectile, high concentration and poor early accuracy |
| Ice Buckler | wrist/forearm shield that shatters under impact |
| Ice Skin | partial ice-body adaptation; blunt force still transfers |
| Ice Tower/Wall | evacuation barrier, route control, or emergency brace; expensive |
| Ice Mine | delayed pressure/temperature trap only after setup; not a free grenade |
| Ice Floor | short slippery patch; allies can fall too |
| Moving constructs | late possibility, never an army default |

### What is excluded

- levels, stats, mana bars, skill notifications, gacha, credits, system prompts;
- effortless blood/flesh freezing;
- instant healing or respawning body parts;
- high-level traps that trivialize every fight;
- game-defined damage numbers;
- sexual material and unrelated crossover plot.

### Planned uses

- A1: frost touch, wedge, brace;
- A2: buckler, restraint, floor, ramp;
- A3: route control and the lesson that barriers can trap allies;
- A4: drones, walls, evacuation channels;
- A7 onward: environmental variety and restraint;
- A10: team-scale barriers with serious cost.

## 2.4 `The_Art_of_Burning.txt`

**Role:** slow found-family care and trust after conflict.
**Observed material:** the useful pattern is not a particular plot. It is the way care accumulates through food, chores, injuries, mistrust, shared work, cultural misunderstanding, and choices made before trust is secure. The early ship/household scenes make practical logistics carry emotional information.

### Functions to adapt

- trust can begin with “get him up,” food, bandages, and a job to do;
- a hostile or defensive teenager can accept care before he can name it;
- the adult group can disagree about whether someone is dangerous while still keeping him alive;
- family grows through repeated ordinary contact;
- care does not require instant absolution.

### Planned uses

- Elena/Henry home scenes;
- A5 Thor/Henry after the reveal;
- Loki's first imperfect attempts at care;
- A9 refugee logistics after Asgard's destruction;
- A11 post-Snap reunion.

### What is excluded

- Fire Nation/Avatar plot;
- captivity/abuse structure;
- copied character dynamics;
- pre-reveal Thor/Henry closeness. The resource supports **post-reveal** slow care, not the rejected earlier assumption.

## 5. Style fingerprint

The compatible style across the mined bank is:

- high dialogue density;
- clear physical blocking;
- short paragraphs during danger;
- one mundane detail near a large event;
- tactical thought without adult omniscience;
- humor as pressure release;
- training shown through failed attempts and small improvements;
- affection expressed through practical action;
- consequences that cause the next scene;
- gradual escalation from school/neighborhood to cosmic/time problems.

The incompatible style tendencies are:

- purple declarations about destiny;
- unexplained competency;
- artificial trauma used as emotional fuel;
- game-system narration without a system premise;
- a protagonist who wins because the outline needs him to;
- dialogue that makes every MCU character sound like the same sarcastic narrator.
