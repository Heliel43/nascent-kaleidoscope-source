# Chapter 1: The Offer You Can't Refuse

I slowly opened my eyes, only to be met by an unfamiliar ceiling and the sensation of a different mattress.

It felt like I had foreign thoughts in my head. I don't know when it started, sometime recently, but I would catch myself thinking of something I had no idea about, in too vivid a recollection to think them mere daydreams. People talking around me, I heard my teacher lecture about something irrelevant, but my mind was completely unfocused.

No, that wasn't right. That was before. The lab. The pressure in my chest.

I rose from the bed and looked around to see that I was in some sort of empty room with no windows or doors.

"Where the hell am I?" I looked at my hand and clenched it and then unclenched it. It was unnaturally cold. "Something doesn't feel right... Am I... dead?"

You would be correct.

"Gah!" I got spooked by the sudden voice and jumped off the bed. "Wh- Who said that!?"

I did.

The voice was coming from everywhere, even in my own head. "Who are you? What are you? What happened to me? Where is this place!?"

In that order: I do not have a name, I am simply an A.I. created for the sole purpose of helping you in your transition to your new life. You have suffered a heart attack while you were sleeping, your parents found you this morning and took you to the hospital where you were pronounced dead. Finally, you are in the nexus where the earth and the afterlife meet. You were brought here because you were chosen.

My face fell at the news. The lab, the late nights, the energy drinks. It tracked.

"Oh, so I really am dead..."

You seem... awfully calm about this, considering the situation.

"Would it be better if I was kicking, screaming, and cursing? It wouldn't bring me back to life. Of course, not counting the shock that's keeping me calm right now." I explained.

I was in the middle of class when I couldn't help but just stare at the ceiling. Even as everyone got up and left, I was still stuck in a daze. Was I going crazy? I knew what burnout was, but didn't that come on gradually, not just out of the blue?

As amusing as it would be to watch that happening, I'd rather get this along.

"Get what-"

Congratulations! You have been chosen to be the new host for the Gamer System.

I blinked at the reveal. "Gamer System? You mean that gimmick in most fanfictions where the OC gets all these broken skills and abilities?"

Correct. I will guide you through the process so that you can get acquainted with the system.

"Alright, let's get started." A screen suddenly popped in front of me.

Well, that was one way of saying we don't give a damn about your life choices.

The next step is your inventory. Either tap on the bag icon on the screen or simply think "Inventory"

I did just that and the window quickly changed. It now showed a series of empty boxes that hold nothing except for two highlighted ones that have my current clothes. On the left of the screen shows what I have equipped right now.

# Chapter 2: Lost in the Woods

*Gasp!* My eyes snapped open as I took in a large amount of oxygen. I looked around exasperated as I was surrounded by trees. "I feel like shit..." I looked down to find not only me wearing a different outfit, but one that has been completely shredded and covered in blood. "What the fuck...?" I reached out and touched one of the bloody areas and noticed that the blood was still somewhat fresh. "What happened to me...?"

*POP*
*POP*
*POP*

[Congratulations on your first ever reincarnation into your second life!]

"Huh...?" I grabbed my head. "Who are you?"

[I am the A.I. that was integrated into your Gamer System before you reincarnated. It's very nice to meet you, sir!]

"Oh right, that voice did mention something like that." I tried to stand but quickly collapsed from a sudden dizzy spell. "What happened to me?"

[You were bleeding out on the side of the tree. Luckily, your Regeneration kicked in at the last minute and saved your life]

"Is that why I'm feeling so dizzy?" I asked. "So, how did I die exactly?" I took another look at my ruined clothes. The large claw marks on my chest indicated that I was attacked. "Oh..." I tried to recollect any memories of what happened but found nothing. "Why can't I remember anything?"

[Simple, because those memories don't belong to you. You don't remember anything because those events never really happened to you]

"I... guess that makes sense. Still, I'd like a brief summary of who I was before I 'awakened'."

[Long story short, you're an orphan. You were adopted but shortly after your foster mother died. Since then, you've traveled from town to town until you tried to become an adventurer. On a job clearing wolves, you and your team were killed.]

"What were their names?"

[Don't know, don't care. They were just extras that existed to fill in those roles]

Harsh.

"... what was his name? My name, before."

[Does it really matter to you?]

"... no, it doesn't." While a part of me did want to know, it wouldn't change the fact that I am me and not that person. Though, it would be pretty awkward if I ran into people that knew him.

"Huh. You know, I just noticed that despite learning about my own death and being in another world, I am not freaking out like you'd normally do in this situation." I remarked.

[That's due to the passive effect of Gamer's Mind. It will curb your anxiety along with other emotions in order to think clearly]

"Is that why I'm so weirdly calm? Oh well, it doesn't matter."

I patted down my pockets, finding everything where it should be. My belt was cracked down the middle and the innards looked like they've been microwaved, I observed with a groan. My folding knife was in my back pocket. My wallet was still there.

I'm hurt, tired, lost, have zero supplies, and I'm probably hallucinating. I closed my eyes and sighed. At least Regeneration worked.

I checked my status without thinking.

Level 5 already. From the tutorial EXP. And my HP was low but climbing. 80 per minute. I could feel it - the wet wheeze I had was gone. Incredible. Just incredible. My inner academic yearned to know the hows and whys of this.

[Why don't you try using your Observe skill on things around you?]

"God dammit..." I let out a sigh as I let go of the tree, finally getting over my dizziness, and started walking. "Well, let's get this over with."

"Yes, finally I'm done with this." I was exasperated. "And it looks like I leveled up, too."

System Update... Update Complete!

I continued the thread through the forest in the hopes of finding an exit, when a weird sound caught my attention. I followed the sound and I began to notice traces of blood on the ground and some of the trees before walking up to a clearing and witnessing a sight that some would find grotesque.

It was a man, or rather what's left of him. His body had been torn apart, missing both of his arms and half of his face, and his chest cavity was opening revealing all of his vital organs.

But he was not alone.

Next to the body was what could only be described as a mass of grey fur with bone-like spikes protruding from its arms, back, and knees. Its back was turned, but I could hear it chewing on something. Suddenly, the creature spit something in my direction as it landed near my foot. When I looked down, I saw that it was, in fact, a finger that it threw away.

This monster was eating the man's arm!

I began to quickly freak out but something forced me to calm down. I recognized the creature from the previous Kael's memories and activated my [Observe] skill.

```

"So that's a Dire Wolf, huh? They're a lot more hairier in real life than in the bestiary." I silently remarked. 'I need to get away. I can't fight this without a weapon.' I slowly made my way back when...

*SNAP!*

I stepped on a tree branch by accident, small but very noisy, causing the beast's body to twitch at the sound. The monster's head slowly turned and saw me, its face covered in scars as well as its hands and feet tipped with long, sharp, white claws. Those beady red eyes stared at me screaming bloody murder and it slowly walked towards me.

I instinctively stepped back as the fear began to take over.

I'm going to die...
I'm going to die here....

'I'm going to die, I'm going to die, I'm going to die, I'm going to die, I'm going to die, I'M GOING TO DIE!'

At that moment, [Gamer's Mind] kicked in again and my fear disappeared leaving the monster perplexed as it could no longer sense my negativity. However, that didn't stop the monster as it jumped, extending its claws in order to rip me to shreds.

My body suddenly went on auto pilot and dodge rolled the incoming attack.

"Huh?" I blinked at the sudden action I performed. "W-How did I do that?"

However, I didn't have time to contemplate these thoughts as the wolf ran towards me and I quickly made a run for it.

"Shit, shit, shit, shit, shit, shit!" I screamed out before I immediately calmed down again. 'I can't keep running like this. If this guy was an adventurer then his weapon must be around here somewhere.'

The Dire Wolf leapt upwards and descended down on me, its sharp claws piercing through my skin like nothing.

-57 HP
HP: 686/850

"Damnit, get off me!" I grabbed the wolf by its head and pushed it back. The wolf saw this and tried to use its full weight to crush me, but I pushed it back. I continued pushing until I got back up and kicked it in the chest, sending it towards a nearby tree and leaving a print of my shoe on its chest.

I looked around frantically. There - a rusted short sword, half buried in leaves. The dead man's? I dove for it.

My hands closed around it as the wolf recovered. It charged again.

I didn't think. I swung.

The blade caught it across the muzzle. It yelped, more surprised than hurt. But I felt something click. My body remembered how to do this. Not me - the previous Kael, and the Muscle Memory perk.

Perk Unlocked!
[Muscle Memory]
You may not know how to fight, but your body does. Fighting is second nature to you, you can counter and block most physical attacks without much effort.

The wolf circled. I adjusted my grip, breathing hard. SP was dropping.

-12 SP from exertion.

I needed to end this fast. I feinted left, then drove the sword forward with both hands. It wasn't clean, it wasn't heroic. It was desperate and pragmatic. The rusted blade punched into its throat.

The wolf gurgled, clawed at me weakly.

-89 HP
HP: 597/850

I twisted. Pulled out. Stabbed again.

It collapsed. Its body twitched, then stilled. Then, like in a game, it started to dissolve into faint blue motes.

```
You have killed a Dire Wolf Alpha! You have gained 120 EXP! You have earned 50 Credits!
Loot: [Wolf Fang x2] [Wolf Pelt - Damaged]
```

I fell to my knees, chest heaving. My hands were shaking, but not from panic - from adrenaline that Gamer's Mind was slowly damping down.

I looked at the other body. The other adventurer. I should bury him. But I didn't have a shovel, and I was still bleeding.

"Sorry," I whispered. "I'll come back. If I can."

I used Observe on the sword.

```
[Rusted Short Sword] - Rank: F
Description: A cheap iron sword, poorly maintained. Better than fists.
Damage: 8-12
Durability: 22/100
```

It would have to do.

I kept walking east. The zookeeper pushes away the mental breakdown that desperately wants out and starts walking east at a slow pace. No matter what happened, he needs to find other people to help him and hope he doesn't stumble into a worse crater.

As I walked, I took stock of myself. The thick book closes with a "whap!" and falls from limp fingers - no, that was someone else's memory. My own thoughts were simpler: find water, find shelter, don't die again.

The forest was dense. Ironbark trees everywhere, moss underfoot. The sunlight beaming down began to dim as evening approached, forming a curious, circular space around me that wasn't as brightly lit as my surroundings.

I needed to make fire before dark. That was basic survival. I had no flint. I had a rusted sword and two wolf fangs.

But I had ID Create.

I remembered the skill description. Creates a pocket dimension with different themes and continuously respawning mobs. The final area will have a mid-boss and dungeon boss.

Could I use it to rest? The description said escaping during boss was impossible, but otherwise...

"ID Create," I whispered.

Nothing happened.

[Please specify theme: Forest, Cave, Ruins, Plains?]

"Empty. Safe. Just empty."

[ID Create: Empty Field - Created]
[Would you like to enter? Y/N]

Yes.

The world shifted. The trees blurred, and suddenly I was standing in a flat, grassy field under a grey sky. No monsters. No sounds. Just grass for maybe a hundred meters in each direction, then a shimmering wall.

Holy shit. It worked.

I sat down hard in the grass. My HP was at 720 now, ticking up. 80 per minute. At this rate I'd be full in two minutes.

I laid back and stared at the grey sky. This was my training room. My safe house. My cheat that wasn't really a cheat, because it still required work.

For the first time since waking up covered in blood, I felt like I might actually survive this.

[ID: Escape?]

"Not yet," I said. "Give me five minutes."

I closed my eyes. Sleep rapidly overtook me, but before it did, I thought I heard something at the edge of the ID - a twig snapping in the real forest. Something else was out there.

I needed to get out. Soon.

# Chapter 3: The Scout

I decided to keep walking though. That would be an adventure for another day.

The world was full of trash, but right outside the window was a pretty sakura tree in full bloom. No, that was another life. Here, the forest was full of ironbark and damp moss, and the only color was the faint blue of my HP bar ticking up.

I had been inside too long. I was getting philosophical. I needed to refocus.

Exercise like this might be pretty boring. Wearing out every part of myself until I couldn't go on, and then sleeping for a few hours until I was fully recovered to do it again, but there was something... Well it was like playing a game. Grinding out some stats in a game, except this was more real. More important.

I escaped the ID. The forest was darker now. My [Observe] told me it was around 6 PM. I needed shelter.

I heard it before I saw it - a low growl, not a wolf. Something bigger. And then a sharp, controlled voice.

"Down!"

A shape moved through the trees to my left. Fast, low. A woman, maybe my age, maybe a little older, with a short bow already drawn. She wasn't looking at me. She was looking past me.

I turned.

Another Dire Wolf, this one larger than the alpha I killed, was stalking from the brush, eyes fixed on me. It must have smelled the blood on my clothes.

I fumbled for my rusted sword. My SP was still low.

The woman didn't wait. She loosed.

The arrow took the wolf in the eye. It yelped, thrashed, and then she was already moving, pulling a skinning knife from her belt and driving it into its throat with a practiced efficiency that made my own desperate stab look amateurish.

The wolf dissolved.

She finally looked at me. Brown hair tied back, leather armor that had seen actual use, not costume use. A scar across her left forearm. Eyes that assessed me in one quick sweep and found me wanting.

"You're bleeding," she said. It wasn't a question.

"Old blood. Mostly," I said. "New blood, a little."

She frowned. "You're the one they sent to clear the east trail? You're supposed to be a team of three."

That must have been the previous Kael's party. The ones who died.

"Team's dead," I said honestly. "I got lucky. Regeneration - I mean, I recovered."

She stared at me for a long moment, then sighed. "Gods. Another F-rank disaster. What's your name?"

"Kael. Kael Thorne."

"Lyra. Lyra Quinn. Junior Scout, Lorn's Guild." She shouldered her bow. "You look like shit, Kael Thorne. Can you walk?"

"Yeah."

"Good. Town's two miles that way." She pointed east, the same direction Taillow had pointed in another story. "You have a weapon at least. That's more than the last idiot I found."

She started walking, expecting me to follow. I did.

For a few minutes we walked in silence. The thick book closes with a "whap!" - no, focus.

"How long have you been out here?" she asked without looking back.

"Since morning. Woke up after the attack. Don't remember much before that. Head injury, maybe."

"That explains the calm. Shock does that." She had a pragmatic tone I appreciated. No melodrama, just assessment. "You a local?"

"Not really from around here," I said, echoing a line from another life. "I'm... not really from around here."

She glanced back, eyebrow raised. "Obvious. Your accent's weird. You walk like you read about walking in a book."

"Thanks."

"It's not an insult. It's just observation. You fight like it too. That sword - you hold it like a hammer."

"I've been told I need practice."

She snorted, a short, amused sound. "At least you're honest. Most boys your age would be puffing up about killing an alpha."

"I killed one. Barely. The other one you killed."

"That was a juvenile. The alpha's mate, probably. They travel in pairs when nesting." She stopped, crouched, and brushed moss off a stone marker I hadn't seen. Old, carved with a faded guild sigil. "We're on the old road now. Safer."

I used Observe on her when she wasn't looking.

```
[Lyra Quinn]
Level: 12
Class: Scout / Tracker
HP: 340/340
Description: Junior Guild member, competent, tired of rescuing newbies. Debt-bound to Lorn's trading family.
Affection: 5/100 (Neutral - Wary)
```

Level 12. Double my level. And Affection. The System tracked that now? That was new.

[System Update: Social Module Unlocked due to meaningful interaction]

Great. Now I was a dating sim too.

We walked another twenty minutes. The forest began to thin. I could see lights ahead - not neon, but lantern light. A palisade wall.

"Shion... why does that name sound familiar?" I muttered, but the village ahead wasn't Shion. A sign, half-rotted, read LORNSWATCH - Population 312.

"Welcome to civilization," Lyra said dryly. "The #1 hotspot in the frontier. Try not to die before we reach the gate."

"Thanks for the rescue," I said, meaning it.

She shrugged. "Don't thank me yet. Guildmaster's going to have questions. And you're going to need to pay for that rescue if you want to keep your adventurer tag."

"Pragmatic."

"I have a family debt to pay. Pragmatic is all I have."

We reached the gate. Two guards in chain, looking bored until they saw the blood on me. Then alert.

Lyra waved them off. "F-rank casualty. East trail. Found one survivor. Needs a healer check and a debrief."

One guard nodded, opening the gate. "Healer's still at the tavern. Rowan's buying."

"Of course he is."

We stepped through. Inside, people were drinking, eating, talking, laughing while some of the occasional fighting in the background. That is until everyone's attention turned to me and as I took a seat at the counter, I could hear the subtle sounds of whispering in the background.

No, that was a different tavern. This one was smaller, quieter. A few tables, a hearth, the smell of stew.

A large man behind the counter - balding, scarred hands - looked up. "Lyra. Who's the stray?"

"Kael Thorne. East trail team. Only survivor. He says he doesn't remember much."

The man - Rowan, I guessed - looked me over with the same efficient assessment Lyra had. "You look like you got mauled and then decided to go for a walk. Sit. You need food and a real bandage, not that moss you're using."

I hadn't realized I was using moss as a bandage. I looked down. I was.

"How did you know?"

"Because I've seen idiots try it before. It works in a pinch, but it itches like hell later." He ladled stew into a bowl. "Eat. Then we'll talk about what happened to Joren and his boys."

Joren. So that was the team leader's name.

I sat. The stew was hot, salty, real. Not chili fries, but better because it was here and I was alive to eat it.

Lyra sat across from me, not next to me, keeping distance. She ordered water.

"You should get some rest," she said after a while, her voice lower. "There is a guest room in the back for you to use. It's a little cramped but it should fit one man and his... sword just fine."

"Thanks."

"Don't thank me. I'm charging you two silver for the guide fee. Guild rules."

"Fair."

She almost smiled. Almost.

```

I'd take it.

The adventurer's guild was a grand building, eye-catching in the peculiar way that government buildings were. Stately, was the word people often used. # Chapter 8: The Empty Field
